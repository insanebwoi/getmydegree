Yes, online and campus degrees are treated as equivalent under the UGC's 2020 regulations, provided the university is recognized and the programme held DEB entitlement for your admission year. In practice, equivalence settles the regulatory question for further study and employment. It does not stop a university or an employer from applying its own written admission criteria or hiring preferences, and it does not override a notification that asks for regular study.

So the answer is settled in principle and conditional in practice. This article looks at what that means for the two situations people ask us about most: studying further, and working in the private sector. Government recruitment has its own rules, which we cover in our guide to [distance degrees and government jobs](/blog/is-a-distance-degree-valid-for-government-jobs).

## What do the 2020 regulations actually say?

They say that undergraduate and postgraduate degrees and postgraduate diplomas from recognized open and distance or online programmes are treated as equivalent to the conventional awards. This is Regulation 22 of the UGC (Open and Distance Learning Programmes and Online Programmes) Regulations, 2020, and the DEB's own FAQ restates it.

Three details in that position matter more than the headline.

- **It attaches to properly entitled programmes, not to distance education in general.** A degree from a university that was not entitled to offer that programme in that mode is not a lesser degree. It is a degree with a problem, and no general statement about equivalence repairs it.

- **The degree title has to fit the UGC's specification of degrees.** The DEB's FAQ ties equivalence to conformity with the UGC's 2014 specification. An unusually named degree is worth a question before you join.

- **Your admission year is what counts.** The DEB's FAQ also explains that an admission made during a recognition period stays valid until the programme is completed, on condition the institution follows UGC norms. Recognition for one year does not carry forward to the next, which is why your own session is the one to check.

The verification you do before enrolling is what makes the equivalence real. Our walkthrough of [checking UGC-DEB approval](/blog/ugc-deb-approval-search-verify-recognition) shows how.

## Can I do a master's after an online or distance degree?

Yes. A recognized online or distance bachelor's degree meets the "bachelor's degree" requirement for postgraduate study in the same way a campus degree does, whichever mode you choose for the master's. What equivalence does not do is remove the other conditions in a university's prospectus.

Before you apply, look for these in the prospectus of the university you want to join.

- **Subject requirements.** An MA or M.Sc in a subject may expect that subject at undergraduate level. A general B.A. may not meet it, whatever the mode.

- **Minimum marks.** Selective programmes often set a percentage or a separate entrance test. Equivalence gets you to the starting line. It does not lift your marks.

- **Scheme or structure rules.** Some universities ask for a particular number of years of study or a named pattern of subjects. Read this before you assume you qualify.

- **Out-of-state eligibility.** Kerala universities may ask students with a degree from another university for an equivalency or eligibility certificate. Check the prospectus and the university's own procedure early, since it is a paperwork step that can take time.

## Can I do a PhD or sit NET with a distance master's?

Usually yes, if the master's is recognized and meets the marks the regulations ask for, but check the current rules for your cycle. The UGC does prohibit M.Phil and Ph.D programmes themselves from being offered in distance or online mode. That rule is about how the research degree is delivered. It is not a statement that your earlier distance master's cannot lead to one.

For a research or teaching career, read two documents before you enrol: the current UGC-NET information bulletin, and the admission rules of the university where you hope to do research. Departments can be selective, and some applicants find that the reputation of the institution matters as much as its legal status. If an academic career is your actual goal, tell your counsellor at the start, because it can change the advice.

## Do private companies accept online degrees?

Most do, but no regulation obliges them to, and that is the honest difference from the public sector. A private employer can write its own hiring policy, and a campus recruitment drive restricted to full-time students is perfectly lawful. Equivalence gives your degree a recognized legal standing. It does not decide a recruiter's preferences.

In practice, three things tend to matter.

- **Background verification.** A verification agency usually writes to the university or uses a database to confirm that you were enrolled and awarded the degree. That is why a real enrolment number and a university record matter more than a certificate that looks impressive.

- **Internal promotion rules.** Many firms ask only for "a graduate degree". Others specify a full-time qualification for certain grades. Ask HR for the requirement in writing, ideally before you pick a programme. Our article on [degree requirements for promotions](/blog/degree-requirements-for-executive-promotions) goes through this conversation.

- **Honesty on the form.** If an application asks for the mode of study, state it. Misdescribing a distance degree as a regular one can turn a perfectly good qualification into a problem of a different sort.

An employer's opinion of your programme also tends to follow your work record. An illustrative composite, not a real person: Rahul, a sales supervisor with eleven years in a Kochi distributor, finishes a distance B.Com. His firm needs a graduate degree for its next grade, HR confirmed that in writing, and the mode never comes up. If he had applied instead to a firm that hires only from full-time campus programmes, his degree would be just as recognized and still not get him through.

## Where does equivalence stop?

Equivalence stops wherever a written rule asks for something more specific, and the table shows the usual cases.

| Situation | What equivalence gives you | What still decides it |
|---|---|---|
| Master's at another university | Your degree counts as a bachelor's degree | Subject, marks, entrance test and any equivalency step |
| Private employer | Recognized legal standing | The employer's own policy |
| Government post asking for a degree | Eligibility in the ordinary case | The notification and service rules |
| Post requiring a "regular course" | Nothing | The Kerala High Court ruled in January 2026 that a distance degree does not satisfy it |
| Overseas job or study | Recognition in India only | That country's and that employer's rules |

The regular-study limit is explained in our article on [Kerala PSC and distance degrees](/blog/distance-degree-valid-kerala-psc). Regulated professions, foreign licensing bodies and overseas universities apply their own rules, so check with that body directly if your plan depends on them.

## What should I do if someone says my degree does not count?

Ask for the requirement in writing. It often turns out to be a specific criterion in a notification, prospectus or HR policy rather than a blanket position, and a specific criterion is something you can plan around.

Then gather your evidence. When you graduate, keep the degree certificate, the consolidated marksheet, your admission letter and a copy of the DEB entitlement for your admission year together. Verification can happen years later, at a promotion or a visa application, and assembling that evidence afterwards is unpleasant.
