A NAAC grade is an outside assessment of an institution's quality for a limited period, and a NIRF ranking is its position against other institutions in one year. Neither one tells you whether a specific online or distance degree is valid. That depends on the university's recognition, the programme's DEB entitlement for your admission year, and the wording of any job notification you plan to use it for. Use grades and rankings to compare universities that have already passed those checks.

These two acronyms appear in every brochure and are regularly presented as though they were the same thing, or as though either meant a degree was recognized. Here is what each one can and cannot do for you.

## What does a NAAC grade actually mean?

A NAAC grade means that the National Assessment and Accreditation Council examined an institution and gave it a letter grade, based on a score out of 4, for a limited period. The assessment looks at matters such as teaching, research, infrastructure, governance and student support.

What it tells you:

- An outside body looked at the institution, against published criteria, at a particular time.

- The institution was willing to go through the process, which is voluntary.

What it does not tell you:

- That a particular programme may be offered at a distance or online. That is a separate entitlement from the UGC's Distance Education Bureau.

- How good your own programme's teaching, study material or student support will be. The assessment covers the institution as a whole, not its distance or online arm in particular.

- That the grade is still current.

## Do NAAC grades expire?

Yes. NAAC accreditation runs for a fixed period, generally five years, and the institution has to go through assessment again to keep it. A grade on a brochure, website banner or old news report tells you nothing until you know when it was awarded and when it ends.

So a statement like "NAAC accredited" needs three follow-up facts: the grade, the cycle and the validity dates. A grade with those details is a claim you can check. A grade without them is only a sales line.

Three situations come up in practice.

- **A current grade with dates.** Bharathidasan University, for example, holds an A+ grade from its third cycle of accreditation. The cycle tells you it has been through assessment repeatedly. Check its validity dates on NAAC's records, as you should for any university.

- **A grade that has lapsed.** Accreditation runs for a fixed period, so a grade quoted in a brochure may have ended years ago. A lapsed grade does not mean the university is not recognized, as these are different questions. It simply means the grade should not be quoted as current, so check the validity date on the NAAC site.

- **A grade past its printed date with an extension.** NAAC has issued validity extensions, and compliance documents for UGC recognition ask for proof of extension where relevant. So if a date looks passed, ask for the extension letter, then check.

The system itself is also changing. In 2025, NAAC announced a move towards accredited or not accredited outcomes with graded maturity levels, in place of the familiar letter scale. Reports differ on how far this has been rolled out, and we could not confirm the detail from NAAC's own pages when we last checked. Expect to see both kinds of claim for some time, and ask for the certificate in either case.

## How does NIRF ranking work, and how is it different from NAAC?

NIRF, the National Institutional Ranking Framework, ranks institutions each year within categories, and unlike NAAC, it gives a comparative position rather than a pass or a grade. It uses five groups of measures: teaching, learning and resources; research and professional practice; graduation outcomes; outreach and inclusivity; and perception.

| | NAAC | NIRF |
|---|---|---|
| What it gives | A grade or accreditation status | A rank in a category |
| What it compares against | Published criteria | Other participating institutions that year |
| How long it lasts | A fixed period, then reassessment | One year's list |
| What to ask for | Grade, cycle, validity dates | Rank, category, year |
| Does it entitle a programme? | No | No |

The category matters as much as the number. A rank in the management, university or open university list means something different, and brochures rarely say which they are quoting. NIRF also publishes bands rather than exact positions further down its lists, so "ranked" may mean a band. Taking part is a choice by the institution, so a university's absence from a list is not evidence that it is weak.

Rankings are most useful for comparing institutions in the same category in the same year. They are least useful as a permanent attribute of a university, which is how they are usually presented.

## Does a NAAC grade or NIRF rank make my degree valid?

No. A high grade on a university with no DEB entitlement for your programme and session does not make that degree valid. An unranked university with proper recognition and entitlement awards a degree the regulations treat as equivalent to a conventional one.

There is one real link between them, and it explains why the confusion exists. The UGC has used NAAC scores and NIRF positions as eligibility conditions for institutions applying to offer these programmes. Recent recognition lists, for example, have referred to a minimum NAAC score of 3.01 out of 4 valid on a stated date, or a top-100 NIRF university rank. That is a condition for the university to apply. It is not a substitute for the listing, so you still need to check your programme with our guide to [checking UGC-DEB approval](/blog/ugc-deb-approval-search-verify-recognition).

## When should I actually care about grades and rankings?

Care about them when your plan depends on how the institution is perceived, and much less when you only need a recognized qualification.

- **They carry more weight** if you hope to study abroad later, apply to an employer that screens on institutional reputation, or follow an academic career.

- **They carry less weight** if you need a degree for a promotion criterion or a government eligibility rule. Those usually ask whether the degree is recognized, not where the university ranks. See our guide to [distance degrees and government jobs](/blog/is-a-distance-degree-valid-for-government-jobs).

## What should I do if a claim looks inflated?

Ask for the grade with its cycle and validity dates, and the rank with its category and year, then check both on the awarding body's own site. A provider who cannot give you those details has not given you a fact. If you find an old grade being advertised as current, that says something about how carefully the rest of the information was prepared.
