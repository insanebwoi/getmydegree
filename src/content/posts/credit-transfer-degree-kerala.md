Yes, in 2026 a student in Kerala who passed part of a degree at a recognized university can usually carry those passed papers into a new programme and finish only what remains, instead of starting again from the first semester. Whether it works in your case depends on three things: where you studied, what you passed, and the rules of the university you move to. The receiving university always has the final word.

This guide walks through how that decision is made, what you should collect before you ask, and where people in Kerala usually get stuck.

## What credit transfer actually is

Every paper you pass in a semester-based degree carries a credit value. Credit transfer means a second university looks at those passed papers, matches them against papers in its own programme, and exempts you from the ones that match. You are then admitted at a later point in the course, sometimes called advanced standing.

It is not a shortcut around study. You still complete the remaining papers, sit the remaining examinations and meet the minimum period of study the receiving university requires. What you avoid is paying for, and sitting again, papers you already passed.

For the basic idea in a shorter form, our earlier piece on [what happens to the credits from an unfinished degree](/blog/credit-transfer-explained) is a good starting point.

A large share of the people who contact us studied for a year or two at a college under Calicut, Kerala, MG or Kannur University and then stopped. The reasons are familiar: a visa came through for the Gulf, the family needed an income, a marriage moved someone to another district, or a run of supplementary papers became too much to carry alongside a job.

Ten years later the same person wants a degree for a promotion, a PSC attempt or a better role abroad. The passed semesters are still on their old marksheets. Credit transfer is how those marksheets become useful again.

## A typical case

This is an illustrative composite of cases we see, not one real person. A woman from the Thrissur coast joined a B.Com at a Calicut University college, passed her first two years, and stopped when she married and moved. A few years on, with her children at school, she wanted to finish. She had all four semester marksheets and two papers she had never cleared.

The assessment in a case like this looks at each passed paper against the receiving programme. The cleared papers that match are exempted. The two uncleared papers are not, so they are studied again along with whatever remains of the final year. The answer she would get is not "you are nearly done" or "start again", but a specific list of what is left, in writing, before she pays anything.

## Who usually qualifies

- You passed at least some papers at a university that was recognized by the UGC when you studied there.

- You can produce marksheets for those semesters, or the old university can issue duplicates.

- The new programme is in the same or a closely related subject, so papers can actually be matched. A B.Com to B.Com or BBA move maps far better than a B.Sc Chemistry to B.Com move.

- The gap since you studied falls within what the receiving university accepts. Universities set their own limits here, so we check this case by case rather than quoting a rule.

**What usually does not carry across:**

- Papers you failed and never cleared. A backlog has no credit value until it is passed.

- Study at an institution that was not recognized at the time.

- Papers with no equivalent in the new programme, even if you passed them.

- Practical or laboratory components, in some cases, where the receiving programme assesses them differently.

If you have a backlog, it is worth asking whether clearing it at your old university first would increase what transfers. Sometimes it does, sometimes it is quicker to repeat the paper in the new programme.

## The Academic Bank of Credits and your APAAR ID

The Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) is a national digital record of the credits a student earns at institutions registered on it. Each student gets an ABC ID, linked to the APAAR ID. Where both your old and new universities are registered and your credits have been uploaded, the record makes transfer more straightforward.

Two honest caveats. First, if you studied some years ago, your old credits may never have been uploaded, so the paper marksheets still do the work. Second, ABC stores credits; it does not force any university to accept them. The receiving university's own rules decide what counts. Our [guide to the ABC ID](/blog/academic-bank-of-credits-abc-id-explained) explains how to create one with DigiLocker.

## Credit transfer and recognition

A transferred degree is only as useful as the degree it becomes. Before anything else, check that the university you are moving to is recognized by the UGC and that the specific programme has UGC-DEB entitlement for distance or online mode for the year you join. Our step-by-step [guide to checking DEB approval](/blog/ugc-deb-approval-search-verify-recognition) shows exactly where to look.

Then check the purpose. For most private-sector roles and most government posts, a recognized distance degree is treated on par with a regular one under the UGC's 2020 regulations. But some Kerala PSC posts require a qualification earned through a regular course of study, and in January 2026 the Kerala High Court confirmed that a distance degree does not meet that requirement. If you have a particular post in mind, read its notification first. We cover this in detail in [is a distance degree valid for government jobs](/blog/is-a-distance-degree-valid-for-government-jobs).

## The documents that decide your case

The assessment is done on paper, so the more complete your file, the faster the answer.

- Every semester marksheet you hold, including supplementary results.

- Your 10th and 12th certificates.

- The transfer certificate and migration certificate from your old college or university, if they were issued.

- The syllabus or course structure for the years you studied, if you still have it or can download it.

- Your Aadhaar and, if you have created them, your ABC and APAAR IDs.

If a marksheet is missing, apply to your old university for a duplicate early. Kerala universities issue them, but it can take time, and it is the most common reason a transfer stalls.

## How fees work after a transfer

Fees are usually charged only for the part of the programme you actually study, not for the semesters exempted. The total is made up of tuition, registration, examination and certificate charges, and attestation if you need it for work abroad.
