The single most expensive mistake in this field is paying for a degree that turns out not to count. It is also the most avoidable, because the records you need are public and the checks take about fifteen minutes.

## The order matters

Check the university first, then the programme, then the mode, then the centre. Most people check the last one first, which is why the mistake happens: a smart office with a university name on the door tells you nothing about whether that university may run that programme in that mode.

## One: is the university recognized at all

Every genuine Indian university is established by an Act of Parliament or a State legislature, or declared a deemed-to-be university. That status is listed by the UGC. If the institution does not appear in the UGC's own lists of universities, nothing else on this page matters.

## Two: is it entitled for distance or online mode

This is the check people skip. A university being recognized does not mean it may offer degrees at a distance. That is a separate entitlement, granted by the UGC's Distance Education Bureau for specific programmes, at specific levels, for specific academic years.

So the question is never "is this university approved". It is "is this university approved to offer this programme, in this mode, in this academic session". Ask for the entitlement in writing, naming the programme and the year.

## Three: does the programme appear on the university's own site

The university's official website is the record that matters. If a programme is being marketed to you but does not appear on the university's own admissions pages, treat that as a refusal until proven otherwise.

Be careful with lookalike domains. Check the address against the one listed in the UGC's records, not the one in the brochure.

## Four: who is actually admitting you

A legitimate admission produces an enrolment number issued by the university, in your name, visible on the university's own student portal. Money paid to a study centre with no university record behind it buys nothing.

## The claims that should stop the conversation

- A degree backdated to a year you did not study.

- A one-year bachelor's degree, or any programme materially shorter than the standard duration.

- "Approval is in process" for the session you are being admitted to.

- A guarantee that you will pass, or that no examination is required.
