To verify an online or distance degree, confirm the university on the UGC's lists, then find the programme on the Distance Education Bureau portal under the correct mode and your academic session, then match both against the university's own website. All of this is public and you can do it yourself in one sitting. If the university, the programme or the session does not show up, get an explanation in writing before you pay anything.

The single most expensive mistake in this field is paying for a degree that turns out not to count. It is also among the most avoidable. This guide takes you through the portals in order. If you want the wider list of checks on receipts, enrolment numbers and sales claims, our guide on [how to spot a fake degree provider](/blog/spot-fake-degree-provider) covers those.

Before you start, have four things written down: the university's full legal name, the exact programme name, the mode you were offered (distance or online), and the session you would join. A vague "MBA from a UGC university" cannot be checked.

## How do I check if a university is UGC recognized?

Search for it on the UGC's own lists at ugc.gov.in, where universities are listed separately as central, state, state private and deemed to be universities. Type the address yourself rather than following a link in a brochure or message.

What to look for when you find the entry:

- **Exact name and location.** The UGC's entry should match what you were told. A university with a similar name in a different city is a different institution. Bharathidasan University in Tiruchirappalli and Bharathiar University in Coimbatore are a common example of two genuine and separate universities that people mix up.

- **The category.** State private and deemed universities have different rules on where they can operate, which matters in the fourth step.

- **The official website.** Use the address given in official records as your reference for the rest of the checks.

While you are there, look at the UGC's list of fake universities. Kerala has had an entry on it, so do not assume it is a problem from somewhere else. A university's absence from that list proves nothing, but its presence ends the matter.

## How do I check DEB approval on the portal?

Go to deb.ugc.ac.in, open the **Recognised Programmes** menu, choose the right mode, and select your academic year. This is the check most people skip, because a recognized university is not automatically allowed to teach at a distance.

Here is the path as the portal was laid out when we checked it in November 2026. Menus may move, so look for the same labels if the page has changed.

1. Open the **Recognised Programmes** menu. It has two parts: **OL** for online learning and **ODL** for open and distance learning. Go to the one that matches what you were offered, not the other one.

2. Under each mode you will find **Recognised** and **Entitled**. Check both. Some institutions are entitled as a category under the regulations, while others hold recognition for a list of programmes granted to them.

3. Use the **Select Year** dropdown to choose your session. Years run in the form 2026-27, 2025-26 and so on, back to earlier sessions.

4. Find the university and read the programme entries against your written notes. An M.Com entry does not cover an MBA. A distance entry does not cover an online programme.

5. If the page shows nothing, try the other list and a different browser, then look at the **ALL Programme** link under **Statistics of ODL & OL programmes** on the home page. Portal lists are sometimes slow to load or update. A blank page is a reason to ask, not a pass or a fail.

Save a dated screenshot or PDF of what you find. It is worth keeping for years, because a recruiter's question about your degree may be about the year you were admitted.

## How can I tell if a programme is allowed in online or distance mode at all?

Look at the **List of Prohibited Programs** on the DEB portal, under the regulations for 2020. It lists disciplines that cannot be offered this way, including engineering, medicine, nursing, pharmacy, law and architecture, along with M.Phil and Ph.D. The Notice Board on the same site is where the UGC publishes later changes, including decisions on health-related subjects, so read recent notices if your subject is anywhere near that area. Our note on [recent UGC rules for applicants](/blog/ugc-updates-for-online-degree-applicants) summarises what has changed.

## Is the university allowed to admit and examine me here?

That depends on the type of university and the mode, so this is the fourth check. The DEB's FAQ explains territorial limits for distance programmes. You can enrol from anywhere in India, but admissions, contact programmes and examinations have to happen within the institution's jurisdiction.

| University type | Where distance activity may happen, per the DEB's FAQ |
|---|---|
| Central | The area allotted under its Act |
| State | The allotted area, and not beyond its state |
| State private | Its headquarters and recognized off-campus centres, not learner support centres |
| Deemed | Its headquarters or government-approved off-campuses |

Online programmes work differently, as learners can enrol from within or outside India.

Why this matters in Kerala: if you are told that an out-of-state university runs a distance examination from a local office, ask the university in writing whether that centre has been approved for it. A counselling office that helps you apply is one thing. A centre presented as the university's teaching or examination site is another, and the UGC has stated in its notices that no university has approval to admit learners through franchising arrangements.

## How do I check the university's own website?

Find the programme on the university's official site, on its admissions or distance education pages, in the mode you were offered. If it appears in the DEB list but not on the site, or the fee and eligibility differ from what you were told, ask why.

Be careful with lookalike domains, especially ones reached from an advertisement. Bharathidasan University's distance education pages sit at bdu.ac.in/cde, for example, and a similar-looking address may belong to someone else.

## What should I do once I have checked everything?

Ask for the same details in writing from whoever is admitting you, and compare them with your notes. Then make sure the paper trail follows.

- **DEB-ID.** Learners enrolling in distance or online programmes have to create a DEB-ID on the portal using an ABC ID. Do this yourself, or at least keep the ID in your own records.

- **Enrolment number.** A legitimate admission produces an enrolment number issued by the university in your name, which you should be able to confirm with the university.

- **Your file.** Keep the DEB screenshot, your admission letter and your receipts together.

If you studied part of a degree before, see how [credit transfer](/credit-transfer) lets you finish only what remains, and apply these same checks to the university that will award the final degree.

Treat "approval is in process" for your session as a no until the listing appears. Treat a claim that the university's name alone is enough in the same way.
