A "one-sitting" B.Tech, a backdated B.Tech or a B.Tech "in one year without attending" is not a real degree. A B.Tech is earned over years of classes, laboratory work and university examinations at an AICTE-approved college, and no university can legitimately hand one over after a single sitting. If someone is offering you a B.Tech certificate on those terms, you are being offered either a fake certificate or a degree in a mode that is not valid.

The people these offers target are usually diploma holders on Gulf contracts, site supervisors and technicians waiting for a promotion. Most are not looking to cheat. They are told the degree is genuine, "UGC approved", and that "everybody does it". This guide explains the warning signs, how to check a certificate and a college yourself, and what to do if you have already paid.

## Is a one-sitting B.Tech valid?

No. There is no recognised route by which a B.Tech is awarded in one sitting. A genuine B.Tech requires you to attend an AICTE-approved institution, complete the labs and assessments, and pass the university's semester examinations, over four years from the first year or not less than three years by lateral entry.

A distance or online B.Tech is not valid either. The UGC's 2020 regulations for distance and online programmes list engineering among the programmes universities may not offer in those modes, and in 2017 the Supreme Court held that engineering degrees awarded by distance mode by deemed universities without AICTE approval were not valid. Our explainer on the [2017 Supreme Court ruling](/blog/supreme-court-distance-engineering-ruling) covers what happened to those degrees.

## How do agents sell fake or invalid B.Tech certificates?

The pitch changes, but the pattern is familiar. Watch for any of these:

- **Speed.** "B.Tech in six months", "one year, no classes", "complete in one sitting".
- **Backdating.** An offer to show the degree as completed in an earlier year, to match a job application or a visa.
- **No admission process.** No entrance test, no eligibility check, no seat allotment, just a fee.
- **No attendance.** No timetable, no labs, or a single "exam week" in another state.
- **A distant university.** A university in another state, with a Kerala "office" or "study centre" that is not a campus.
- **Guarantees.** "Verification guaranteed", "attestation will be arranged", "PSC will accept it".
- **Cash and pressure.** Payment in cash or to a personal account, a "last few seats" deadline, and reluctance to give receipts in the university's name.
- **Certificates first, details later.** You are shown sample certificates instead of the university's regulations or AICTE approval.

Any single one of these is a reason to stop. Several together are a near certainty. Our wider checklist on [how to spot a fake degree provider](/blog/spot-fake-degree-provider) applies to engineering as well.

## Why is a fake B.Tech dangerous even if nobody checks now?

Because certificates are checked again and again, often years later. They can be verified at PSC recruitment, at appointment, at promotion, during Gulf attestation and at professional registration abroad. A certificate that passes once can still be questioned the next time.

When it is found out, the consequences can include losing the job or promotion that rested on it, losing a visa or work permit, and being barred from future recruitment. Using a forged certificate is a criminal offence in India, and Gulf countries treat forged documents seriously too. The money paid to the agent is usually gone.

## How can I verify a B.Tech certificate?

Check the certificate against the university that is supposed to have issued it, not against anything the agent gives you.

1. **Check the university is real and recognised.** The UGC publishes lists of recognised universities, and also a list of fake universities, on its website. Look up the name exactly as printed.
2. **Check the college was AICTE-approved** for that branch and year. AICTE publishes approved institutions and their intake.
3. **Ask the university for verification.** Universities, through their examinations or controller's office, can confirm whether a certificate in a given name and register number was issued. Employers and embassies use this route too.
4. **Check the mode.** Marksheets and the degree certificate should not say distance, online or correspondence for a B.Tech.
5. **Match the dates.** A genuine B.Tech has semester marksheets for every term, with examination dates that fit a normal academic calendar.

For Kerala engineering colleges affiliated to KTU, results and university notices are published on ktu.edu.in. Our guide to [verifying recognition on UGC-DEB](/blog/ugc-deb-approval-search-verify-recognition) shows how to search the official lists.

## How do I check a college before I pay any fee?

Ask for the following in writing, and check each one yourself:

- **AICTE approval** of the institution and of the specific programme and intake, including any working-professional seats, for the current academic year.
- **The affiliating university**, and the college's presence on that university's list of affiliated colleges.
- **The admission route.** In Kerala, the first year is through KEAM, and lateral entry and working-professional seats need the LET conducted by LBS Centre.
- **A fee receipt in the college's name**, not an individual's or an agency's.
- **The class timetable and lab schedule.**

If an agent cannot show these, or asks you to "trust the process", do not pay.

## What are the genuine ways to get a B.Tech while working?

There are three valid routes, and none of them is fast.

- **B.Tech for working professionals.** In Kerala, KTU offers it at AICTE-approved institutions from 2024-25, with evening or flexible classes, including weekends and holidays. You join the third semester, it takes not less than three years, and you need at least one year of full-time employment plus a qualifying LET score.
- **Regular lateral entry.** Diploma, D.Voc and B.Sc holders join the third semester through the LET. For the 2026 admission, the LET was held on 13 June 2026.
- **Regular full-time B.Tech** from the first year, through KEAM.

Our guide to [B.Tech for working professionals in Kerala](/blog/btech-working-professionals-kerala) explains the eligibility and steps. The 2027 notifications are not out yet; watch the KTU and LBS Centre websites.

## What should I do if I have already paid an agent?

Act quickly and keep everything.

- **Stop further payments**, however the agent frames them.
- **Collect evidence:** receipts, bank transfers, chats, brochures, the agent's address and any documents you received.
- **Verify what you were given** with the university directly, using the steps above.
- **Do not use the certificate** for any application, attestation or promotion until it is verified.
- **File a complaint** with the local police if you were cheated, and tell your family so others are not drawn in.

If you need a real qualification quickly for an IT role rather than an engineering post, a recognised distance or online BCA or B.Sc may be a valid alternative. That is a different degree, not a B.Tech, but it is genuine.
