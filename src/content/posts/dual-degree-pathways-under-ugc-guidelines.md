Yes, you can pursue two degrees at the same time in India under the UGC's Guidelines for Pursuing Two Academic Programmes Simultaneously, issued in April 2022 and partly modified in June 2025. The permitted combinations are two full-time programmes in physical mode with non-overlapping class timings, one physical programme with one distance or online programme, or up to two distance or online programmes. The guidelines do not cover PhD programmes, and each university has to allow it under its own rules. So the guideline makes it possible, but it does not settle your individual case.

For a long time, holding two degree enrolments together was not generally permitted. That position has changed, and it opens a genuine option. It also gets misrepresented, so it is worth setting out carefully.

## Which two degree combinations does the UGC allow?

The UGC allows three combinations, and each carries its own condition.

| Combination | Main condition |
|---|---|
| Two full-time programmes in physical mode | Class timings of the two must not overlap |
| One full-time physical, one ODL or online | The ODL or online programme must be from an institution recognized to run it |
| Up to two ODL or online programmes | Both must be from institutions recognized to run them |

The word "simultaneously" should not be read as "on any terms". Both degrees remain governed by UGC regulations and, where they apply, by the relevant statutory or professional council. A programme that cannot be offered in distance or online mode, such as engineering, medicine, nursing, pharmacy, law or architecture, does not become available in that mode because of these guidelines.

Two campus programmes with separate attendance rules are permitted on paper, but they are hard in practice. Non-overlapping class times are only the start: you would also have to satisfy each programme's attendance requirement and sit both sets of examinations.

## What do I need to check before enrolling in two programmes?

The first check is whether both universities will actually accept it. The UGC asked universities to devise mechanisms through their statutory bodies to allow simultaneous study, so institutional rules still matter, and they may not read the same way at both universities.

Go through this list before paying either fee:

- **Recognition.** Both universities must be recognized. Check each one yourself.

- **DEB entitlement.** Any distance or online programme must have entitlement in that mode, for your admission year. Our guide to [checking UGC-DEB approval](/blog/ugc-deb-approval-search-verify-recognition) shows how.

- **Written permission.** Ask each university directly whether it permits simultaneous enrolment, and get the answer in writing. Ask specifically about your pair of programmes.

- **Timetables and examinations.** The guideline only speaks of class timings, but a clash between two examination dates is your problem to solve. Compare the calendars before enrolling, not after.

- **Your existing course.** If you are already a student somewhere, read that institution's rules too. Some colleges have attendance or conduct rules, and your principal or department should hear about it from you.

An institution's staff may not know the current position, and an institution may have conditions of its own. Keep every answer in writing, as you may need it at verification.

## Is a second degree done earlier still valid?

Yes, it can be, if it was done properly under the rules that applied at the time. This is the point the June 2025 change addressed. The 2022 version said no retrospective benefit could be claimed by people who had already done two programmes together. The Commission's modified version replaced that: programmes pursued simultaneously before the guidelines are to be treated as valid if they followed the UGC's First Degree and Master Degree Regulations, the statutes or ordinances of the universities concerned, and the norms of any statutory professional council and of the Distance Education Council or Bureau, wherever applicable.

Those conditions are real. If you did two degrees before 2022, the modification helps you only if the earlier study met them. Collect both sets of admission records, marksheets and any entitlement evidence. If a recruiter or university raises a question, show them the UGC letter dated 5 June 2025 along with your records, and ask them to apply it in writing.

## Will two degrees at once help me for a job or Kerala PSC?

A second degree helps only if a specific post or employer needs it, because each qualification is judged by that notification's wording. Three things decide whether any degree counts: the university's recognition, the programme's DEB entitlement for your admission year, and the wording of the job notification.

Where a notification or Special Rules require a "regular course of study", a distance degree does not satisfy it, as the Kerala High Court held in January 2026. So a person holding a regular B.Com and a distance BA may find that only the regular one can satisfy such a clause. Our article on [distance degrees and Kerala PSC](/blog/distance-degree-valid-kerala-psc) shows how to read the clause.

There are situations where it genuinely helps:

- Someone doing a full-time campus degree who adds a distance [B.Com](/courses/bcom) or BBA to broaden their eligibility.

- Someone completing a PG degree while adding a diploma or second PG in a complementary field.

- Someone whose employer requires one qualification while their career plan requires another.

As an illustrative composite, not a real individual: a final-year campus student in Thrissur is aiming at both a commerce job and PSC posts in a language stream. A distance BA alongside her campus B.Com looks attractive. Before she pays, she confirms that her college permits it, checks the BA's entitlement for that admission year, reads two recent notifications for the posts she wants, and compares her final-year examination calendar with the BA's. Only then does she know whether the second enrolment gains her anything, or whether finishing the B.Com and starting the BA afterwards serves her just as well.

## Can I do two degrees while working full time?

You can, but we talk most working professionals out of it. If one programme takes six to ten hours a week, which is our working figure, two programmes will take around twice that, on top of the job, sustained for two or three years. Finishing one degree well is worth more than half-finishing two, and a failed semester in both programmes is a real outcome, not a hypothetical one.

A sensible test: have you already kept up a study routine for one full semester while working? If not, start with one. Doing them one after the other costs you time, but it greatly reduces the risk of losing both. A short certificate alongside a degree may give you what you wanted from the second programme at much lower strain. For the single-degree route, see our guide on [finishing a degree while working](/blog/studying-while-working-full-time).

If you still want to go ahead, plan it this way:

- Check the examination calendars of both programmes against each other before enrolling.

- Stagger the start dates by one semester where possible, so the heavy weeks do not coincide.

- Ask each university how credits are recorded, and link both to your [Academic Bank of Credits](https://www.abc.gov.in/) account where the university is registered for it.

- Keep written confirmation from both universities that simultaneous enrolment is permitted, along with both sets of fee receipts.
