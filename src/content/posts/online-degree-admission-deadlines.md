Applications rarely fail because someone missed the date. They fail because a document was missing on the date, and the missing document took three weeks to obtain.

## How the calendar actually works

Distance and online programmes in India run on two admission cycles a year, one around July to August and another around January to February. Each has a published closing date, and universities routinely extend it once. Planning around the extension is a bad habit: the extension is not guaranteed, and the last week is when verification queues are longest.

Treat the first announced date as the real one.

## The documents that hold people up

Most applications need the same set. Gather them before you choose a programme, not after.

- 10th and 12th marksheets and certificates.

- The degree certificate and consolidated marksheet for any previous qualification, for PG applicants.

- A government photo identity document.

- A recent photograph and a specimen signature, in the file sizes the portal asks for.

- A transfer or migration certificate, where the previous institution issued one.

- An Academic Bank of Credits identifier, which most universities now expect at enrolment.

The two that reliably cause delay are a lost marksheet and a migration certificate from an institution you left years ago. Both can be reissued, and both take weeks. Start those the day you decide to apply.

## A sensible sequence

Four weeks out, confirm the university's entitlement for the programme and mode, and request any duplicate documents you need. Two weeks out, complete the application and upload everything. One week out, confirm the fee instalment and check that the portal shows your submission as complete rather than saved as a draft.

The last step is the one people miss. A saved draft is not an application.

## After you submit

Admission is confirmed when the university issues an enrolment number, not when a payment clears. If a week passes with no enrolment number and no written explanation, ask in writing and keep the reply.
