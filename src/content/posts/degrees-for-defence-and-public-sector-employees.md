Serving and retired defence personnel and public sector employees can generally study for a recognized distance or online degree, but the right programme depends on three things specific to them: the written service or promotion rule, whether they need permission to study, and whether the examinations can survive a transfer. Those who have retired have more freedom, and their question is usually what the degree should do for a second career.

The options themselves are the ordinary ones: B.Com, BBA, BCA, BA or B.Sc at bachelor's level, and MBA, MCA, M.Com or MSW at master's level. What differs is everything around the degree.

## Can serving defence personnel do a degree during service?

Generally yes, but check your own service's requirements before enrolling, because the rules on permission and the demands of your posting are not ones a university can change. Ask your unit's education or personnel channel whether prior permission or an intimation is needed, and get the answer on record. We are not going to summarise schemes or concessions here that we cannot confirm for your service and rank. Your own channel is the reliable source, and any arrangement it describes should be confirmed in writing.

Public sector staff should ask the same thing of their personnel or HR section. Some PSUs have conduct rules or study-leave conditions about higher study, and some link any incentive to prior approval.

## Which document decides whether a degree counts for promotion?

The written criterion decides: a recruitment rule, promotion policy, departmental notification or service rule. That document is the specification. It will usually name the level, sometimes the discipline, and sometimes the mode of study.

Read it before you choose anything. A degree that meets a general expectation but not the written criterion is an expensive mistake, and we have seen it made. Where the wording is ambiguous, ask your establishment or personnel section in writing, because their interpretation is the one that will be applied to your file.

For a recognized programme, validity depends on three things: the university's recognition, the programme's DEB entitlement for your admission year, and the wording of the notification or rule. If that wording asks for a "regular course of study", a distance degree does not meet it. The Kerala High Court held this in *Kerala PSC v. Vaisakh C.L.* (2026) for a post whose rules carried that condition. Our guide to [distance degrees and government jobs](/blog/is-a-distance-degree-valid-for-government-jobs) covers the general position. If you are a Kerala government or state service employee, read our [guide for Kerala government employees](/blog/degree-for-kerala-government-employees) too.

Central PSUs and state PSUs do not all follow one rulebook. Staff at a shipyard, refinery or fertiliser company in Kochi will be reading a different policy from someone in a state corporation. A colleague's experience at another PSU tells you little about yours.

## What happens to my degree if I am posted elsewhere mid-programme?

It usually comes down to the examination rules, so sort those out before you enrol rather than after the movement order. The two modes are quite different here.

| Question | Open and distance learning | Online |
|---|---|---|
| Where are examinations held? | Under UGC rules, at centres within the university's territorial jurisdiction | Through the university's online system or at a proctored centre, depending on the university |
| What does a posting change? | You may have to travel to the centre on fixed dates | You need the connection and a suitable room wherever you are |
| What to ask | Where exactly is the centre, and can it be changed between semesters? | What equipment and room rules apply, and what happens if the connection fails? |

Online sounds like the obvious answer for anyone who moves, but look at it honestly. Remote proctoring needs a working camera, stable internet and a private room. Ship-borne staff, field units and sensitive stations may have none of these, or may not be allowed to use them. In that case, a centre-based paper planned around leave could be the safer choice. Our article on [proctored and centre-based examinations](/blog/proctored-exams-and-assessment-models) sets out what each one demands on the day.

Also ask about a break in study. A deployment can cost you a semester, so find out whether the university allows re-registration after a gap, how long the maximum period is, and what it costs. Do not leave this until you need it.

## What should a retired soldier or PSU employee study?

Study whatever the next job asks for, and read a few real job adverts before you decide. Retirement gives you more time and fewer rules, but it also removes the employer's written criterion, so you need to find your own target.

- **Security, facilities or administration roles in the private sector.** Many adverts ask simply for a graduate. A BA or BBA is often enough, and an MBA becomes open once you hold a bachelor's.

- **Accounts or a family business.** A B.Com is usually the more useful choice.

- **Fresh government or PSC applications.** Read the notification first, including any ex-servicemen provision and any regular-study clause.

- **Work in the Gulf.** Check attestation and recognition for the destination country before choosing a programme, because acceptance of distance and online degrees varies there.

If you have been out of study for decades, that is ordinary, and our article on [returning to a degree after a long gap](/blog/degree-after-long-gap) is written for you.

Resettlement training is a separate matter. The Directorate General Resettlement, under the Ministry of Defence, runs pre- and post-retirement training for armed forces personnel, but those courses are not the same as a university degree. Check with it directly if you are coming up for release.

## Do service training and army certificates count towards a degree?

Do not assume they do. A UG admission needs 12th pass from a recognized board, and a PG admission needs a bachelor's degree, so the first check is whether your service-issued education certificate meets that entry condition at the university you have in mind. It is the university's decision, so ask for it in writing before you pay. A certificate that served you well for a service purpose may not be treated the same way for admission.

Credit for technical courses and service training is a similar question. Any such credit depends entirely on the receiving university's rules, and we would not plan a timeline around it until you have the answer in writing. Passed papers from an earlier degree at a recognized university are a more common basis for credit, so bring those marksheets too.

## Five checks before you enrol

- The written criterion, plus your personnel section's reading of it if the wording is unclear.

- Any permission or intimation your service or PSU requires.

- The programme's DEB entitlement for your admission year, checked on the official site.

- The examination location and the policy on changing centres.

- The rule on breaks in study and re-registration.

Keep the entitlement evidence for your session with your certificates. Verification at promotion can come years later, and this is hard to reconstruct at that point.
