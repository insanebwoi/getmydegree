Serving personnel and public sector employees face a specific version of this problem. The qualification requirement is usually written down precisely somewhere, the posting may move, and the working pattern is not negotiable.

## Start with the written criterion

In government and public sector employment, the qualification needed for a grade or promotion is normally specified in a recruitment rule, a departmental promotion notification or a service rule. That document is the specification. It will usually name the level, sometimes the discipline, and sometimes the mode.

Read it before you choose anything. A degree that satisfies a general expectation but not the written criterion is an expensive mistake, and we have seen it made.

Where the wording is ambiguous, ask your establishment or personnel section in writing. Their interpretation is the one that will be applied to your file.

## Mode and recognition

Degrees earned through recognized open and distance or online programmes are treated as equivalent to campus degrees. What makes that hold is that the programme was entitled for that mode in the session you studied it.

For government purposes this is not a detail. Verification at the time of promotion will check the university's recognition and the programme's entitlement. Keep a copy of the entitlement evidence for your session along with your certificates.

## Transfers and postings

The practical risk for serving personnel is a posting that moves mid-programme. Two things protect you.

First, prefer remotely proctored examinations over centre-based ones where the choice exists, because they travel with you. Where a centre is required, ask whether the university allows a change of examination centre between semesters and what notice it needs.

Second, ask what the university's policy is on a break in study, and whether re-registration is possible after a gap. A deployment that costs you a semester should not cost you the degree.

## Prior learning and service training

If you completed part of a degree earlier, those credits may still carry. Service training and technical courses sometimes map to credits as well, depending on the university. It is worth asking rather than assuming, because it can shorten the programme materially.

## Before you enrol

Confirm the written promotion criterion, the programme's entitlement for your session, the examination format and location policy, and the break-in-study rule. Those four cover almost every way this goes wrong.
