KTU's B.Tech for working professionals is a regular B.Tech at AICTE-approved colleges, with classes in the evenings or at weekends and holidays, for employed diploma, B.Sc, B.Voc and D.Voc holders. You join the third semester through the Lateral Entry Test and study for not less than three years.

These are the 30 questions we are asked most often at our Thrissur and Tirur branches. The answers are short; each links to a fuller guide where one exists. Where a figure changes every year, we say which year it is from.

## What is a B.Tech for working professionals?

It is an AICTE-approved B.Tech, affiliated to KTU in Kerala, with class timings set for people in full-time jobs. You attend in person, do the same labs and examinations, and join at the third semester.

### 1. Is it a distance or online B.Tech?

No. A B.Tech by distance, online or correspondence mode is not valid in India. The UGC's 2020 regulations exclude engineering from those modes, and the 2021 and 2022 amendments kept it excluded. This programme is classroom-based. See [distance B.Tech in Kerala](/blog/distance-btech-kerala).

### 2. Who approves it?

AICTE approves the institution and its working-professional intake. In Kerala, the degree is from KTU (APJ Abdul Kalam Technological University), the affiliating university for most engineering colleges in the state.

### 3. Since when has it existed?

AICTE discontinued the old evening and part-time B.Tech around 2019, then allowed "B.E./B.Tech for working professionals" from 2023-24. KTU has offered it from 2024-25 at AICTE-approved institutions.

### 4. Is it the same as the old evening B.Tech?

It replaces it. The current programme has its own conditions, including proof of employment, a workplace-distance rule and the LET. Our guide to [part-time and evening B.Tech in Kerala](/blog/part-time-btech-kerala) explains what changed.

### 5. How many colleges offer it nationally?

AICTE reported 137 institutes approved for working-professional programmes across India in October 2023. The number will have changed since.

## Who is eligible?

Employed people with an accepted qualification and at least one year of continuous full-time work in a registered organisation. The details below are from the KTU notice and college pages for 2026.

### 6. Which qualifications are accepted?

A diploma in engineering (three-year, or two-year lateral), a B.Sc with Mathematics at Plus Two, a B.Voc, or a three-year D.Voc.

### 7. What marks do I need?

At least 45% in the diploma or B.Sc, or 40% for reserved categories.

### 8. How much work experience is needed?

At least one year of continuous full-time employment in a registered organisation, with an Employment Certificate from your employer.

### 9. Is there an age limit?

There is no upper age limit.

### 10. Can B.Sc graduates join?

Yes, with 45% marks and Mathematics at Plus Two. B.Sc candidates may need to clear Engineering Graphics and Engineering Mechanics.

### 11. Can I join with only Plus Two?

Usually not. The admission qualification is the same as for lateral entry. With Plus Two alone, the route is a full-time first-year B.Tech through KEAM.

## How does admission work?

You qualify the B.Tech Lateral Entry Test (LET), then apply to colleges with working-professional seats, which rank candidates by LET score.

### 12. Who conducts the LET?

LBS Centre for Science and Technology, for the Directorate of Technical Education, Kerala. Applications are on lbsapplications.kerala.gov.in. Our [Kerala LET guide](/blog/kerala-btech-lateral-entry-let) covers it in full.

### 13. When is the next LET?

The 2026 LET was held on 13 June 2026. The 2027 notification is not out yet; watch the LBS Centre website.

### 14. What is the qualifying mark?

For 2026, the minimum qualifying mark was reported as 15% for the general category, lower for reserved categories.

### 15. Can I be admitted without the LET?

Only as a fallback. If LET-qualified candidates run out, colleges may admit others on their qualifying marks. Do not plan around it.

### 16. Can final-year diploma students apply for the LET?

Yes, final-year diploma students may apply. For the working-professional programme, you also need the year of employment.

## Where and when are classes held?

In the evening hours or at other flexible timings, including weekends and holidays, at the college. Each college sets its own timetable.

### 17. How long does it take?

Not less than three years, as you join at the third semester.

### 18. How far can my workplace be from the college?

AICTE's 2024-25 norms were reported as 50 km, down from 70 km. One Kerala college's page states 75 km. Check the current notice. Our explainer on [AICTE rules](/blog/aicte-working-professional-btech-rules) has the details.

### 19. Can I study from the Gulf?

Generally not, because your workplace must be within the permitted distance of the college and you must attend in person.

### 20. Which colleges offer it in Kerala?

The list changes every year. For example, for 2026, College of Engineering Thalassery listed B.Tech Mechanical (30 seats) and SJCET Palai listed B.Tech Electrical and Electronics (30 seats).

### 21. Do all branches run every year?

No. Each branch needs approval for working-professional seats, and some colleges run a batch only if enough students join. SJCET Palai, for example, listed a minimum of 10.

## What does it cost?

Fees are set by each college and change every year. Read each college's current admission page before you plan.

### 22. What were the fees in 2026?

As listed on the colleges' 2026 admission pages: College of Engineering Thalassery, ₹40,000 a year in tuition, with a total first payment of ₹56,460. SJCET Palai, ₹50,250 per semester in tuition, plus a ₹10,000 refundable caution deposit, a ₹10,000 special fee a year, a ₹2,625 exam fee per semester, and KTU fees.

### 23. How do I compare fees?

Add up the full three years, including university and exam fees. Some colleges quote per year and others per semester. See [what a degree costs and how EMI works](/blog/what-a-degree-costs-and-how-emi-works).

## Is the degree valid?

Yes, it is a regular-mode B.Tech from an AICTE-approved college affiliated to KTU, which is the kind of degree engineering posts ask for. Still, check the wording of each notification or rule you are relying on.

### 24. Is it accepted for Kerala PSC?

Kerala PSC Assistant Engineer posts ask for a B.Sc or B.Tech in the relevant engineering branch from a recognised university or equivalent. Read each notification for the branch it requires.

### 25. What is the Assistant Engineer pay scale?

As an example, notification 180/2025 for Assistant Engineer (Civil), PWD, lists ₹55,200–1,15,300.

### 26. Will it work in Saudi Arabia?

Engineers there must be accredited by the Saudi Council of Engineers, which assesses qualifications and experience. Distance-mode degrees are widely reported to be rejected; a regular-mode B.Tech avoids that issue. Check with the SCE. See [degree attestation for the Gulf](/blog/degree-attestation-gulf).

### 27. Does NBA accreditation matter?

It can, for work abroad. NBA represents India in the Washington Accord, as a permanent signatory since 13 June 2014. Accreditation is programme-wise and time-limited, so check the specific programme on nbaind.org.

### 28. How do I verify the programme?

Check AICTE's approval for the institution and its working-professional intake, the KTU notice, and that your admission papers say regular mode. Our guide on [spotting a fake degree provider](/blog/spot-fake-degree-provider) lists warning signs.

## What are the alternatives?

AMIE, and a recognised BCA or B.Sc followed by an MCA if you need an IT career rather than an engineering degree. Neither is a B.Tech.

### 29. Is AMIE equal to a B.Tech?

Not everywhere. A 2012 MHRD memo granted recognition, later withdrawn from 1 June 2013 for new enrolments, and the status has since been contested. Some Kerala PSC Assistant Engineer notifications, such as Civil, list AMIE as an alternative. Check each one.

### 30. What if I want an IT job?

If the job asks for "B.Tech or BE", only a B.Tech will do. If it asks for a computer science graduate, a recognised distance [BCA](/courses/bca) or B.Sc, then an [MCA](/courses/mca), often will.
