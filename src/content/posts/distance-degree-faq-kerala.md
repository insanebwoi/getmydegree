A distance degree from a UGC-recognized university, in a programme with UGC Distance Education Bureau (DEB) entitlement for your admission year, is treated as equivalent to a regular degree for most jobs and further study in Kerala and across India. The main exceptions are posts whose rules ask for a regular course of study, and professional programmes that cannot be taught in distance mode at all. Below are the 25 questions we are asked most often, with short, direct answers.

## Recognition and validity

### 1. Is a distance degree valid in Kerala?

Yes, when two conditions hold: the university is recognized by the UGC, and the specific programme had DEB entitlement for the academic year you were admitted. Under the UGC (Open and Distance Learning Programmes and Online Programmes) Regulations, 2020, such degrees are equivalent to conventional degrees.

### 2. How do I check whether a university and programme are approved?

Look up the university on the UGC website, then check the programme on the DEB website for your admission year. Do not rely on a brochure or a screenshot. Our guide to [verifying UGC-DEB approval](/blog/ugc-deb-approval-search-verify-recognition) walks through it step by step.

### 3. Is a distance degree accepted for Kerala PSC?

For most posts that ask for "a degree from a recognized university", yes. But where the Special Rules or notification require a qualification through a regular course of study, a distance degree does not qualify. Read each notification. Our article on [Kerala PSC and distance degrees](/blog/distance-degree-valid-kerala-psc) explains how to read the qualification clause.

### 4. What did the Kerala High Court decide in 2026?

In *Kerala Public Service Commission v. Vaisakh C.L.* (2026:KER:6103), decided on 29 January 2026, the court held that a distance qualification did not satisfy a Librarian Grade IV notification that required a regular course. The ruling applies to posts with that wording, not to every post.

### 5. Is a distance degree accepted for central government jobs and banks?

SSC graduate-level examinations, UPSC civil services and bank recruitment through IBPS and SBI generally ask for a degree from a recognized university, which a recognized distance degree satisfies. Check each notice for subject requirements, cut-off dates and any special conditions.

### 6. Will a distance degree work for jobs in the Gulf?

It can, but it is not automatic. The degree usually needs attestation through the university or state, the Ministry of External Affairs and the relevant embassy, and some Gulf authorities scrutinise distance and online degrees closely at the equivalency stage. Read our guide to [online degrees and Gulf jobs](/blog/online-degree-valid-gulf-jobs) before you choose.

### 7. Does the marksheet or certificate say "distance"?

Practice varies by university. Some mention the mode of study on documents, others do not. It does not affect validity where the programme is properly recognized, and we would never suggest hiding how you studied.

## Admission and eligibility

### 8. Who can apply for a distance bachelor's degree?

Anyone who has passed 12th from a recognized board. Returning after a long gap is common and is not in itself a barrier to admission.

### 9. Who can apply for a distance master's degree?

Anyone holding a bachelor's degree from a recognized university. Some programmes may ask for a degree in a related subject, so check the specific programme.

### 10. Which degrees can I study through distance or online mode?

The programmes we place students into are B.Com, BBA, BCA, BA and B.Sc at undergraduate level, and MBA, MCA, M.Com and MSW at postgraduate level.

### 11. Which degrees cannot be done through distance mode?

UGC rules do not allow programmes such as engineering, medicine, nursing, pharmacy, law, architecture and physiotherapy in distance or online mode. Anyone offering them that way should be avoided.

### 12. How long does a distance degree take?

The bachelor's degrees we place students into are three-year programmes, and the master's degrees are two-year programmes. Credit transfer can reduce the part you still have to complete. Be wary of anyone offering a full degree in one year.

### 13. When can I join?

Universities admit students in set academic sessions, and the dates differ by university, programme and year. A counsellor will tell you the current session for the programme you choose.

## Earlier study and credit transfer

### 14. I left my degree half-way. Do I have to start again?

Not necessarily. If you passed papers at a recognized university, those credits may be transferable, subject to the receiving university's rules. An assessment tells you what counts. See our page on [credit transfer](/credit-transfer).

### 15. What is the Academic Bank of Credits and APAAR ID?

The Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) is a national digital record of the credits you earn at registered institutions. Your APAAR ID links you to that record. It helps credits move between institutions registered on ABC, subject to the receiving university's rules.

### 16. My studies were many years ago. Can they still count?

Sometimes. Each university sets its own rules on how old earlier credits can be and how closely the syllabus must match. Bring whatever marksheets you have and get an assessment rather than assuming either way.

### 17. I failed a few papers in my old degree. What happens to them?

Papers you passed may count towards credit transfer. Papers you did not pass will usually need to be studied and cleared in the new programme. An assessment will show exactly which.

## Study, examinations and results

### 18. Can I study while working full time?

Yes. Most students we place are working. Expect to plan around six to ten hours a week in fixed slots. Our guide to [studying while working full time](/blog/studying-while-working-full-time) covers the habits that work.

### 19. Are there examinations?

Yes. Every legitimate degree has assessment. Depending on the university and programme, that may be written examinations at a centre, proctored online examinations, assignments, or a mix. Anyone promising a degree without examinations is not offering a real one.

### 20. Do I need to attend classes?

Distance programmes are mainly self-study with printed or digital material. Some include contact sessions or online classes. Ask about the pattern for your programme before you enrol, especially if your work hours are irregular.

### 21. What documents will I receive at the end?

Usually a consolidated marksheet, a provisional certificate, the degree certificate and, on request, a migration certificate. Collect all of them promptly and check that your name matches your 10th certificate exactly.

## Fees, documents and next steps

### 22. How much does a distance degree cost?

It depends on the university and programme. Fees are usually made up of tuition, registration, examination and certificate charges, plus attestation if you need it. A counsellor gives you the full breakdown in writing before you decide.

### 23. Can I pay in instalments?

Yes. Monthly instalment plans with no interest and no processing fee are available.

### 24. What documents do I need to apply?

Typically your 10th and 12th certificates, any college marksheets if you studied before, a bachelor's degree certificate for a master's programme, an identity document and photographs. Once documents are complete, an enrolment number is usually issued within about 48 hours.

### 25. How do I know a provider is genuine?

Ask for the university name, the programme, its DEB entitlement for your admission year and the full fee in writing, then check them yourself on the UGC and DEB websites. A genuine provider will hand these over without hesitation. We never take payment before you have seen all four.
