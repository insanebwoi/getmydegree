An Academic Bank of Credits ID is your personal account number in a national digital record of the credits you earn, and your university asks for it because it needs somewhere to deposit those credits. You create it yourself online through DigiLocker, and the current portal issues it as an APAAR ID. It is useful and now routinely expected at admission, but it is only a record. It does not make you eligible for anything, and it does not oblige any university to accept your earlier study.

Applicants are often asked for this number with no explanation, then told about two or three other IDs that sound like the same thing. This guide sorts those out, explains how to create yours, and is honest about what it will and will not do.

## What is the difference between an ABC ID, an APAAR ID and a DEB ID?

The ABC ID and APAAR ID refer to the same learner identity in practice, while the DEB ID is a separate number for distance and online students. You will hear the names used loosely, so here is how they fit together.

| Name | What it is | Where you get it |
|---|---|---|
| Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) | The national register that stores credits against the student | [abc.gov.in](https://www.abc.gov.in/) |
| APAAR ID | Your permanent lifelong learner ID, which you need in order to use ABC services. Many universities still call it your ABC ID | Through DigiLocker |
| DEB ID | An ID on the UGC Distance Education Bureau portal for students joining distance or online programmes, generated using your ABC ID | [deb.ugc.ac.in](https://deb.ugc.ac.in/) |
| University enrolment number | Your number at one university, and the proof that it has admitted you | Issued by the university |

The order matters, because one depends on another: DigiLocker first, then the APAAR/ABC ID, then the DEB ID. UGC notices from the 2024-25 academic year made the DEB ID a condition of admission to distance and online programmes for Indian learners. Check the DEB portal for the current position when you apply.

## How do I create an ABC ID?

You create it through DigiLocker, using the education section and the APAAR ID service. Menu labels change from time to time, so treat these as the general steps.

1. Sign in to DigiLocker, or create an account and complete its identity authentication. The login options shown on the ABC portal include Aadhaar, PAN and driving licence.

2. Go to the education category and open the APAAR ID creation service.

3. Select your institution, your admission year and your roll number, then submit.

4. Download the APAAR card from DigiLocker and save the number with your admission documents.

We have not seen a charge on this official route. If someone asks you for a fee simply to create the ID, ask them what it is for.

## What goes wrong, and how to avoid it

These are the problems that actually hold up applicants in Kerala, and most of them can be solved before you apply.

- **Your name differs across documents.** Malayali names are a regular source of this. Your SSLC book may show an initial, as in "Anu K", while your Aadhaar spells out the full name. Compare your Aadhaar, 12th certificate and earlier marksheets before you create the ID. It is easier to deal with a mismatch now than after a university has flagged it at enrolment.

- **The authentication code goes to a number you no longer use.** This is common among Gulf returnees and those still working abroad, whose Aadhaar may be linked to an old Indian SIM. Update the mobile number on your Aadhaar first if you need to, and allow time for it.

- **You have no current institution to select.** The form asks for an institution, an admission year and a roll number. If you stopped studying years ago, or your old college is not listed, do not simply pick a random entry to get through the form. Ask the university admitting you what it wants, or ask us.

- **You leave it until enrolment week.** Because the DEB ID depends on this ID, a delay here can hold up the whole application. Do it when you gather your documents. Our [step-by-step application guide](/blog/step-by-step-degree-application-guide) shows where it fits.

## Will my ABC ID show the credits from my old college?

Only if the institution was registered on the system and actually uploaded your records, which is unlikely for study from some years ago. Older credits are not automatically in the register. An empty account does not mean those credits have been lost. It only means they are not in this particular record, and they will be assessed from your paper marksheets in the traditional way.

She is in the same position as someone who has never opened one: the marksheets are the evidence, and it would be a mistake to assume the old semesters are gone because the screen is blank. If credits you expected are missing or wrong, the correction comes from the institution that awarded them, not from the register. Approach that university with your marksheets and ask it to deposit or correct the record.

## What the ABC ID does and does not do

This is where the marketing tends to run ahead of the facts. Credits are stored digitally and can transfer between registered institutions, always subject to the receiving university's rules.

**It does:**

- Give you one lifelong identity that follows you across programmes and institutions.

- Hold credits your registered institutions upload, in a form that can be checked without chasing your old college.

- Make the record useful if you change institution or come back to study after a break.

- Serve as the base for the DEB ID that distance and online students need.

**It does not:**

- Give you a right to admission at a later semester. The receiving university still decides which credits it accepts against its own papers.

- Make an unrecognized institution's study count, or prove that a programme had DEB entitlement in your admission year.

- Replace your marksheets or degree certificate at job verification. Keep the originals.

- Settle whether your degree qualifies you for a post. That depends on the university's recognition, the programme's DEB entitlement for your admission year, and the wording of the job notification.

If anyone tells you that having an ABC ID means your credits are "already transferred", or that it makes your degree valid, they have misunderstood it or are overselling.
