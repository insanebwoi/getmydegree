Regulation in this field changes often enough that any article naming a specific circular will be out of date within a year. What does not change is the shape of the rules, and knowing that shape lets you check the current position yourself.

## The four things regulation decides

Whether the institution is a university at all. Whether it may teach at a distance. Which programmes it may teach that way. And whether the resulting degree is equivalent to a campus one.

Every genuine question an applicant has is one of those four, and each is answered by a different record.

## Entitlement is per programme and per year

This is the point most applicants get wrong. The Distance Education Bureau grants entitlement for named programmes, at named levels, for a defined period. A university entitled to offer a B.Com online is not automatically entitled to offer an MBA online, and entitlement held two years ago is not proof of entitlement now.

When you ask a counsellor about approval, ask for it in that form: this programme, this mode, this academic session. A general assurance that "the university is UGC approved" does not answer the question.

## Equivalence is settled, in principle

The established position is that a degree earned through a recognized open and distance or online programme is treated as equivalent to the corresponding campus degree, for employment and for further study. That equivalence depends entirely on the programme having been entitled at the time you studied it, which is why the entitlement check is the whole game.

Eligibility for a particular post still follows that recruiter's own notification. Equivalence means the degree is not disqualified for its mode. It does not override a notification that asks for something specific.

## Programmes that cannot be taught this way

Programmes requiring supervised practical or clinical training are generally outside what may be offered at a distance. If a programme in that family is offered to you online, that alone is reason to stop and verify.

## How to check the current position

Go to the UGC and Distance Education Bureau's own websites rather than an aggregator, search for the university by its full legal name, and look for the programme list attached to the current session. Where anything is ambiguous, ask the university directly in writing and keep the reply.
