The UGC rules that matter most for online degree applicants right now are these: you must have a DEB-ID, health-related subjects such as psychology have been withdrawn from online and distance mode, foreign degree equivalence moved to the UGC in 2025, and each admission session has its own last date. The base rule has not changed. Your degree depends on the university's recognition, the programme's DEB entitlement for your admission year, and the wording of any job notification you plan to use it for.

Regulation changes often enough that any article naming a circular can age quickly. This one was checked in November 2026, so treat it as a starting point, and confirm anything important on the Notice Board at the [UGC Distance Education Bureau](https://deb.ugc.ac.in/).

The framework is still the UGC (Open and Distance Learning Programmes and Online Programmes) Regulations, 2020, with later amendments. Entitlement is still given for named programmes and is checked by year, so an approval held two years ago is not proof of approval now. And degrees from properly entitled programmes are still treated as equivalent to conventional ones, as explained in our article on [online and campus degree equivalence](/blog/equivalence-of-online-and-campus-degrees). The changes below sit on top of that.

## Do I need a DEB-ID to join an online degree?

Yes. The DEB portal describes the DEB-ID as a unique ID that a learner must create to enrol in distance or online programmes, and you generate it using an Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) ID. The stated purpose is to make sure learners are admitted only to programmes from recognized institutions.

What this changes for you:

- **Create your ABC ID first.** It can be set up through DigiLocker. Our [ABC ID explainer](/blog/academic-bank-of-credits-abc-id-explained) covers the steps.

- **Fix your details before you start.** If your APAAR or ABC record has an error in your name or date of birth, the portal tells you to correct it there first and only then create or update the DEB-ID. Spelling differences between SSLC and Aadhaar records are worth sorting out early.

- **Keep the ID yourself.** It is your record. Do not let an agent create it and leave you without the details.

The form also asks you to confirm that you have verified the recognized programmes and institutions. That is a reason to actually look at the listing.

## Can I still study psychology or other health subjects online?

Not in the health-related subjects the UGC has withdrawn. In 2025 the UGC decided that allied and healthcare programmes covered by the National Commission for Allied and Healthcare Professions Act, 2021 may not be offered in distance or online mode from the July-August 2025 session. Reports of the UGC notice name psychology, microbiology, food and nutrition science, biotechnology, and clinical nutrition and dietetics.

What this changes for you:

- If a counsellor offers one of these subjects online or by distance for a current session, stop and ask to see where it is listed for that session.

- In a degree with several specialisations, such as a B.A. with a range of subject choices, reports say only the health-related subject is withdrawn. The rest of the programme can continue. Ask the university which subject combinations are open before you choose.

- If you were admitted before the change and are affected, ask the university in writing how your programme is being handled. Do not rely on the agent's reply alone.

Other programmes have been barred for longer. Engineering, medicine, nursing, pharmacy, law, architecture and similar programmes are not permitted in distance or online mode, and the DEB portal publishes the prohibited list.

## How has foreign degree equivalence changed?

Equivalence for foreign degrees moved from the Association of Indian Universities to the UGC, under regulations notified in April 2025. It is not automatic, and franchised qualifications are excluded.

What this changes for you: if you are considering a UK or other overseas online degree and intend to use it in India, the question is no longer just "is this university genuine". Ask whether the qualification is one the UGC would consider, how it is delivered, and whether anything in the arrangement is a franchise. Anyone who says a foreign degree is the same as an Indian one without mentioning this is skipping a real step.

## When is the last date for online degree admission?

It changes with each session, so check the current notice rather than last year's date. For the August 2026 session, the DEB portal gives 13 October 2026 as the last date to admit learners, after an extension from 15 September. That session has now closed.

The UGC's public notice of 31 August 2026 asked institutions planning programmes from the January/February 2027 session to apply through the DEB portal between 1 and 15 September 2026, and it was reported that applying does not itself secure recognition.

What this changes for you: a university may be taking enquiries for January 2027 before the list for it is settled. If the listing for that session is not showing your programme, "approval is in process" is not an entitlement. Wait, ask for it in writing, or choose a programme already listed. Our article on [the July and January admission cycles](/blog/degree-admission-cycles-july-and-january) explains how the two sessions work.

## Which rules are being talked about but are not yet relevant?

Two come up often in sales conversations.

- **Shorter degrees under the 2025 regulations.** The UGC's Minimum Standards of Instruction Regulations, 2025 brought in accelerated and extended routes for undergraduate degrees. We could not confirm from the material we checked that these apply to distance or online programmes. If someone offers you a shortened distance degree citing them, ask the university for the written rule.

- **A single new regulator.** The Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan Bill, 2025 proposes one body to replace the UGC, AICTE and NCTE. As of our last check, PRS listed it as still with a Joint Committee of Parliament. It is not law, so the UGC rules above continue to apply. Nobody can honestly tell you what it means for your degree yet.

One other development is not a UGC rule at all but matters just as much. In January 2026 the Kerala High Court, in *Kerala PSC v. Vaisakh C.L.*, held that where Special Rules or a notification require a "regular course of study", a distance degree does not satisfy it. Our [Kerala PSC article](/blog/distance-degree-valid-kerala-psc) explains how far that goes.

## How do I check the current position myself?

Use the DEB portal's Notice Board and Recognised Programmes lists, and the UGC's own site, rather than an aggregator or a provider's summary. Search by the university's full legal name and select your session. Where anything is unclear, ask the university in writing and keep the reply.
