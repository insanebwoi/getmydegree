GATE, the Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering, is the national exam that most engineers in India use for two things: getting into an M.Tech at a government-supported institution, and applying for engineering jobs at the many public sector undertakings (PSUs) that recruit through GATE scores. A GATE score stays valid for three years from the date the results are announced.

Working engineers can and do write GATE. There is no rule that you must be a fresh graduate, and the score can be used for an M.Tech, including programmes for working professionals, or for a PSU application. The hard part is preparation alongside a job, and choosing what you want the score for before you start. This guide covers both.

## What is GATE?

GATE is a computer-based national examination in engineering, technology, architecture, science and humanities subjects. It is conducted jointly by the Indian Institute of Science and the IITs on behalf of the National Coordination Board – GATE, Ministry of Education, with one institute organising it each year.

For GATE 2027, the organising institute is IIT Madras. According to its July 2026 announcement, GATE 2027 has 30 test papers, the syllabus has been revised for the first time in five years, and a new paper in Robotics and Automation has been introduced. Candidates can write one paper, or two from the permitted combinations.

## When is GATE 2027, and is registration still open?

According to the [official GATE 2027 website](https://gate2027.iitm.ac.in/), the exam is scheduled for 6, 7, 13, 14, 20 and 21 February 2027, with results on 19 March 2027. The site states that new application registration is closed, and that the dates are liable to change.

So if you did not register by the deadline in October 2026, GATE 2027 is no longer an option, and your realistic target is GATE 2028. Watch the official website of the organising institute for that cycle; we will not guess its dates here. Starting preparation now, a full year ahead, suits a working engineer far better than a rushed few months.

## Who is eligible to write GATE?

For B.E. and B.Tech candidates, the GATE 2027 eligibility rules allow those currently in the third year or higher, as well as those who have already completed the degree. The eligibility page sets no cut-off on the year you graduated, so a working engineer who finished years ago can still apply.

Some professional qualifications are also accepted under conditions. AMIE from the Institution of Engineers (India), for example, is listed, subject to completion of Section A or its equivalent and to the qualification being approved as equivalent to a B.E./B.Tech by the authorities the brochure names. Read the full eligibility criteria for your cycle on the official website, because they are revised each year. Our article on [AMIE vs B.Tech](/blog/amie-vs-btech) explains why AMIE is treated differently in different places.

## How is GATE used for M.Tech admission?

A valid GATE score is used for admission to master's and direct doctoral programmes at institutions supported by the Ministry of Education and other government-supported institutions, often with financial assistance for eligible students. Some other colleges also consider GATE scores for postgraduate admission.

A few things to understand:

- **A GATE score is not an admission.** Each institute applies its own eligibility rules, cut-offs and selection process.
- **Financial assistance depends on the institute and category of seat.** Check the current rules of the institute rather than assuming every GATE-qualified student receives it.
- **In Kerala, M.Tech admission to many colleges runs through the annual prospectus of the Directorate of Technical Education.** How GATE and non-GATE applicants are ranked is set out there each year.

If you want to keep your job while studying, M.Tech programmes for working professionals are worth a look. Our guide to M.Tech for working professionals in Kerala covers what is available.

## Which PSUs recruit through GATE?

Many PSUs use GATE scores in recruitment for engineering posts. The official GATE 2027 website lists, as a non-exhaustive set, organisations such as AAI, BHEL, BSNL, Coal India, GAIL, HAL, IOCL, NHAI, NPCIL, NTPC, ONGC, POWERGRID, RINL and SAIL, among others.

How it usually works:

1. You write GATE in the paper that matches your branch.
2. A PSU publishes its own recruitment notification, saying which GATE paper and which year's score it will accept.
3. You apply to the PSU separately, quoting your GATE registration details.
4. The PSU uses the GATE score to shortlist, and its notification says whether further stages, such as an interview, follow.

The GATE organisers state plainly that qualifying in GATE does not guarantee admission, a scholarship or a job. Every PSU notification has its own age limit, branch list, minimum marks in the B.Tech and other conditions, so read each one in full.

## Can working engineers really prepare for GATE?

Yes, but only with a plan that respects your working hours. GATE tests the core of your B.Tech branch, plus general aptitude and engineering mathematics, and those are subjects that fade if you have spent years on site or in a plant.

What tends to work for people with a full-time job:

- **Fix the goal first.** An M.Tech target and a PSU target need different scores and different timing.
- **Use the official syllabus for your paper** as your checklist, especially since it was revised for GATE 2027.
- **Study in short, regular blocks** on weekday evenings and longer blocks at weekends, rather than in bursts before the exam.
- **Practise under time pressure** with past papers, because the test is computer-based and timed.
- **Plan leave around the exam weeks,** which are usually in February.

## What if I do not have a B.Tech yet?

Then GATE comes later. You need to be at least in the third year of a B.E./B.Tech, or hold a qualification that the GATE rules accept, before you can apply.

For diploma holders working in Kerala, the usual route is to complete a recognised B.Tech first, either through regular lateral entry via the LET or through the KTU B.Tech for working professionals at an AICTE-approved college. Our guide to [B.Tech for working professionals in Kerala](/blog/btech-working-professionals-kerala) explains eligibility and admission. A distance or online B.Tech is not valid in India and will not help you here; see [distance B.Tech in Kerala](/blog/distance-btech-kerala).

## Is GATE the only way to a postgraduate degree while working?

No. GATE is the main route for an M.Tech at government-supported institutions, but it is not the only kind of postgraduate study.
