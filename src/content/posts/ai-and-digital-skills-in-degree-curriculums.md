Nearly every university now advertises something with artificial intelligence or data in the name. Some of it is a genuine addition to the curriculum. Some of it is the same syllabus with a new cover. Telling them apart is not difficult.

## What a real addition looks like

Ask for the paper list, not the programme brochure. A genuine specialisation shows up as named papers with credit weightings attached: statistics and data analysis, database systems, an applied analytics paper, a project. If the only evidence is a line in the marketing copy and the paper list is unchanged from the ordinary programme, the specialisation exists in the brochure.

The second question: does the assessment involve producing anything. A specialisation assessed entirely through written examination teaches you to describe the tools. One with a project makes you use them. For a CV, the second is worth considerably more.

## Where these electives genuinely help

In commerce and management, analytics electives map directly onto work people are already doing badly in spreadsheets, and the skill transfers to the job immediately.

In science and computing, the additions are usually substantive because the base programme already supports them.

In general arts programmes, a digital marketing or applied analytics elective is often the most employable component of the degree.

## What it does not replace

An elective inside a degree is not a substitute for a specialist qualification, and nobody should choose a university on this basis alone. The degree is the credential. The elective is a useful line underneath it.

It also should not override the practical factors. A programme with an excellent analytics track and examinations you cannot physically attend is the wrong programme.

## Questions worth asking before you enrol

- Which named papers make up the specialisation, and what are their credits.

- Is there a project or capstone, and is it assessed.

- Does the final marksheet and degree name the specialisation, or only the base degree.

That last one matters more than people expect. If the specialisation does not appear on the certificate, it is something you mention in an interview rather than something an employer's filter will read.
