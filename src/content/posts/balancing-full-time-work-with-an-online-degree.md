People rarely abandon a degree because the material was too hard. They abandon it because a bad month became a bad semester and re-entry felt impossible. Nearly all of the difference is in how the time is arranged.

## Fix the hours before the semester starts

Eight to ten hours a week is the realistic figure for most programmes. Decide which hours, specifically, and put them in the same place every week. A fixed weekday evening and a longer weekend block is the pattern that survives longest.

The point of fixing them in advance is that you stop making the decision. A study slot you negotiate with yourself each week is one you will lose to a bad day at work.

Tell the people you live with which hours those are. Most failures at this stage are domestic rather than academic.

## Work backwards from the assessment dates

Get the semester calendar on day one and mark the assignment deadlines and examination dates in the same place you keep your work calendar. Then look for collisions with your known busy periods and deal with them now, while there is room to move.

A deadline you meet three days early costs the same hours as one you meet at midnight and costs nothing in stress.

## Study in a way that suits the assessment

If the programme is assessed by written examination, practise writing answers under time rather than re-reading material. If it is assessed by assignments, start each one the week it is set and leave it open, rather than treating it as a single evening's work.

Re-reading feels productive and is the weakest use of the hours. Answering questions from memory is uncomfortable and is what actually works.

## Plan for the bad month

There will be one: a project deadline, an illness, a family situation. Know in advance what the university's rules are for a deferred examination, a re-sit, or a break in study, and what they cost. Knowing the mechanism exists is what makes a bad month survivable rather than terminal.

If you miss something, contact the university that week. The gap between a problem and asking about it is what turns one paper into a lost semester.

## Use your work

Where assignments allow a real example, use one from your job. It is faster to write, it is better than an invented case, and it makes the degree immediately useful rather than parallel to your working life.
