Engineering work in Kerala in 2026 is concentrated in a handful of places: the state's IT parks, Cochin Shipyard in Kochi, the Vizhinjam International Seaport near Thiruvananthapuram, government departments such as PWD, the Kerala Water Authority and KSEB, and private construction and contracting, much of it connected to the Gulf. Each hires different kinds of engineers, and each has its own qualification rules.

We do not give hiring numbers here, because reliable figures are rarely published and they change quickly. What we can tell you is where the work is, how each employer recruits, and what qualification it asks for, so you can plan your degree and skills around a real target.

## Where are the engineering jobs in Kerala in 2026?

The main employers of engineers in Kerala are IT and electronics companies in the IT parks, the shipbuilding and port sector, state government departments and boards, and private construction and engineering contractors. Smaller numbers work in manufacturing, renewable energy installation and teaching.

For many Kerala engineers, the Gulf remains part of the picture, either directly or through contractors who work in both places. It is worth deciding early whether your target is a Kerala job, a Gulf job, or one that leads to the other, because the qualification checks differ.

## What engineering work is there in Kerala's IT parks?

Kerala's government-owned IT parks are Technopark in Thiruvananthapuram, Infopark in Kochi and Cyberpark in Kozhikode. Most of the work is software development, testing and IT services, with some electronics, embedded systems and engineering services firms as well.

These jobs suit computer science, electronics and IT graduates most directly, though engineers from other branches move in through data, testing and design-software roles. Companies recruit on their own, through campus drives and job portals. Skills and projects matter a great deal here; our article on [the skills employers want from engineers in 2026](/blog/engineering-skills-2026-employers) covers what to build.

## What kind of engineers does Cochin Shipyard employ?

Cochin Shipyard is a Government of India company under the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways, set up in 1972, that builds and repairs ships. It was chosen to build the country's first indigenous Air Defence Ship, and its ship repair work covers Navy, Coast Guard and merchant vessels. That needs mechanical, electrical, electronics, civil and naval architecture skills.

The shipyard also runs training for graduate engineers to become marine engineers. It recruits through its own notifications, published on its careers page, so watch that rather than agents' adverts. Eligibility, including the required degree, is set out in each notification.

## What is the Vizhinjam seaport, and what work does it bring?

The Vizhinjam International Seaport near Thiruvananthapuram was commissioned by the Prime Minister on 2 May 2025. It was developed by Adani Ports and SEZ and has been described in news reports as India's first dedicated deepwater container transshipment port.

A working port needs engineers in operations, maintenance of cranes and equipment, electrical systems, civil works and logistics, and any further phases of development would bring construction work. We have not seen reliable published figures for engineering hiring, so treat claims of large numbers of jobs with care, and apply only through the operator or its listed contractors.

## Which government departments hire engineers in Kerala?

The Public Works Department, Kerala Water Authority, KSEB and the Irrigation Department all employ engineers, and most direct recruitment to Assistant Engineer posts is through Kerala PSC. These posts ask for a B.Sc or B.Tech in the relevant branch from a recognised university, and some civil AE notifications also accept AMIE.

Examples from Kerala PSC notifications:

- **PWD, Assistant Engineer (Civil)** (notification 180/2025), with the pay scale listed as ₹55,200–1,15,300.
- **Kerala Water Authority, Assistant Engineer**, which has asked for an engineering degree in the branches the post lists.
- **KSEB, Assistant Engineer (Electrical)**, which has asked for a degree in electrical engineering, with listed alternatives.

Diploma holders are recruited to overseer and similar grades, and some departments fill part of their AE posts from in-service staff who hold the degree. Our coming guide to [the Kerala PSC Assistant Engineer qualification](/blog/kerala-psc-assistant-engineer-btech) has the details.

## Where do civil and site engineers find work?

Mostly with private builders, contractors and consultancies, from housing and commercial buildings to roads and public works taken on by contract. Some of these firms also work on Gulf projects or recruit for them, so a Kerala site job can sometimes lead to a Gulf posting.

On site, diploma holders usually work as supervisors and degree holders as site or project engineers. If you are a site supervisor with a diploma and your firm reserves the engineer grade for graduates, the degree is what moves you up. Our article on [diploma vs B.Tech for grade and promotion](/blog/diploma-vs-btech-salary-promotion) explains how.

## Are there jobs in electrical, solar and EV work?

Yes, and this is an area of steady growth. KSEB is a major public employer of electrical engineers, while rooftop solar installation, EV charging and service, and electrical contracting are growing in the private sector. These jobs suit electrical and electronics engineers who add solar design, drives and power electronics to their skills.

Our coming guide to [EEE careers in KSEB, solar and EV](/blog/btech-electrical-solar-ev-jobs) looks at this in more depth.

## Which qualification do you need for these jobs?

For engineer posts, a recognised engineering degree, earned in regular mode. Kerala PSC AE posts, engineer grades in most companies and Gulf engineer registration all ask for it. A diploma qualifies you for technician, supervisor and overseer roles.

If you are a working diploma holder, the valid ways to get the degree are the KTU B.Tech for working professionals, with evening or weekend classes, or regular lateral entry through the LET. A B.Tech by distance or online mode is not valid in India. Our guide to [B.Tech for working professionals in Kerala](/blog/btech-working-professionals-kerala) explains the eligibility, and the [distance B.Tech guide](/blog/distance-btech-kerala) explains why the distance route is closed.
