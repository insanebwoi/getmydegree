Most people who call us are not choosing between a degree and no degree. They are choosing between the degree they think they should do and the one they can actually finish while holding a job. Those are different questions, and the second one decides whether you graduate.

## Start from the reason, not the subject

There are usually three reasons behind the call. A promotion that needs a formal qualification on file. A government post whose notification asks for a recognized degree. Or a change of field that needs a credential to make the application credible.

Each points somewhere different. A promotion usually needs any recognized degree, quickly, in a related discipline. A government post needs the qualification the notification names, from a recognized university, with nothing ambiguous about the mode. A change of field needs the subject to match where you are going, which is the only case where the specialization matters more than the speed.

Name the reason before you look at any programme list. It removes most of the list.

## Judge the time commitment honestly

A three-year UG or two-year PG programme delivered in a flexible mode is usually built around eight to ten hours a week, concentrated before assignment deadlines and examinations. That is not a small number when you already work six days.

The realistic question is not whether you can find ten hours in a good week. It is whether you can find them in a bad one. Programmes fail on the bad weeks. If your work has predictable busy seasons, say so during counselling, because it changes which intake you should take and sometimes which university.

## What actually separates the options

- The mode. Open and distance learning and fully online learning differ in how you attend, how you are assessed and how much of the schedule you control.

- The assessment pattern. Continuous assignments spread the load; a single end-of-semester examination concentrates it. Neither is better, but one will fit your work rhythm better than the other.

- The specialization. At PG level this shapes the CV line. At UG level, employers mostly read the degree, not the electives.

- Credit for what you already did. If you left a degree partway through, some of it may still count, which changes the time and the cost more than any other factor.

## The shortest path is usually the one you finish

We would rather place someone into a programme they complete than the more impressive one they abandon in the second semester. A B.Com finished in three years does more for a career than an unfinished MBA.
