You spot a fake degree provider by checking records, not by judging how professional the office looks. Ten checks cover almost every case: the UGC's list of fake universities, the UGC's list of recognized ones, the programme's DEB entitlement for your session, the university's own website, who the receipt is made out to, the enrolment number, the programme duration, the date on the degree, the examination arrangements, and how the degree will later be verified. Each one is a yes or no. If any one fails, stop.

Our earlier piece on [red flags before enrolling in an online degree](/blog/red-flags-before-enrolling-in-an-online-degree) describes the warning signs in what a provider says. This one is the checklist: the specific things to look up and the documents to ask for.

## Checks on the official record

### 1. Search the UGC list of fake universities

The UGC publishes, and periodically updates, a list of self-styled institutions that call themselves universities without any legal authority to award degrees. It is on the UGC's official website. Search it for the exact name you have been given, and for close variants, because fake institutions often use names that sound like real ones.

Not appearing on this list proves nothing on its own. Appearing on it ends the conversation.

### 2. Confirm the university is on the UGC's recognized lists

A genuine university in India is set up by an Act of Parliament or a State legislature, or declared a deemed-to-be university. The UGC lists central, state, state private and deemed universities separately. Find the institution on the correct list, and note its exact name and location as the UGC records them. You will use that in the next checks.

If the name on your brochure differs from the UGC's record by a word, or the city is different, ask why before going further.

### 3. Check DEB entitlement for this programme, mode and session

Recognition as a university does not allow it to teach at a distance. Distance and online programmes need separate entitlement from the UGC's Distance Education Bureau, granted for particular programmes, in a particular mode, for particular academic sessions.

Ask the provider for the entitlement in writing, naming the programme and the session you are joining, then find it on the DEB's own site. "Approval is under process" is not an entitlement. Our guide to [verifying UGC and DEB approval](/blog/ugc-deb-approval-search-verify-recognition) walks through the search.

### 4. Find the programme on the university's official website

Type the university's web address yourself, taking it from the UGC's record rather than from a brochure, a message or a search advertisement. The programme you are being offered should appear on that site, in that mode. Look out for domains that copy a real university's name with an extra word or a different ending.

## Checks on the money and paperwork

### 5. Insist on a receipt in the university's name

Every payment should produce a receipt that shows where the money went. Where the fee goes to the university, the university's name should be on the receipt or the payment confirmation. Where an admission partner collects part of it as a service fee, that should be stated clearly and separately, with the university fee shown on its own.

A handwritten slip, a receipt in the name of a person, or a payment to a personal account or UPI ID with no further paperwork is a reason to stop paying.

### 6. Get the enrolment number and check it yourself

A genuine admission produces an enrolment or registration number issued by the university, in your name, usually visible on the university's own student portal. Once your documents are complete, we usually see it issued within about 48 hours.

If weeks go by with no number, or the number only exists on the provider's own website, you do not have an admission. Ask in writing, and if it still does not come, contact the university directly using details from its official site.

## Checks on what you are being sold

### 7. Treat "degree in 3 months" as a fabricated document

Programme duration is fixed by regulation, and a bachelor's degree is a multi-year programme. Any offer of a degree in three months, six months or one year, where no credit transfer has been formally assessed, is not a faster route. It is a document that will not survive verification.

Genuine shortcuts do exist in limited forms, such as credit transfer or lateral entry, but they are decided by the university under its written rules, after it has seen your earlier marksheets. They never let you skip the remaining study itself.

### 8. Refuse any back-dated degree

Some providers offer a degree showing an earlier year of passing, so that it looks as though you graduated before you actually studied. This is not a grey area. A degree dated to years in which you were not enrolled is a fabricated record, and using it for a job, a promotion or a visa puts your employment, and sometimes more, at risk.

Your degree should show the year you actually completed the programme.

### 9. Ask exactly how examinations are held

Every genuine programme assesses you. Ask where examinations take place, whether they are at an examination centre or proctored online, how many there are each semester, and what happens if you miss one. A provider who says you will not have to sit any examination, or offers someone else to sit them for you, is selling something that is not a degree.

Our guide to [proctored exams and assessment models](/blog/proctored-exams-and-assessment-models) explains what legitimate online assessment looks like.

## The check most people forget

### 10. Ask how the degree will be verified later

Employers, Kerala PSC, banks and attestation authorities verify a degree with the university that issued it, not with the agent who sold it. Ask how that verification works: whether the university confirms degrees on request, whether it issues them through DigiLocker, and whether your credits are recorded with the Academic Bank of Credits against your APAAR ID.

A provider who cannot explain this, or who offers "verification" through a website of its own, is telling you the degree is not meant to be checked.

## A word about unfamiliar accreditations

Fake providers often display certificates from councils, boards or "international accreditation" bodies with official-sounding names. In India, the authority to award a degree comes from the law that created the university, and the regulator for distance and online degrees is the UGC through the DEB. NAAC grades and NIRF ranks are real and checkable on their own sites. An ISO certificate or an overseas accreditation you cannot find on any government record adds nothing.

## Practical next steps

- Write down the exact university name, the programme, the mode and the session you have been offered.

- Run checks 1 to 4 yourself on the official sites. It takes less than half an hour.

- Before paying, ask for the DEB entitlement and the full fee breakdown in writing.

- After paying, keep every receipt and expect an enrolment number you can see on the university's portal.
