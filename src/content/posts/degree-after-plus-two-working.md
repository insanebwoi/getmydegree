If you started working after Plus Two, you can still earn a full bachelor's degree without giving up your job. A distance or online degree from a UGC-recognized university, in a programme that holds Distance Education Bureau entitlement for your admission year, is treated as equivalent to a regular degree under the UGC's 2020 regulations. The eligibility is a pass in the 12th from a recognized board. What you need to get right is the choice of programme and the checks before you pay.

## Who this is for

A great many people in Kerala went to work at seventeen or eighteen. Some had to support family, some got an offer they could not refuse, some simply did not see the point of college at the time. Ten years later they are supervisors in a supermarket, accountants in a family business, technicians, sales staff, Gulf workers home on leave, or running their own shop, and the missing degree has become the thing that stops a promotion, a transfer or an application.

A typical case we see, described here as an illustrative composite rather than a real person: someone in their late twenties who joined a textile showroom after Plus Two, now manages the billing section, and has been told the next step up needs a degree. They cannot leave the job, they work six days a week, and they have not opened a textbook in a decade. People in exactly that position finish degrees every year, mostly by fixing a few hours a week and sticking to them.

If that is you, the route is open. It does not require leaving work, moving to a campus town or attending daily classes.

## Eligibility in plain terms

For the bachelor's programmes we place students into, the requirement is a pass in the 12th from a recognized board. That includes Kerala Higher Secondary, Vocational Higher Secondary, CBSE, ICSE, other state boards, and recognized open schooling systems.

- The stream you studied in Plus Two can matter for some programmes. A science background is commonly expected for a B.Sc, for example, while B.Com, BBA and BA admissions are usually open across streams. The university's prospectus is the final word.

- There is generally no upper age limit for distance and online admission. Check the specific university's rules all the same.

- Keep your 10th certificate ready as well. It is the usual proof of your name and date of birth, and every later document should match it exactly.

- If you passed Plus Two a long time ago, see our guide on [starting a degree after a long gap](/blog/degree-after-long-gap) for the documents and the affidavit some universities ask for.

## Choosing a programme by what you want it for

Start from the reason, not from the course name.

- **For accounts, billing, banking or finance work:** a B.Com is the most direct fit and is widely understood by employers.

- **For supervisory, sales or business roles, or running your own business:** a BBA covers management, marketing and operations.

- **For IT support, software or data work:** a BCA is the computing degree, and suits people already working near technology.

- **For Kerala PSC and other government recruitment that asks for any graduate degree:** a BA is a common choice, though any recognized degree meets a general graduate requirement.

- **For a science route:** a B.Sc, where the subject and the practical arrangements suit you.

## The checks you do before paying

This part protects you more than anything else in the process.

- The university must appear in the UGC's lists of recognized universities.

- The programme must hold DEB entitlement for distance or online mode for the academic session you are joining. Ask for it in writing, naming the programme and the year.

- The programme should appear on the university's own official website.

- If you have a specific government post in mind, read its notification. Following the Kerala High Court's January 2026 decision in *Kerala Public Service Commission v. Vaisakh C.L.*, where a post's rules require a qualification earned through a regular course of study, a distance degree does not meet that requirement. Most posts that ask for a graduate degree do not say this, but you must check the ones you care about.

We never take a payment before you have seen the university name, the programme, its DEB entitlement and the full fee in writing. Our guide to [verifying UGC and DEB approval](/blog/ugc-deb-approval-search-verify-recognition) shows you how to check these yourself.

## Fitting it around a working week

Most working students manage with six to ten hours a week, fixed in advance. Two weekday evenings and a longer block at the weekend is the pattern that holds up best. Reading can be done in fragments: on the bus, at lunch, in the quiet hour of a shift. Writing assignments and preparing for examinations need longer uninterrupted time.

Shift workers and people whose hours change week to week should look closely at the assessment model before choosing. Some programmes rely on scheduled examinations at a centre, others on proctored online examinations, others on a mix with assignments. Pick the pattern you can actually turn up for. Our piece on [finishing a degree while working full time](/blog/studying-while-working-full-time) covers the habits that separate those who finish from those who stall.

## How fees are usually structured

A degree fee is usually made up of several parts: tuition, a registration fee, examination fees, and later charges for the certificate and, if you need it for work abroad, attestation. Ask for every component in writing before you commit, so nothing appears for the first time in year three.

We offer monthly instalments with no interest and no processing fee, which suits most people on a salary. The counsellor gives you the full fee breakdown for the specific university and programme in writing. Our guide to [what a degree costs and how instalments work](/blog/what-a-degree-costs-and-how-emi-works) explains the structure further.

## Telling your employer

You do not need your employer's permission to study. It still helps to mention it. A manager who knows you have an examination is more likely to adjust a shift than one who finds out the day before. And when the degree arrives, the person deciding your promotion already knows you earned it while doing the job.

If your employer reimburses education costs or links a degree to a pay grade, ask HR what documents they will need at the end, so you collect them in the right form.

## Practical next steps

- Find your Plus Two certificate and marksheet and your 10th certificate, and check the name and date of birth match.

- Write down, in one line, what you want the degree for.

- Shortlist one or two programmes that fit that purpose.

- Ask for the university name, the DEB entitlement for your session and the full fee, all in writing.

- Verify the university and the entitlement on the official UGC and DEB sites before paying.

Once your documents are complete, admission is handled end to end and the enrolment number is usually issued within about 48 hours.
