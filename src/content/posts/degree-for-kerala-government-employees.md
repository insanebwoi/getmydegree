A recognized distance or online degree can help a Kerala government employee, police officer or KSRTC staff member move up, but whether it counts for a particular promotion depends on the rules of your own service, not on the degree alone. Promotions are governed by the Special Rules (or service regulations) for each post, and those rules set the qualification, the feeder category and sometimes the mode of study. Before you enrol, read the rules for the post you are aiming at, get your department's permission to study, and confirm the programme is recognized for your admission year.

## Why a degree matters in government service

Many people join government service with a 12th, a diploma or an incomplete degree. For years that is fine. Then a promotion list comes round and the next post up needs a graduate qualification, or a by-transfer appointment to another post is open only to graduates. Colleagues with the same seniority move ahead.

There is a practical side as well. Promotion lists are drawn up periodically, and a qualification acquired after the relevant date may have to wait for the next list. Starting earlier rather than later gives you more room. Since a bachelor's degree is a three-year programme, unless earlier study can be counted through credit transfer, the timing matters.

A degree can also open the door to direct recruitment through Kerala PSC for higher posts, including some recruitment streams set aside for serving employees. In each case the notification or the Special Rules decide who is eligible.

## Promotion depends on your Special Rules

Every post in Kerala government service is covered by rules that say how it is filled: by promotion, by transfer from another category, by direct recruitment, or a mix. Those rules also say what qualification is needed.

- **Find the Special Rules for the post you want.** Your office, establishment section or service association can usually point you to them. They are the controlling document, not a circular or a colleague's memory.

- **Read the qualification clause carefully.** "A degree of a recognized university" is different from "a degree in a specified subject", and both are different from "a degree obtained through a regular course of study".

- **Check whether a departmental test is also required.** In many services, promotion depends on passing specified departmental tests as well as holding the qualification. A degree does not replace those.

- **Check the date that counts.** Usually you must hold the qualification by a particular date for your name to be considered.

## The regular-course caveat

Under the UGC (Open and Distance Learning Programmes and Online Programmes) Regulations, 2020, a degree from a recognized university earned through distance or online mode, with valid DEB entitlement, is treated as equivalent to a conventional degree. That is the general position.

But a service's own rules can ask for more. Where Special Rules or a notification require a qualification earned through a "regular course of study", a distance degree does not satisfy them. The Kerala High Court applied this in January 2026 in *Kerala Public Service Commission v. Vaisakh C.L.* (2026:KER:6103), a case about the post of Librarian Grade IV, where a distance qualification was held not to meet a regular-course requirement.

For most promotion and by-transfer rules that simply ask for a degree, this will not arise. But check. Our explainer on [whether a distance degree is valid for government jobs](/blog/is-a-distance-degree-valid-for-government-jobs) goes through the ruling.

## Permission to study

Government employees are generally expected to inform their department, and in many offices to obtain permission or a no-objection certificate, before joining a course of higher study. The exact requirement and wording vary by department, so ask your own office how it is handled.

Where permission is given, it is commonly on conditions along these lines:

- the course must not interfere with your official duties;

- the government bears no cost for it;

- any time off for examinations or contact classes comes from your own eligible leave;

- the permission can be withdrawn by the department.

Apply in writing through proper channels and keep a copy of the order. If your office has a standard format, use it. If it does not, a short letter stating the university, the programme, its mode of study and its duration, along with a confirmation that study will be outside office hours, is usually what is expected. When you later submit the degree for promotion or service book entry, having the permission on file avoids awkward questions.

Study leave under the Kerala Service Rules is a separate matter with its own conditions. Most employees doing a distance degree do not need it, because the study happens outside working hours.

## Police personnel

Police work brings irregular shifts, festival and election duty, and little control over your own calendar. That makes the choice of programme matter more than usual.

- Choose a programme whose examination pattern you can realistically attend. Ask how examinations are scheduled and what happens if you are on duty that day.

- Get your permission from the appropriate authority in your unit before enrolling, not after.

- Check the promotion or by-transfer rules for the post you want. Police ranks have their own rules and departmental requirements.

## KSRTC staff

KSRTC is a state corporation, so its employees are governed by the corporation's own service regulations rather than the general Special Rules for government departments. The principle is the same: find the regulation that governs the post you want, read the qualification clause, and check whether the corporation requires permission for higher study. Shift patterns for drivers, conductors and depot staff make a fixed weekly study plan essential.

Rotating duties are the main risk. A week of early shifts followed by a week of late ones means your study slots move too. Plan two sets of study hours, one for each pattern, and switch between them, rather than trying to keep one fixed time that only works half the month. Reading fits well into breaks at the depot; writing practice needs a quieter block at home.

## Choosing the degree

- **Any-discipline requirement:** a B.Com, BA or BBA all satisfy "a degree of a recognized university".

- **Subject-specific requirement:** match the subject exactly. If the rule names commerce, a BA will not do.

- **Already a graduate:** a postgraduate degree such as an MBA or M.Com may help for posts that ask for one, or for later movement into administrative roles.

If you studied part of a degree earlier, [credit transfer](/credit-transfer) may reduce how much remains. Whatever you choose, confirm the university is recognized and the programme had DEB entitlement in your admission year.
