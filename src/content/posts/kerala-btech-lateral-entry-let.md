In Kerala, a diploma holder does not have to start a B.Tech from the first year. Through lateral entry you join the third semester, which is the start of the second year, and finish in three years. The gateway is the B.Tech Lateral Entry Test (LET), conducted by LBS Centre for Science and Technology for the Directorate of Technical Education.

Diploma holders are the main group, but D.Voc and some B.Sc holders can also use it. The same test is used for KTU's B.Tech for working professionals, so it matters whether you plan to study full-time or keep your job. This guide covers both.

## What is B.Tech lateral entry in Kerala?

Lateral entry is direct admission into the third semester of a B.Tech for students who already hold a qualification that covers the first year. In Kerala, the qualifying exam is the LET, and admission is to engineering colleges affiliated to KTU (APJ Abdul Kalam Technological University).

You complete the remaining six semesters, with the same labs, project and examinations as other students, and receive the same B.Tech. It is not a shortcut and not a different degree. Our general explainer on [lateral entry into a degree](/blog/lateral-entry-degree) shows how it differs from credit transfer.

## Who can write the Kerala LET?

Holders of a diploma in engineering, a D.Voc, or a B.Sc can apply, subject to the minimum marks set in the prospectus for the year. The 2025-26 prospectus described eligible diplomas as a minimum three-year diploma, a two-year lateral-entry diploma, or a three-year D.Voc in the same or an allied sector.

For reference, KTU's working-professional notice and college pages for 2026 set these conditions:

- a diploma in engineering (three-year, or two-year lateral) with 45% marks (40% for reserved categories);
- a B.Sc with 45% marks and Mathematics at Plus Two; B.Sc candidates may need to clear Engineering Graphics and Engineering Mechanics;
- a B.Voc or a three-year D.Voc.

The regular lateral-entry prospectus may state its conditions differently, so read the current one line by line before you apply.

Students in the final year of their diploma may also apply for the LET, so you do not have to lose a year waiting for your result. You will still need to pass the diploma with the required marks before admission is confirmed.

Keep your semester marksheets ready. If a final result is delayed, ask the college what provisional document it will accept and by what date.

## Who conducts the LET, and when is it held?

LBS Centre for Science and Technology conducts the LET on behalf of the Directorate of Technical Education, Kerala. Applications are made online through lbsapplications.kerala.gov.in during the window announced in the notification.

Use only the official portal. Do not pay an agent to "book" a seat or get a "direct" lateral-entry admission; there is no such thing outside the published process. If you are unsure whether a website is genuine, compare it with the links on the LBS Centre and DTE home pages listed at the end of this article.

The 2026 LET was held on 13 June 2026. The 2027 notification has not been published yet, so we cannot give you a date, and you should be cautious of anyone who does. Check the LBS Centre website regularly from the start of 2027.

## What is the qualifying mark for the LET?

For the 2026 test, the minimum qualifying mark was reported as 15% for the general category, with a lower figure for reserved categories. Qualifying only puts you on the rank list; a higher score gives you a better rank and a wider choice of colleges and branches.

The test pattern, syllabus and marking scheme are set out in the prospectus. Read that section first when the 2027 notification comes out, and prepare from it rather than from coaching-centre summaries.

## Does my diploma branch have to match the B.Tech branch?

Usually it has to be the same or an allied branch. The 2025-26 prospectus spoke of diplomas "in the same or allied sector", so a mechanical diploma normally leads to B.Tech Mechanical, an electrical diploma to Electrical and Electronics, and so on.

If you hold a diploma in a branch that is not offered near you, or want to switch fields, check the allied-branch table in the current prospectus before you list your options. Do not assume you can switch to an unrelated branch through lateral entry.

## Can I do lateral entry while working?

Yes, through KTU's B.Tech for working professionals. It uses the same LET, admits you to the third semester, and holds classes in the evenings or at weekends and holidays at AICTE-approved colleges with working-professional seats.

The extra conditions are at least one year of continuous full-time employment in a registered organisation, an Employment Certificate from your employer, and a workplace within the permitted distance of the college. Colleges rank working-professional applicants by LET score; if LET-qualified candidates run out, they may admit others on qualifying marks. The full details are in our guide to the [B.Tech for working professionals in Kerala](/blog/btech-working-professionals-kerala).

| | Regular lateral entry | Working-professional B.Tech |
|---|---|---|
| Entry point | Third semester | Third semester |
| Entrance | LET | LET (qualifying marks as a fallback) |
| Classes | Full-time, daytime | Evenings, weekends, holidays |
| Work experience | Not required | At least one year, with Employment Certificate |
| Duration | Three years | Not less than three years |

## How should I prepare for the 2027 LET?

Start with the documents, then the syllabus. Most problems we see are paperwork, not marks.

1. **Gather your marksheets** for every semester of your diploma, D.Voc or B.Sc, plus your SSLC and, if you have it, Plus Two.
2. **Get your community and income certificates** in order early if you plan to claim a reservation or fee concession.
3. **If you are working**, ask your employer for the Employment Certificate now, and confirm that your organisation is registered.
4. **Download the 2027 prospectus** as soon as it is out and read the eligibility, syllabus and allotment sections.
5. **Revise your diploma basics**, especially mathematics and the core subjects of your branch.
6. **Apply only on the official portal**, and save every receipt and confirmation page.

Our article on [studying while working full time](/blog/studying-while-working-full-time) has tips on fitting preparation around shifts.

## What if I do not clear the LET or my marks are too low?

You still have options, but be honest about which ones are real. You can write the LET again in the next cycle. For the working-professional programme, a college may admit candidates on qualifying marks if LET-qualified applicants run out, though you cannot count on that.

What is not an option is a "distance B.Tech" or a lateral entry sold outside the official process. A B.Tech by distance or online mode is not valid in India, as our guide to [distance B.Tech in Kerala](/blog/distance-btech-kerala) explains. If your goal is an IT job rather than engineering, a recognised BCA or B.Sc followed by an MCA may serve you better.
