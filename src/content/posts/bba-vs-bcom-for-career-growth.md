BBA and B.Com sit next to each other on every programme list, cost roughly the same and take the same three years. They lead to noticeably different places.

## What each degree is actually about

B.Com is built around accounting, taxation, commercial law and finance. It is a technical degree in the commercial subjects, and it is the natural base for professional accounting qualifications and for finance roles that expect that grounding.

BBA is built around management: organisational behaviour, marketing, operations, human resources, with accounting present but not central. It is broader and less technical, and it points toward general management, sales, operations and administration.

One trains you in the numbers. The other trains you in running the thing the numbers describe.

## Choose by where you already are

If you work in accounts, audit, banking operations, taxation or anything where a ledger is involved, B.Com matches what you do daily and your experience makes the coursework lighter. It is also the safer base if a professional finance qualification is anywhere in your plan.

If you work in sales, retail management, logistics, administration or customer operations, BBA matches the work and reads correctly to a promotions committee. It is also the more comfortable base for an MBA later, though an MBA will take a B.Com graduate just as readily.

## Where the choice matters less than people think

For government recruitment, most notifications ask for a bachelor's degree in any discipline from a recognized university. Where that is the requirement, either degree qualifies and the choice is purely about your career, not eligibility. Always read the specific notification rather than assuming.

For private employers, the degree is usually a threshold rather than a differentiator at UG level. What the degree does is get the file past the filter.

## The practical differences

- B.Com coursework is heavier on computation and lighter on writing. BBA is the reverse.

- B.Com is easier to self-study if you already work with accounts, because the material is familiar.

- BBA's case-based assessments suit people who write comfortably and dislike quantitative examinations.
