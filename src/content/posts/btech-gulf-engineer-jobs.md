For an Indian engineer heading to the UAE, Qatar or Oman, the job offer is only the first step. The degree has to be attested, and in each of these countries engineering has its own extra check: a stricter recognition route in the UAE, registration with the authorities in Qatar, and a professional classification certificate in Oman. If any one of these fails, the visa or work permit under an engineer's title can stall.

The common thread is that these checks are built for a regular B.Tech from a recognised university. A distance or online B.Tech is not valid in India, and it should not be the degree you carry to the Gulf. Below we go through the shared first step and then each country, with links to the official sources so you can confirm the current rules yourself.

## What checks does an Indian B.Tech go through for a Gulf engineering job?

Usually three layers. First, attestation of the degree in India and by the destination country's embassy. Second, recognition or verification of the degree by the destination country. Third, for engineering roles, some form of registration or professional certificate with the country's engineering authority or society.

The first layer is the same for almost every Gulf country. The second and third differ, and they change more often than people expect. Recruiters in Kerala sometimes quote rules that were true three years ago, so always check the current position with the authority or with your employer's HR team in the Gulf.

## How does degree attestation work for the Gulf?

Attestation is a chain of stamps that confirms your degree is genuine. For an Indian degree it normally runs through verification at state level, then the Ministry of External Affairs, then the embassy or consulate of the country where you will work. Some countries ask for a further step after you arrive.

Start early. The chain involves your university, the state authorities and the MEA, and any missing marksheet or name mismatch sends the file back. Our guide to [degree attestation for the Gulf](/blog/degree-attestation-gulf) explains each stage and the common delays. If you have returned from the Gulf and are planning your next move, [degrees for Gulf returnees](/blog/degree-for-gulf-returnees) may also help.

## Is an Indian engineering degree recognised in the UAE?

A regular engineering degree from a recognised Indian university can be recognised in the UAE, but engineering is treated more strictly than most subjects. When the UAE Ministry of Education [announced its "University Certificates Recognition" system](https://www.moe.gov.ae/En/MediaCenter/News/Pages/MoE-unveils-the-%E2%80%9CUniversity-Certificates-Recognition%E2%80%9D-system-to-replace-the-%E2%80%9CUniversity-Qualification-Equivalency%E2%80%9D-system-f.aspx) in June 2023, replacing the earlier "University Qualification Equivalency" system for foreign degrees, it relaxed several conditions, but stated that specialised professions such as engineering, medicine and law were excluded from those general simplifications.

In plain terms:

- The relaxations in the 2023 system, including acceptance of degrees earned fully by distance or e-learning in general subjects, do not simply carry over to engineering.
- An engineering degree is assessed under the stricter conditions that apply to professional fields.
- Responsibility for higher-education recognition in the UAE has been described differently in different reports since 2023, so check the UAE government's current portal for which ministry handles it and what it asks for.

For engineering posts, some employers and authorities in the UAE may also ask for registration with a professional or licensing body, depending on the emirate and the type of work. Ask your employer exactly what your role needs before you travel.

## Do engineers need to register in Qatar?

Yes, engineers working in engineering professions in Qatar are required to register with the authorities. In May 2018 it was reported that foreign nationals holding work permits in any engineering profession had to register with, and sit an examination set by, the engineering committee of what was then the Ministry of Municipality and Environment, and that those who did not would be unable to renew their permits.

Two cautions:

1. That announcement is several years old, and the ministry has been reorganised and renamed since. The details of registration, exams and exemptions may have changed.
2. The same report noted exemptions, for example for degree holders whose permit is for a non-engineering job and who do not work as engineers.

So if a Qatar offer names you as an engineer, ask the employer which registration you need, whether an exam is involved for your branch, and how long it takes. Do not rely on a recruiter's summary.

## What changed for engineers in Oman in 2025?

From 1 August 2025, Oman's Ministry of Labour stopped issuing or renewing work permits for people under the "engineer" designation unless they hold a valid professional classification certificate. The certificate is issued by the Oman Society of Engineers and must be approved by the Sector Skills Unit for engineering professions.

This applies both to engineers already working in Oman and to those seeking new engineering jobs there. If you are in Oman now, do not leave the certificate until your residence card is close to expiry; if you are applying from Kerala, ask the employer whether the classification will be done before or after you arrive. The assessment criteria and documents are set by the Oman Society of Engineers, so confirm them with the society.

## Why does the mode of your B.Tech matter in the Gulf?

Because every one of these checks starts from the assumption that the degree is a regular, recognised engineering degree. A B.Tech earned by distance or online mode fails that test at home before it reaches the Gulf.

The UGC's 2020 regulations for open, distance and online programmes exclude engineering, and the Supreme Court held in November 2017 that engineering degrees awarded by distance mode by deemed universities without AICTE approval were not valid. A degree that is not valid in India cannot be fixed by attestation. Our guide to [distance B.Tech in Kerala](/blog/distance-btech-kerala) explains this in detail, and [online degrees and Gulf jobs](/blog/online-degree-valid-gulf-jobs) covers the non-engineering subjects where online degrees are treated differently.

A B.Tech for working professionals from an AICTE-approved institution is a regular-mode degree: classes in person, in the evenings or at weekends, with the same laboratories and examinations. Ask the college how the certificate and transcripts describe the programme, and keep that in writing for any Gulf verification.

## How do the three countries compare?

A simple summary, to check against the official sources before you act:

| Country | Engineering-specific check | Who to ask |
|---|---|---|
| UAE | Degree recognition, with engineering excluded from the 2023 general simplifications; registration may apply depending on role and emirate | UAE recognition portal, your employer |
| Qatar | Registration of engineers with the ministry's engineering committee; an exam was announced in 2018 for foreign engineers | Qatar Ministry of Municipality, your employer |
| Oman | Professional classification certificate from the Oman Society of Engineers, required for engineer work permits since August 2025 | Oman Society of Engineers, Ministry of Labour |

Saudi Arabia has its own system through the Saudi Council of Engineers, covered in our separate article on Saudi Council of Engineers registration.

## What should I do before accepting a Gulf engineering offer?

Work through this list while you still have time to fix problems:

- Confirm your degree is a regular B.Tech from a recognised university, with all semester marksheets in hand.
- Start attestation as soon as you are serious about the Gulf.
- Ask the employer, in writing, which recognition and registration steps your role needs, and who pays for them.
- Keep experience letters on letterhead, with dates and duties.
- If you hold AMIE or a diploma rather than a B.Tech, ask the authority how it treats your qualification before you resign from anything. Our comparison of [AMIE vs B.Tech](/blog/amie-vs-btech) explains why this varies.
