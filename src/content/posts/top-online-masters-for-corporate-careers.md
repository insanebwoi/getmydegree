Postgraduate programmes get chosen by reputation more often than by fit. Here is what each of the common ones actually supports, so the choice can be made against the role you want.

## MBA

The broadest option and the one most widely understood by employers. It supports movement into general management, and its specialisation carries most of the signal: finance, marketing, human resources, operations, systems.

It is the right answer when you want to manage a function rather than deepen a technical skill, and when the promotion criterion is written generally enough that a management qualification satisfies it.

## M.Com

A specialist commerce degree, deeper and more technical than an MBA in the accounting and finance subjects. It is the stronger qualification for accounts, audit, taxation and finance operations roles, and for anyone whose career sits inside the numbers rather than above them.

It is also the natural continuation for a B.Com graduate who wants depth rather than breadth, and it is often the better base for teaching or for professional finance qualifications.

## MA in human resources or an HR-specialised MBA

For people already working in HR operations, recruitment or payroll who need a formal qualification to move into a business partner or management role. The HR-specialised MBA is generally read as the more corporate credential; the MA is the more academic one. Both satisfy most promotion criteria.

## Data and analytics M.Sc programmes

The most technical of the group, and the one with the sharpest eligibility conditions: these usually require a quantitative bachelor's degree, and the coursework assumes comfort with statistics and programming.

They are worth it when the role you want is genuinely technical. They are the wrong choice as a general career upgrade, because the coursework is hard in a way that the others are not, and it is unforgiving if the base is not there.

## The way to choose

Write down the title of the role you want in three years. Look at how people currently in that role are qualified. Pick the programme that matches, and pick the specialisation that matches more precisely.

If two options both fit, choose on the practical factors, which is to say the examination pattern, the total cost and the schedule. Those decide whether you finish.

## Before enrolling

Confirm the entitlement for the programme and mode in the current session, the full fee, the examination pattern, and whether the specialisation appears on the certificate. If it does not appear on the certificate, it is a conversation point rather than a credential.
