Yes, a distance degree is valid for most central and all-India government jobs that ask for a degree from a recognized university. That answer depends on three things: the university's recognition, the programme's DEB entitlement for your admission year, and the wording of the job notification. If any one of them fails, the degree may not help you for that post, however good the university's name sounds.

This is the first question almost every applicant asks us, and it deserves a precise answer rather than a reassuring one. This guide looks at the central recruiters: UPSC, SSC, the banks and the railways. If your target is a state post, our separate article on [distance degrees and Kerala PSC](/blog/distance-degree-valid-kerala-psc) covers how to read a PSC notification, and [government jobs with a distance degree in Kerala](/blog/government-jobs-distance-degree-kerala) covers planning.

## Why is a distance degree treated the same as a regular one?

It is treated the same because the UGC's own regulations say so, on conditions. Regulation 22 of the UGC (Open and Distance Learning Programmes and Online Programmes) Regulations, 2020 treats degrees from recognized distance and online programmes as equivalent to the corresponding conventional degrees.

The conditions are checkable in public records before you pay anything.

- **The university.** It must be established by an Act of Parliament or a State Act, or notified as deemed to be a university, and it must appear on the UGC's lists.

- **The programme.** It must have UGC Distance Education Bureau (DEB) entitlement for that mode in the academic year you were admitted.

If a provider cannot show you both, stop there. Our [Get My Degree reviews and validity guide](/blog/get-my-degree-reviews-validity-guide) explains how we check the universities we work with.

## Is a distance degree valid for the UPSC civil services?

Yes, in the ordinary case, because the civil services notification asks for a degree from a university set up by a Central or State Act or a deemed university, or an equivalent qualification, and does not ask about the mode of study. It sets no restriction on the discipline either, so a B.A. or B.Com earned through distance mode meets the bar in the same way as a campus one.

Two cautions apply.

- Other UPSC examinations have their own conditions. Technical services need professional degrees such as engineering, which cannot be earned through distance or online mode in India at all.

- Passing the preliminary examination does not settle eligibility. Documents are examined later in the process, and that is when a gap in your paperwork would come out.

## Is a distance degree accepted for SSC exams?

Yes, and SSC is the recruiter that has been most explicit about the condition. SSC's Combined Graduate Level notices have carried a clause on open and distance degrees. The 2022 notice said such degrees "stand automatically recognized for the purpose of employment to posts and services" under the Central Government, but only where approved by the Distance Education Bureau. It also said that a degree not recognized for the period in which you earned it will not be accepted.

That last point is what catches people. SSC is not asking whether the university is approved today. It is asking whether your programme was approved when you studied it. So a candidate who joined in a year not covered by the entitlement cannot repair the problem later by pointing to a newer approval.

Some SSC notices have also asked such candidates to produce the DEB approval itself at verification. Save a copy of the DEB listing for your admission year while you are still studying, and read the clause in the current notice, since wording can change between cycles. A few CGL posts also ask for a particular subject, so read the post-wise table as well.

## Can I get a bank job with a distance degree?

Yes, because IBPS and SBI officer and clerical recruitment generally ask for graduation in any discipline from a university recognized by the Government of India, or an equivalent the Central Government recognizes. A properly entitled distance degree fits that wording.

Banks tend to be strict about two other things.

- **The cut-off date.** The result usually has to be declared by a date in the notice. A degree you finish a month later does not count for that cycle, so work back from the date before you choose an admission session.

- **The proof of graduation.** You may be asked for the marksheet or certificate along with a stated percentage of marks. Keep the exact figure from your consolidated marksheet ready, because the form and the document have to agree.

Specialist officer posts differ. Many of them ask for a named professional qualification, so check the notice for that post rather than the general rule for banks.

Railway recruitment for graduate-level non-technical posts follows the same general pattern of a degree from a recognized university, with any extra condition stated in the notice.

## The exception: posts that require a regular course

Equivalence has a limit, and it matters for Kerala PSC in particular. Some posts are governed by Special Rules or a notification that asks for a qualification earned through a **regular course of study**. For those posts, a distance degree does not qualify, however well recognized the university.

In January 2026 the Kerala High Court applied exactly that rule. In *Kerala Public Service Commission v. Vaisakh C.L.* (2026:KER:6103), it held that a library science degree earned through distance education could not be accepted for the post of Librarian Grade IV, because the notification required a regular course. An equivalency certificate did not change that. ([Law Trend report](https://lawtrend.in/distance-education-degree-invalid-for-librarian-post-when-rules-mandate-regular-course-of-study-kerala-high-court-set-asides-tribunal-order/) · [judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/118624959/))

The case concerned a Kerala post, but the reasoning is about wording, and wording appears in central notifications too. So before you enrol with a particular post in mind, read its notification and service rules. If they say "regular course" or "regular study", ask us before choosing a distance programme. If they only ask for a graduate degree, a recognized distance degree is generally accepted.

## Where do candidates actually get into trouble?

Most problems at document verification come from paperwork, not from distance mode itself.

| What goes wrong | How to avoid it |
|---|---|
| The DEB entitlement does not cover the admission year | Check the listing for the year you join, not an earlier or later one |
| Name or date of birth differs between documents | Keep your SSLC certificate, degree, marksheets and application identical, and correct errors early |
| Certificates were never collected | Collect the provisional certificate, consolidated marksheet and migration certificate when they are issued |
| The degree arrived after the cut-off | Check the notice date against your final result date before you apply |
| Nothing proves when you enrolled | Keep your admission letter and fee receipts for as long as you hold the degree |

An illustrative composite, not a real person: Anu, a clerk in a private firm in Thrissur, plans to sit SSC CGL. Before she enrols, she checks that the university is on the UGC's lists, finds the B.Com on the DEB listing for her admission year, and saves a copy. She then reads the last CGL notice for the posts she wants and finds no regular-study condition. Four years later, she has the record that verification will ask for, and she did not have to assemble it in a hurry.
