To use an Indian degree in the Gulf, it normally goes through three stages: verification by the state where the university is located, then the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA), then either an apostille or embassy attestation depending on the destination. Saudi Arabia, Oman and Bahrain are parties to the Apostille Convention, so an MEA apostille is the usual end point on the Indian side. The UAE, Qatar and Kuwait are not, so after MEA attestation the document goes to that country's embassy and then to its foreign ministry after you arrive.

That is the shape of it. The details below tell you where each step happens, what to carry, and where people lose weeks.

## Before you start: attestation is not recognition

Attestation confirms that your certificate is genuine and issued by the institution named on it. It does not mean the destination country recognizes the degree for a particular job or licence. For distance and online degrees in particular, that second question can be harder, and we cover it separately in [is an online degree valid for Gulf jobs](/blog/online-degree-valid-gulf-jobs). Sort out recognition first; attestation is wasted money if the employer will not accept the degree anyway.

## Step 1: state-level verification

The MEA attests or apostilles a document only after the state government has authenticated it. For educational certificates, that is usually the education or HRD department of the state where your university is located, or a nodal authority the state has appointed.

For Kerala candidates, two cases are worth separating.

- **Certificates from institutions in Kerala.** NORKA Roots runs certificate authentication centres in Thiruvananthapuram, Ernakulam and Kozhikode. It handles authentication of educational certificates issued by Kerala institutions and can then forward them for embassy attestation or apostille. Its page states clearly that only certificates from educational institutions in Kerala are attested.

- **Certificates from universities outside Kerala.** If your degree is from a university in Tamil Nadu, Madhya Pradesh or elsewhere, NORKA will not authenticate it. State verification has to come from the authority designated by that state. This is the step candidates most often get wrong, and it is why you should confirm the route for your specific university before you book anything.

Some universities also issue a verification letter directly, and some destinations ask for one. Ask the university's examination or verification section what it provides.

## Step 2: MEA attestation or apostille

Once the state has authenticated the document, it goes to the MEA. The MEA does not accept documents from individuals directly; you submit through the outsourced agencies it designates, which have collection offices in several cities. The current list of agencies and the current fee are on the MEA's attestation and apostille page. The government fee is modest, the agency charges a service fee on top, and both change from time to time, so read the page rather than a figure in an old forum post.

What you receive depends on the destination.

- **Apostille.** For countries that are parties to the Apostille Convention, the MEA attaches an apostille. No further embassy attestation is required for those countries.

- **Attestation.** For countries that are not parties, the MEA attests the document, and it then has to go to the destination country's embassy in India.

## Step 3: which Gulf country needs what

According to the Hague Conference's status table for the Apostille Convention:

- **Saudi Arabia** has been a party since December 2022. An MEA apostille is the usual route for Indian documents going there.

- **Oman** has been a party since 2012, and **Bahrain** since 2013. An MEA apostille is the usual route for both.

- **UAE, Qatar and Kuwait** are not listed as parties. For these, the route is MEA attestation, then attestation by that country's embassy or consulate in India, and then attestation by its Ministry of Foreign Affairs after you arrive. The UAE's MOFA step, in particular, is required before most employers and authorities will accept the certificate.

Employers and individual authorities sometimes ask for steps beyond these. Check with the employer or the authority that will receive the document.

## The UAE has an extra layer

For the UAE, attestation is often only half the job. If the employer, a licensing body or a visa grade needs your degree to be formally recognized, that goes through the UAE's higher education ministry, which runs its own recognition service. Reporting on the service indicates that the authenticity of the certificate is checked through verification agencies approved by the ministry, which contact your university directly. That check is separate from anything done in India, and it depends on your university responding promptly.

Two consequences for you. Make sure your university's examination or verification office has a working contact, and keep copies of every marksheet and certificate in the exact form issued, because the agency will compare them with the university's records. And ask the employer early whether ministry recognition is needed for your role, so you can start both processes together rather than one after the other.

## Documents to have ready

Requirements vary by authority, but this list covers most cases.

- The original degree certificate, plus clear photocopies.

- Semester-wise or year-wise marksheets. Some authorities, including NORKA, do not attest consolidated marksheets or transcripts, so keep the individual ones.

- Your passport, with copies of the first and last pages.

- Recent passport-size photographs.

- The provisional certificate, if your final degree has not been issued. NORKA's page notes it accepts a provisional certificate only within six months of issue.

- Your SSLC or 10th certificate, which some authorities ask for alongside the degree.

Make sure your name and date of birth match exactly across the degree, the marksheets and the passport. A spelling difference that seemed harmless in India can stop the process at an embassy.

## Where people lose time

- **Starting with the wrong state.** If your university is outside Kerala, the state verification happens in that state, not at a NORKA centre.

- **Waiting for the degree certificate.** Some universities take months to issue the final degree after results. Request it as soon as you are eligible.

- **Going through touts.** The MEA itself warns against unauthorised intermediaries. Use the designated agencies, NORKA Roots for Kerala certificates, or a licensed service you have checked.

- **Attesting the wrong document.** If the employer wants the degree and the marksheets, attesting only the degree means starting again.

- **Leaving it to the last fortnight.** Each stage takes its own time, and embassy queues vary. Start as soon as you have a job offer in sight.

## Practical next steps

Make a single sheet with your university's name and state, the destination country, and what the employer has asked for. From that you can work out whether you need an apostille or embassy attestation, and which state authority does the first verification. Then check the current MEA page for agencies and fees, and NORKA Roots if your certificate is from a Kerala institution.

If you are still choosing a degree and expect to work in the Gulf, confirm recognition before you enrol and ask the university what it issues for verification.
