Distance and online programmes in India admit in two cycles, one around July and August and another around January and February. People tend to treat whichever is next as the only option, which occasionally costs them a better start.

## What the two cycles are

The July cycle aligns with the traditional academic year and is the larger of the two. More universities run it, more programmes are offered in it, and the cohort is bigger.

The January cycle is the second admission window. Fewer programmes may be available, and for some universities certain specialisations only open in July.

The degree awarded is identical. The cycle affects when you start and finish, not what you receive.

## Which one to take

Take the next one if your documents are ready, the programme you want is open, and nothing in your working year makes the first semester unusually difficult.

Wait for the following one if a required document will take weeks to obtain, if the specialisation you want is not open in this cycle, or if your first semester examinations would land in your busiest period at work. A semester's delay is cheaper than a failed first semester, which is where most people who drop out actually drop out.

## Plan the examination months, not the start month

This is the part people skip. Ask when the first and second semester examinations fall for the cycle you are considering, then look at your own year: audit season, financial year end, peak production periods, planned family commitments.

If the examination months land squarely on your worst weeks, the other cycle moves them by six months for free.

## Deadlines and extensions

Universities frequently extend a closing date, and applicants frequently plan around the extension. That is a bad habit. The extension is discretionary, the verification queue in the final week is the longest, and a document problem discovered then leaves no time to fix it.

Treat the first announced date as real and aim to be complete a fortnight before it.

## What does not change between cycles

Eligibility, fees, the entitlement checks you should run, and the documents required. None of that varies by window, so the preparation is the same whichever you take.
