If you want to work as an engineer in Saudi Arabia, your B.Tech has to pass one more check after the usual attestation: professional accreditation from the Saudi Council of Engineers (SCE). The SCE looks at your academic qualification and your experience, and without its accreditation you cannot work under an engineer's title in the Kingdom. India's AICTE has itself circulated this requirement to Indian institutions.

For most readers in Kerala, the practical question is whether their particular degree will get through. A regular B.Tech from a recognised university, properly attested, is the kind of degree the system is built for. A B.Tech earned by distance or online mode is the kind that is widely reported to be refused, and it is not valid in India in the first place. This guide explains what to prepare and what to avoid.

## What is the Saudi Council of Engineers?

The Saudi Council of Engineers is the professional body that regulates the engineering profession in Saudi Arabia. Engineers, Saudi and foreign, must obtain professional accreditation from it before they can practise as engineers there.

Think of it as the Saudi equivalent of a licensing step. Your employer can offer you a job, and your visa can name you as an engineer, but the SCE decides whether your qualification and experience meet its standard for that title. The current rules, categories and fees are published on the SCE's own website, and they are revised from time to time, so treat any summary, including this one, as a starting point rather than the final word.

## Do Indian engineers need SCE accreditation to work in Saudi Arabia?

Yes. Indian engineers who are employed in engineering roles in Saudi Arabia need SCE professional accreditation. AICTE issued a circular to Indian institutions confirming that engineers must obtain this accreditation to work as engineers in the Kingdom.

This matters even if you are already in Saudi Arabia on another job title. If your role changes to an engineering post, or your employer wants your visa profession to read "engineer", the accreditation question comes up at that point. Many people from Kerala first learn about it only when their employer's HR asks for an SCE number, which is late in the process.

## What does the SCE check in your degree?

The SCE assesses your academic qualification and your professional experience. In practice that means it looks at the degree itself, the university that awarded it, and whether the programme is the kind of engineering qualification it accepts.

The points that usually come up are:

- **The university.** It should be a recognised university in India. For an Indian B.Tech, that means a UGC-recognised university, with the engineering programme run at an AICTE-approved institution where AICTE approval applies.
- **The programme.** A bachelor's degree in engineering in a defined branch, with the full marksheets or transcripts for every semester.
- **The mode of study.** Whether you attended a regular programme, as opposed to distance or online study.
- **Genuineness.** Whether the certificate is real and was actually issued by that university. Verification of Indian certificates is now routine across the Gulf.
- **Experience.** Your work history, supported by experience letters from employers.

Exactly how each point is weighed, and what grade of accreditation you are placed in, is set by the SCE's current rules. Check those on the SCE website before you apply.

## Will the SCE accept a distance or online B.Tech?

Do not plan on it. It is widely reported by applicants and recruiters that the SCE rejects engineering degrees earned by distance mode. We have not found an official SCE statement that spells this out, so check directly with the SCE if you are in this situation.

There is a more basic problem, though. A distance or online B.Tech is not valid in India. The UGC's 2020 regulations for open, distance and online programmes exclude engineering, and in November 2017 the Supreme Court held that engineering degrees awarded by distance mode by deemed universities without AICTE approval were not valid. A degree that is not accepted at home will not become acceptable abroad. Our guide to [distance B.Tech in Kerala](/blog/distance-btech-kerala) sets out the full position, and [whether an online degree is valid for Gulf jobs](/blog/online-degree-valid-gulf-jobs) covers the subjects where online degrees are a different matter.

## Is a B.Tech for working professionals accepted by the SCE?

A B.Tech for working professionals from an AICTE-approved institution, awarded by a recognised university such as KTU, is a regular-mode engineering degree in India. You attend classes in person in the evenings or at weekends, do the same laboratories and examinations, and are awarded a B.Tech. That puts it on a different footing from a distance degree.

What we cannot tell you is how the SCE will read every certificate in every case, because that is its decision. Two practical steps help:

1. Before you join, ask the college how the degree certificate and transcripts will describe the programme, and keep that answer in writing.
2. Keep your own evidence of regular attendance: the admission letter, fee receipts, and the college's confirmation that the programme was conducted in person.

If you already hold such a degree and are applying, ask the SCE how it treats it before you resign from anything at home.

## What about AMIE or a diploma?

AMIE from the Institution of Engineers (India) is treated differently by different employers and authorities, and its recognition in India has a contested history since 2013. Do not assume the SCE treats it as equal to a B.Tech; ask the SCE directly. Our comparison of [AMIE vs B.Tech](/blog/amie-vs-btech) explains the Indian position.

A polytechnic diploma is a technician-level qualification, not an engineering degree. If you hold a diploma and want engineer status in the Gulf, the honest route is to complete a recognised B.Tech first, usually through lateral entry. See [how lateral entry into a degree works](/blog/lateral-entry-degree).

## How do I prepare my documents from Kerala?

Start with attestation, because nothing else moves without it. An Indian degree used in Saudi Arabia normally goes through the attestation chain at home (state-level verification, then the Ministry of External Affairs) and then the Saudi embassy or consulate. Our step-by-step guide to [degree attestation for the Gulf](/blog/degree-attestation-gulf) covers the order and the common delays.

Then build a complete file:

- the degree certificate and the provisional certificate if you have one;
- marksheets or a consolidated transcript for every semester, including supplementary attempts;
- your passport, and your residence permit if you are already in Saudi Arabia;
- experience letters on company letterhead, with dates, job title and duties;
- any certificate of the institution's approval you can obtain, in case it is asked for.

Keep scanned copies of everything in one folder. Missing pages and unexplained gaps are a common reason for delay in any Gulf verification.

## What mistakes cost Kerala engineers the most?

The costly mistakes are usually made in India, years before the Saudi application. The ones we see repeatedly are:

- **Buying a "distance B.Tech" or a "one-sitting" degree** from an agent, expecting it to pass Gulf checks. It is not valid in India, and certificates are verified with the issuing university.
- **Leaving attestation until after the job offer.** Attestation can take weeks; start as soon as you are serious about the Gulf.
- **Assuming one country's acceptance covers another.** Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar and Oman each have their own rules. Our article on engineering jobs in the UAE, Qatar and Oman deals with those separately.
- **Relying on a recruiter's word about SCE rules.** Check the SCE website or ask the SCE yourself.
