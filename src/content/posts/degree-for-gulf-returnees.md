If you have come back to Kerala after years in the Gulf, a recognized distance or online degree is one of the most practical ways to turn that experience into a job, a promotion or a business that a bank will take seriously. You can study from home, keep working while you do it, and in many cases count earlier college study towards it. The key decisions are choosing a programme that is properly recognized, picking a subject that fits what you plan to do next, and setting up a routine that survives the first busy months of resettling.

## Why returnees come to us

The reasons are usually practical rather than academic. A returnee who managed a warehouse in Dubai or a sales team in Riyadh applies for a supervisory post in Kerala and finds the shortlist begins with "graduate". Someone who ran accounts for a Gulf trading firm wants to set up on their own and needs a qualification that clients and lenders recognize. Others simply want to finish what they started at nineteen, before family and the visa took over.

Experience does matter. But Indian employers, especially larger companies, banks and anything connected to government, tend to filter on qualifications first and read experience second. A degree gets you past that first filter.

There is also a quieter reason. After years of being valued for what you could do at work, coming home to a job market that asks first about paper qualifications can feel like starting again. Finishing a degree changes that conversation. It lets your experience be read on its own merits, rather than being set aside at the first screening.

## Start with what you already have

Many returnees left a degree half-way to go abroad. If you passed some semesters or years at a recognized university, that study may still count.

- **Find your old marksheets.** Even partial records help. If they are lost, the university's examination branch can usually issue duplicates.

- **Ask about credit transfer.** Under current UGC rules, credits earned at one recognized institution can in many cases be carried to another, subject to the receiving university's rules. Our guide to [credit transfer for incomplete degrees](/credit-transfer) explains how the assessment works.

- **Check how long ago it was.** Older study is not automatically excluded, but universities set their own limits. An assessment will tell you what counts.

If you never started college, you begin from the first year with your 12th certificate from a recognized board. That is a perfectly normal starting point. Our article on [returning to study after a long gap](/blog/degree-after-long-gap) covers what to expect.

## Choosing a subject that fits your next step

The right degree depends on what you plan to do in Kerala, not on what sounds impressive.

- **Going back into a company role** such as sales, operations, logistics or administration: a BBA or B.Com is the usual choice, and an MBA later if you already hold a degree.

- **Accounts, finance or your own trading business:** a B.Com gives you the commerce grounding that clients and banks expect.

- **IT, networking or technical support work:** a BCA, if your Gulf job was on the technical side.

- **Social sector, NGO or community work:** a BA, or an MSW at postgraduate level.

- **Government recruitment:** a degree in any discipline from a recognized university is the usual requirement, but read each notification, because some posts ask for a regular course of study.

If you might go back to the Gulf later, keep that in mind now. Gulf employers and ministries can scrutinise distance and online degrees closely at the equivalency stage. Read our guide to [whether an online degree is valid for Gulf jobs](/blog/online-degree-valid-gulf-jobs) before deciding.

## Recognition: check before you pay

Returnees are a target for poor providers, partly because they are assumed to have savings and to be in a hurry. Before you pay anything, confirm three things for yourself.

- The university is recognized by the UGC.

- The specific programme has UGC Distance Education Bureau entitlement for the year you are admitted.

- The fee and all its parts are written down: tuition, registration, examination, certificate and any attestation charges.

A one-year degree, a backdated certificate, or an offer to skip the examinations are not shortcuts. They are signs that the qualification will not survive a background check. Our list of [red flags before enrolling](/blog/red-flags-before-enrolling-in-an-online-degree) is worth five minutes.

## Fitting study around resettling

The first year back is often the busiest: finding work, sorting out a house, children changing schools, sometimes starting a business. Study has to fit into that, not compete with it.

- **Plan six to ten hours a week**, in fixed slots, rather than "whenever there is time".

- **Tell your family what the degree is for.** The evenings you need are easier to protect when everyone at home knows why.

- **Use the time you already have.** Reading fits into a bus journey or a wait at a government office. Writing needs a quieter block.

- **Know the examination pattern.** Some programmes use written examinations at centres, others use assignments or proctored online exams. Pick one you can actually sit.

- **Expect the first examination to feel strange.** Writing answers by hand against a clock is a skill that fades after years away. Practise with past questions rather than re-reading, and it comes back quickly. Our guide to [studying while working full time](/blog/studying-while-working-full-time) has more on this.

## Support for returnees beyond the degree

The Kerala government's NORKA Roots agency works with returning non-resident Keralites, including rehabilitation and self-employment support. Its scheme for return emigrants, known as NDPREM, is aimed at people starting or expanding a business, through partner banks. Eligibility rules and amounts change, so check the current terms directly with NORKA Roots rather than relying on a news report or an agent.

If anyone tells you that NORKA support depends on enrolling in a particular course, check with NORKA Roots directly before believing it. Government support and private admissions are separate things.

A degree and a business plan are not alternatives. Many returnees do both, and a commerce or management degree makes the business side easier to run.

## What it costs

Fees depend on the university and programme. A counsellor gives you the full fee breakdown in writing before you commit, covering tuition, registration, examination and certificate charges. Monthly instalment plans with no interest and no processing fee are available, which helps when income is still settling after the move.

## Next steps

- Gather your 10th and 12th certificates, and any college marksheets you still have.

- Write down what you want the degree to do: a specific job, a promotion, a business, or a possible return abroad.

- Book an assessment. We check whether earlier study can be counted, suggest programmes that fit your plan, and show you the university, the programme's DEB entitlement and the full fee in writing before any payment.

- Once admitted, your enrolment number is usually issued within about 48 hours of complete documents, so you can start without a long wait.
