The biggest red flags before enrolling in an online degree are a promise that you will pass, a degree dated to a year you did not study, a duration far shorter than normal, "approval in process" for your session, and pressure to pay today. Any one of these is reason to stop and check before paying anything. Most of them show up in the first phone call, if you know how to listen.

Most people who lose money in this field are not careless. They are busy, they are dealing with someone confident and helpful, and the problems are not obvious. This article is about what is said to you in a sales conversation. For the records to look up afterwards, use our ten-point guide on [how to spot a fake degree provider](/blog/spot-fake-degree-provider).

## What do fake degree agents usually say?

They usually offer certainty, speed or a discount rather than documents. Here are the lines we hear about most, what they tend to mean, and what to ask in reply.

| What you hear | What it usually means | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| "You will definitely pass, we take care of everything" | Someone is promising a result only an examination can give | "How exactly are the papers conducted, and who sits them?" |
| "We can give you the degree dated 2022" | A fabricated record | Nothing. End the conversation |
| "A full degree in one year" | Either no real programme, or a credit decision nobody has made yet | "Show me the university's written decision on my file" |
| "UGC approved, sir, don't worry" | An answer to a different question | "Which programme, which mode, which session?" |
| "Approval is in process, you can join now" | No entitlement for your session at this moment | "I will join when it is listed" |
| "This offer closes tonight" | Pressure so you will not check | "Send the offer in writing and I will reply after I verify" |
| "The portal is not updated, trust us" | The record may not support them | "Then ask the university to write to me" |

## Why is a degree dated to a past year so dangerous?

It is dangerous because it is not a shortcut but a false document, and using one can cost you the job you wanted it for. Verification can happen years later, and what was hidden at first tends to come out when it is checked against the university's own records of enrolment and examinations.

Be careful with softer versions of the same offer: "we will adjust the year", or "your old papers can be shown as this batch". Genuine credit transfer exists, and it is decided by the university from your real marksheets, with your real dates. Our [credit transfer page](/credit-transfer) explains how that works.

## Is it a red flag if a counsellor asks me to pay today?

Yes. Urgency by itself is one of the clearest warning signs, because a real admission has published dates and a fee that will still be there tomorrow. There are genuine last dates, and the UGC sets them for each session. But a real deadline is one you can look up. A discount that exists only until tonight is one you cannot.

The other money signals to listen for:

- **A total that stays vague.** "It is only so much per semester" with nothing on examination, registration or certificate charges. Ask for every charge in writing. Our piece on [what a degree costs and how instalments work](/blog/what-a-degree-costs-and-how-emi-works) shows what a complete total includes.

- **Payment first, details later.** Any provider should be able to show you the university, programme, entitlement and fee before taking money. That is the standard we hold ourselves to, and you should ask it of anyone.

- **Payment to a person.** A personal UPI ID or account, with a promise that a receipt will follow.

- **A loan signed on the spot.** Ask whether there is interest or a processing fee, and take the paper home to read. Our own instalment plans are monthly with no interest and no processing fee, so ask any provider to state its terms in the same plain way.

## What promises about jobs should make me suspicious?

Be suspicious of any promise that a degree will be accepted for every job, and of any placement claim you cannot see evidence for. Validity depends on the university's recognition, the programme's DEB entitlement for your admission year, and the wording of the job notification, and no salesperson can speak for a notification not yet written.

Kerala applicants should listen especially for "it is valid for PSC, no problem". That is too simple. After the Kerala High Court's January 2026 ruling, a distance degree does not satisfy a post whose rules require a regular course of study. An honest counsellor will ask which post you have in mind and suggest you read the notification. Our article on [Kerala PSC and distance degrees](/blog/distance-degree-valid-kerala-psc) explains this.

Similar caution applies to these:

- A NAAC grade with no cycle or date. Grades expire.

- A health subject such as psychology offered online. The UGC has moved to withdraw such subjects from these modes.

- "This foreign degree works the same as an Indian one." Equivalence is not automatic, and franchised qualifications are excluded.

- A nursing, law or engineering degree offered through distance mode. These are not permitted in that mode at all.

## How can I tell a genuine counsellor from an agent selling a fake degree?

A genuine counsellor answers specific questions with documents, and is willing to tell you when something will not work. Admission offices are normal in this sector, including ours, so the existence of an office or agent is not the problem. What matters is how they behave.

Watch for these arrangements.

- **The centre presents itself as the university.** The UGC has said no university has approval to admit learners through franchising arrangements, so ask directly: "Who is admitting me, and in whose name is the receipt?"

- **Examinations "at our office".** For distance programmes, the DEB says examinations have to be held within the institution's jurisdiction. If someone promises a convenient local arrangement for an out-of-state university, ask the university to confirm it.

- **Your details stay with them.** Your DEB-ID and enrolment number should be in your hands. If they are not, ask why.

- **Your documents stay with them.** Scanned copies are generally enough at enquiry stage. Be wary of anyone holding on to your original certificates until you pay.

- **They become irritated when you check.** Someone who has the documents has no reason to mind you reading them.

An illustrative composite, not a real person: Jaseena, in Kozhikode, gets a call about a degree. The caller is friendly and knows her situation. He offers a discount until evening, says the university is "UGC approved", and asks for a part payment on UPI. She asks which session the DEB entitlement covers, and he says it is in process and that the portal is always late. She says she will wait until it is written down, and the calls become more urgent. Nothing in that call proves the university is fake. It does tell her she has been given no fact she could check, and that is reason enough not to pay.

## What should I do if I have already paid?

Ask in writing for the enrolment number and the university receipt, and keep every message. If neither arrives, stop paying further instalments and raise it with the university directly, using contact details from its official website rather than those you were given. If the university has no record of you, you will have that in writing for any complaint you decide to make.
