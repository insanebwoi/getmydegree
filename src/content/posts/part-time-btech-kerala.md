The old part-time and evening B.Tech courses, as they used to run, no longer take new students. AICTE discontinued evening and part-time B.Tech around 2019. What exists in Kerala in 2026 is KTU's B.Tech for working professionals, a regular programme at AICTE-approved colleges with classes in the evenings or at weekends and holidays.

So if you searched "part-time B.Tech Kerala", the programme you are looking for has a new name and new conditions. You need a diploma or another accepted qualification, at least a year in a full-time job, a workplace within reach of the college, and a qualifying score in the Lateral Entry Test. Below we explain what changed and what to watch out for.

## Is there a part-time B.Tech in Kerala in 2026?

Not under that name. The scheme that replaced it is the B.Tech for working professionals, which KTU has run from 2024-25 at AICTE-approved institutions. It is effectively the evening and weekend B.Tech for employed people, with entry at the third semester and a duration of not less than three years.

If someone offers you a different kind of evening or part-time B.Tech, for example one that starts from the first year, ask for the university notice and the AICTE approval for it in writing before you go further.

## What happened to the old evening B.Tech courses?

AICTE discontinued evening and part-time B.Tech around 2019. Some Kerala colleges ran evening B.Tech courses before that; the College of Engineering Trivandrum is one example.

Then, from 2023-24, AICTE allowed "B.E./B.Tech for working professionals" at approved institutions as a flexible-timing course. KTU brought it to Kerala colleges from 2024-25. If you studied in or heard about an older evening batch, the rules that applied then are not the rules for new admissions now.

## How is the working-professional B.Tech organised?

It is a regular B.Tech with different class hours. Classes are held in the evening hours or at other flexible, convenient timings, which can include weekends and holidays, and each college sets its own timetable.

You attend in person. Labs, workshops, internal assessments and the KTU semester examinations are all part of it, the same as for full-time students. You join at the third semester, like a lateral-entry student, and study for not less than three years.

## Who can join an evening or weekend B.Tech in Kerala?

Employed people with a diploma in engineering, a B.Sc with Mathematics at Plus Two, a B.Voc or a three-year D.Voc. Per the KTU notice and college pages for 2026:

- diploma in engineering (three-year, or two-year lateral) with 45% marks (40% for reserved categories);
- B.Sc with 45% marks and Mathematics at Plus Two; B.Sc candidates may need to clear Engineering Graphics and Engineering Mechanics;
- B.Voc or three-year D.Voc;
- at least one year of continuous full-time employment in a registered organisation, with an Employment Certificate from the employer;
- no upper age limit.

You also need to qualify the B.Tech Lateral Entry Test (LET), conducted by LBS Centre for the Directorate of Technical Education. Colleges rank candidates by LET score; if LET-qualified candidates run out, they may admit others on qualifying marks.

## How far from the college can I work?

Your workplace has to be within a set radial distance of the institution. AICTE's 2024-25 norms reportedly set this at 50 km, reduced from 70 km. At least one Kerala college's page states 75 km, so check the current notice of the college you are applying to.

This is the condition that most often surprises people. Someone working in Kochi and hoping to attend a college in Kannur will usually not qualify for that college. ## Which Kerala colleges offer it?

Only AICTE-approved colleges with separate approval for working-professional seats, and the list changes year to year. Two examples from the 2026 admission:

|---|---|---|---|
| College of Engineering Thalassery | Mechanical | 30 | ₹40,000 a year |
| SJCET Palai | Electrical and Electronics | 30 (runs only if at least 10 join) | ₹50,250 per semester, plus other fees |

These are examples, not a full list. Fees and seats change every year, so read each college's admission page for the current figures. Our pillar guide to the [B.Tech for working professionals in Kerala](/blog/btech-working-professionals-kerala) has a fuller breakdown of the listed charges.

## Is a weekend B.Tech the same as an online B.Tech?

No. A weekend B.Tech for working professionals means classes on Saturdays, Sundays or holidays at an approved campus, which is valid. An online or distance B.Tech means studying remotely, which is not valid in India for engineering.

The UGC's 2020 regulations on open, distance and online programmes list engineering among subjects that cannot be offered in those modes, and the 2021 and 2022 amendments kept it excluded. In 2017 the Supreme Court held that engineering degrees awarded by distance mode by deemed universities without AICTE approval were not valid. Some advertisements blur "weekend" and "online" on purpose. Our guide to [distance B.Tech in Kerala](/blog/distance-btech-kerala) explains the difference in more depth.

## What if I do not have a diploma?

Then the working-professional B.Tech is usually not open to you, because its admission qualification is the same as for lateral entry. Without a diploma, B.Sc, B.Voc or D.Voc, the B.Tech route is the regular first-year programme through KEAM, which is full-time.

KEAM, conducted by the Commissioner for Entrance Examinations, is a computer-based test in Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry; KEAM 2026 engineering was held from 17 to 22 April 2026. If a full-time course is not possible and your aim is an IT career, a recognised BCA or B.Sc followed by an MCA may be the better fit. See [B.Sc vs BCA for tech jobs](/blog/bsc-vs-bca-for-tech-jobs).

## What should I check before joining?

Verify the programme yourself before you pay:

1. The institution and its working-professional intake appear in AICTE's approval for the year.
2. The programme appears in the KTU notice for the year.
3. The admission documents say "B.Tech for working professionals", regular mode, not distance or online.
4. The college's written conditions on workplace distance, class timings and minimum batch size.
