If you are a diploma holder in a job in Kerala and you want a B.Tech without resigning, the route is KTU's B.Tech for working professionals. It runs at AICTE-approved engineering colleges, admits you into the second year (third semester), and holds classes in the evenings or at weekends and holidays. You still attend in person, do the labs and write the KTU examinations.

It is not a distance or online B.Tech. Those are not valid in India, and nothing in this guide should be read as an exception. What follows is the eligibility, the admission steps, a few example colleges from the 2026 admission, and the checks we ask every student to make before paying a fee.

## What is a B.Tech for working professionals?

It is a regular B.Tech programme, approved by AICTE and affiliated to APJ Abdul Kalam Technological University (KTU), with classes timed for people who are employed. KTU began offering it from 2024-25 at AICTE-approved institutions. Students join at the third semester, like lateral entrants, and study for not less than three years.

A little history explains why the programme exists. AICTE discontinued the old evening and part-time B.Tech courses around 2019. From 2023-24 it allowed "B.E./B.Tech for working professionals" at approved institutions as a course with flexible timings, and KTU adopted it for Kerala colleges the following year. Some colleges, such as the College of Engineering Trivandrum, had run evening B.Tech courses earlier under the older scheme. Our guide to [part-time and evening B.Tech in Kerala](/blog/part-time-btech-kerala) covers what changed.

## Who is eligible for KTU's working-professional B.Tech?

You need an accepted qualification, a year of full-time work, and an employer who will certify it. Based on the KTU notice and college admission pages for 2026, the requirements are:

- **Qualification**, any one of:
  - a diploma in engineering (three-year, or two-year lateral entry) with at least 45% marks (40% for reserved categories);
  - a B.Sc with at least 45% marks and Mathematics as a subject at Plus Two. B.Sc candidates may be required to clear Engineering Graphics and Engineering Mechanics;
  - a B.Voc, or a three-year D.Voc.
- **Employment**: at least one year of continuous full-time employment in a registered organisation, supported by an Employment Certificate from your employer.
- **Age**: there is no upper age limit.

A diploma in a branch close to the B.Tech branch you want is the usual case. If your diploma and the offered branch do not match, ask the college in writing whether you are eligible before you apply.

## Do I need to write the LET?

Yes, in the normal course. Admission requires you to qualify the B.Tech Lateral Entry Test (LET, sometimes called BLET), which LBS Centre for Science and Technology conducts for the Directorate of Technical Education, Kerala. Colleges rank working-professional applicants by their LET score.

There is one exception worth knowing. If a college runs out of LET-qualified candidates, it may admit others on the basis of their qualifying marks. That is a fallback, not a plan. Write the LET.

For the 2026 admission, the LET was held on 13 June 2026, and the minimum qualifying mark was reported as 15% for the general category, lower for reserved categories. Applications go through lbsapplications.kerala.gov.in. Our guide to [Kerala B.Tech lateral entry and the LET](/blog/kerala-btech-lateral-entry-let) explains the test and the application in detail.

## Which colleges offer it in Kerala?

Only AICTE-approved institutions that have separate approval for working-professional seats, and the list changes every year. We are not able to give a complete list here, and you should be wary of anyone who claims a fixed one.

Two examples from college websites for the 2026 admission:

- **College of Engineering Thalassery** listed a B.Tech in Mechanical Engineering for working professionals, with 30 seats.
- **SJCET Palai** listed a B.Tech in Electrical and Electronics Engineering for working professionals, with 30 seats, and said the batch would run only if at least 10 students joined.

If a programme needs a minimum number of students to start, ask the college what happens to your fee if the batch does not run.

## How far can my workplace be from the college?

Your workplace has to be within a set radial distance of the institution, so that you can realistically attend. The figure reported in AICTE's 2024-25 norms is 50 km, reduced from 70 km earlier. At least one Kerala college's page states 75 km.

Because these figures do not agree, treat the current year's AICTE handbook and the college's own notice as the final word. If you work in the Gulf or another state, this rule alone usually rules the programme out until you return. Our article on [AICTE rules for B.Tech for working professionals](/blog/aicte-working-professional-btech-rules) sets out the national norms.

## When are classes held, and how long does it take?

Classes run in the evening hours or at other flexible, convenient timings, which can include weekends and holidays. The exact timetable is set by each college. Lateral-style entry means the programme takes not less than three years.

Expect regular attendance, laboratory sessions, internal assessments and the KTU semester examinations, the same as any B.Tech student. Most working students find that the evenings and weekends fill up quickly. Before you commit, talk honestly with your family and your supervisor about shift changes and overtime. Our piece on [studying while working full time](/blog/studying-while-working-full-time) has practical advice.

## Is it valid for Kerala PSC, promotions and jobs abroad?

It is a regular-mode B.Tech from an AICTE-approved college affiliated to KTU, which is the kind of qualification engineering posts ask for. Kerala PSC Assistant Engineer notifications, for example, ask for a B.Sc or B.Tech in the relevant branch from a recognised university or equivalent.

Check two things for your own goal. First, read the specific notification or promotion rule, because each one sets its own qualification. Second, if you plan to work abroad, remember that some countries register engineers with their own body. Saudi Arabia, for instance, requires engineers to be accredited by the Saudi Council of Engineers, and distance-mode degrees are widely reported to be rejected there. A regular-mode B.Tech avoids that problem. The [degree attestation guide](/blog/degree-attestation-gulf) explains the paperwork.

## How do I apply for the 2027 admission?

Watch for the notifications, because the 2027 cycle has not been announced yet. Based on the 2026 cycle, the order is roughly:

1. **Check your eligibility now.** Collect your diploma, B.Sc or D.Voc marksheets for every semester.
2. **Get your Employment Certificate.** Ask your employer early; it must show continuous full-time employment in a registered organisation.
3. **Watch the LBS Centre website** for the LET notification and apply on lbsapplications.kerala.gov.in within the window.
4. **Write the LET.** In 2026 it was held in June; the 2027 date will be in the new notification.
5. **Shortlist colleges** with working-professional seats within the permitted distance of your workplace, and read each college's notice for seats, fees and its own application steps.
6. **Verify AICTE approval** for the working-professional intake before you pay.

## What should I check before paying any fee?

Check the approval yourself, in writing. An agent's word or a brochure is not enough.

- The institution and the working-professional intake appear in AICTE's approval for the year.
- The college is affiliated to KTU, and the programme is listed in the KTU notice.
- The admission letter and receipt describe the programme as a B.Tech for working professionals in regular mode, not distance or online.
- The fee schedule shows the full three-year cost, including university and exam fees.

If someone offers a B.Tech with no classes, no LET and a finish in one or two years, it is not this programme. Our checklist on [how to spot a fake degree provider](/blog/spot-fake-degree-provider) lists the warning signs.
