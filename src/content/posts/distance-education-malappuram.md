If you live in Malappuram district and want a degree while working, raising a family or planning a move abroad, a distance or online programme from a UGC-recognized university, in a programme with DEB entitlement for your admission year, is the practical route. Our Tirur centre, near the bus stand at Forza Mall, handles admission from first conversation to enrolment, and many of our Malappuram students begin the process while still working in the Gulf.

This guide is built around the questions we hear most often at the Tirur centre.

## Why Tirur

Tirur is where a great deal of the district already comes for business. The railway station is on the main line through Malabar, and buses from Ponnani, Kuttippuram, Valanchery, Kottakkal, Tanur, Parappanangadi and Tirurangadi meet at the bus stand. Our centre is close to that stand, inside Forza Mall, so a visit fits into a trip you might be making anyway.

The district is not short of education. The University of Calicut's campus is at Thenhipalam, and the Malayalam University is in Tirur itself. What many adults here lack is not access to a college but the time to attend one. That is the gap distance and online study fills.

## The three groups we see most

**People back from the Gulf.** Malappuram has long sent its young men, and increasingly its young women, to the Gulf. Some come back after ten or fifteen years with savings, experience and no degree, and find that jobs at home, or a better position if they return abroad, now ask for one. Our article on [degrees for Gulf returnees](/blog/degree-for-gulf-returnees) goes deeper into this.

**People still working in the Gulf.** Many of our Tirur students enrol while they are in Jeddah, Dubai or Doha, with a family member bringing documents to the centre. Online programmes suit this best, though examination arrangements differ by university and programme, and some require you to be present at a centre in India. We confirm this before you enrol, not after.

**Women studying from home.** A large share of the people who come to us from the district are women who married soon after the 12th or after a year of college. With children in school, they now have a few quiet hours a day and want to finish what they started. Many come with a husband or parent, and that is welcome. There is more on this in [degree options for homemakers in Kerala](/blog/degree-for-housewives-kerala).

## Studying from abroad: what to check first

If you are in the Gulf now, or plan to go back, ask four questions before choosing a programme.

- Is the examination online, or must you sit it in person in India? If in person, can you plan leave around it?

- Will you need the degree attested for a Gulf employer or visa category? If so, the route is university attestation, then MEA attestation or apostille, then the embassy, and in the UAE the foreign ministry as well. Plan for it at the end.

- Will the country's education ministry need to assess the degree for equivalency? Some Gulf authorities scrutinise distance and online degrees more closely than regular ones. This is a real risk, and we would rather you heard it from us now. Our guide on [whether an online degree is valid for Gulf jobs](/blog/online-degree-valid-gulf-jobs) explains what to check.

- Is the name on your passport identical to the name on your 10th and 12th certificates? A mismatch causes more trouble at attestation than anything else.

## Which programme makes sense

For returnees setting up a shop, a trading business or a small agency, a B.Com or BBA gives the accounting and management grounding that most of them were doing by instinct already.

For people in IT support, mobile and computer service, or office systems, a BCA or B.Sc is the closer fit.

For women planning to teach in private schools or tuition centres, or to attempt general PSC examinations, a BA or B.Com is often the starting point, followed by a postgraduate degree later.

For graduates with years of supervisory experience in the Gulf, an MBA is the usual step up, either an Indian distance MBA or the UK online MBA from Plymouth Marjon University. The full list is on our [courses page](/courses).

## If you left a degree halfway, or want a PSC post

Plenty of people in the district left a degree at a Calicut University college when a visa or a marriage came along. Those passed semesters may still count. Credit transfer lets a receiving university accept your passed papers so you complete only what remains. Bring every marksheet you have, and read our [guide to degree credit transfer in Kerala](/blog/credit-transfer-degree-kerala) beforehand if you like.

A recognized distance degree is treated as equivalent to a regular degree for most purposes under the UGC's 2020 regulations. The exception that matters in Kerala is any post whose rules require a qualification through a regular course of study. The Kerala High Court confirmed in January 2026 that a distance degree does not meet that requirement. If a PSC post is your goal, bring its notification and we will read it together.

## Fitting study into a Malappuram household

Studying as an adult here usually means studying around other people. For a returnee, it may be a new business that needs attention at odd hours. For a homemaker, it is school runs, meals and older relatives. For someone still in Riyadh or Sharjah, it is a six-day week and a time difference.

What works, in our experience, is fixing a small number of study hours each week and protecting them, rather than hoping for free time to appear. Tell the family which evenings are for study. Keep the study material in one place. Write examination dates on the same calendar the household uses. If a month goes badly, tell us early, because most problems with assignments and examinations are solvable when raised in time.

## Fees, and visiting the Tirur centre

Fees vary by university, programme and admission year, so we do not quote numbers here. The total is made up of tuition, registration, examination fees, certificate charges and, for those working abroad, attestation. You see the complete breakdown in writing, with the university and programme named and its DEB entitlement shown, before you pay. Monthly instalments are available with no interest and no processing fee.

The centre is near Tirur Bus Stand, at Forza Mall, Tirur 676101. If you are coming by train, Tirur railway station is a short auto ride away. The phone number is 73566 44343, and office hours are Monday to Saturday, 9am to 7pm.

Bring your 10th and 12th certificates, any degree marksheets, Aadhaar, your passport if you have one, and a few passport-size photographs. If you are abroad, a family member can bring copies, and we can begin on the phone. Once your documents are complete, the enrolment number is usually issued within about 48 hours.

## Practical next steps

- Decide what the degree is for: a job here, a job abroad, a promotion, or your own satisfaction. The answer shapes the programme.

- Gather your certificates and check that your name is spelled the same way on all of them.

- If you work abroad, find out whether your employer or visa category will need attestation or equivalency.

- Call or visit the Tirur centre for a first conversation. There is no charge for it.
