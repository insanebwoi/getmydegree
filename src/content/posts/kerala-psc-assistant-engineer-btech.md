Kerala PSC Assistant Engineer posts ask for a degree in engineering (B.Sc Engineering or B.Tech) in the branch the post needs, from a recognised university, or a qualification recognised as equivalent. Some AE notifications, such as those for Civil, also accept the Associate Membership of the Institution of Engineers (India), known as AMIE, as an alternative. The exact wording changes from post to post, so the notification you are applying under is the final word.

For a working diploma holder, the important point is what does not count. A B.Tech earned by distance, online or correspondence mode is not a recognised engineering degree in India, and it will not carry you through PSC's certificate verification. A B.Tech for working professionals from an AICTE-approved college, or a B.Tech through lateral entry, is a regular degree and does.

## What is the qualification for Kerala PSC Assistant Engineer?

The usual requirement is a B.Sc (Engineering) or B.Tech in the relevant branch from a recognised university, or an equivalent qualification. "Relevant branch" means the branch named in the notification: Civil for a Civil AE post, Mechanical for Mechanical, and so on.

Each notification has its own qualification clause, and some carry extra notes on equivalent qualifications. Read that clause line by line, including any footnotes, before you apply. If your degree title differs from the one named, check whether a government order recognises it as equivalent.

## Which branches do Assistant Engineer posts cover?

Assistant Engineer posts are notified for different departments and boards, and each post names its branch. Branches you will see in AE notifications include, for example:

- Civil engineering
- Mechanical engineering
- Electrical and electronics engineering
- Electronics and communication engineering

Your degree branch has to match the post. A B.Tech in Mechanical does not qualify you for a Civil AE post, however much site experience you have. This matters when you choose a branch for a working-professional or lateral-entry B.Tech: pick the one that matches the posts you actually want to apply for, and the diploma you already hold.

## Does Kerala PSC accept AMIE for Assistant Engineer?

Sometimes. Some AE notifications, for example in Civil, list AMIE as an alternative to a degree. Others may not. You have to check the qualification clause of the specific notification.

AMIE's recognition history is complicated. A 2012 Ministry of Human Resource Development memo granted it recognition in perpetuity, but that was withdrawn with effect from 1 June 2013 for new enrolments, and the position since then has been contested in the courts. Employers treat it differently. So "PSC accepts AMIE" is true only for the notifications that say so. Our guide to [AMIE vs B.Tech](/blog/amie-vs-btech) explains the history and who it still suits.

## What is the pay scale for a Kerala PSC Assistant Engineer?

It is set in each notification. As an example, the notification for Assistant Engineer (Civil) in the Public Works Department, notification number 180/2025, lists a scale of pay of ₹55,200–1,15,300.

Other AE posts, in other departments or boards, may carry different scales. Always read the scale printed in the notification you are applying under rather than relying on a figure from an earlier year or another department.

## Is a distance or online B.Tech valid for Kerala PSC?

No. Engineering is among the programmes that the UGC's 2020 regulations for open and distance learning and online programmes do not allow universities to offer in those modes. In 2017 the Supreme Court also held that engineering degrees awarded by distance mode by deemed universities without AICTE approval were not valid.

A distance B.Tech is therefore not a "recognised" degree for an AE post. Certificates can be checked at the verification stage, again at appointment and again later in service, so a degree that slips through once can still be questioned. For the wider picture on distance degrees and PSC, which is different for subjects like B.Com or BA, see [whether a distance degree is valid for Kerala PSC](/blog/distance-degree-valid-kerala-psc). For the engineering rules specifically, see [whether an online B.Tech is valid in India](/blog/online-btech-valid-india).

## Is a B.Tech for working professionals accepted for PSC Assistant Engineer?

Yes, as long as it is from an AICTE-approved institution under a recognised university. A B.Tech for working professionals is a regular programme with flexible class timings. You attend classes and labs at the college and receive the same B.Tech as a full-time student.

In Kerala, KTU offers B.Tech for working professionals at AICTE-approved institutions from 2024-25. It is a lateral-entry style programme: you join the second year (third semester), and it takes not less than three years. The eligibility includes:

- a diploma in engineering with 45% (40% for reserved categories), or a B.Sc with 45% and Mathematics at Plus Two, or a B.Voc or three-year D.Voc;
- at least one year of continuous full-time employment in a registered organisation, shown by an Employment Certificate from your employer;
- qualifying the B.Tech Lateral Entry Test (LET) conducted by LBS Centre for Science and Technology.

There is no upper age limit for admission. PSC posts have their own age limits, which are set in each notification, so check those before you plan your timeline.

## Can a working diploma holder become a PSC Assistant Engineer?

Yes, but it takes a degree first, and it takes time. A realistic path looks like this:

1. **Choose the branch.** Match it to your diploma and to the AE posts you want.
2. **Qualify the LET.** For the 2026 admission, the LET was held on 13 June 2026. The 2027 notification is not out yet; watch the LBS Centre website for it.
3. **Join a working-professional or regular lateral-entry B.Tech.** Our guide to [B.Tech for working professionals in Kerala](/blog/btech-working-professionals-kerala) lists the steps.
4. **Finish the degree.** Not less than three years for the working-professional route.
5. **Apply under the right notification.** Read the qualification clause, the age limit and the date by which the qualification must be held, which PSC notifications state.

If you are already in government service with a diploma, promotion to engineer grades follows your department's service rules, which are separate from direct recruitment through PSC. Our article on [degrees for Kerala government employees](/blog/degree-for-kerala-government-employees) covers what to check.

## What should I check in every AE notification?

Before you apply, go through these points in the notification itself:

- **Qualification clause.** The exact degree, the branch, and whether AMIE or another equivalent is listed.
- **Department and post.** AE posts in different departments are usually separate notifications with separate category numbers.
- **Scale of pay.** As printed for that post.
- **Age limit and relaxations.** As printed for that post and your category.
- **Qualifying date.** The date by which you must hold the qualification.
- **Mode of selection.** Whether there is an OMR or online test, and the syllabus.

Download the notification from the Kerala PSC website rather than relying on a summary forwarded on social media.
