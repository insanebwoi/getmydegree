AICTE allows engineering colleges it has approved to run a B.E. or B.Tech for working professionals: a regular programme with classes in the evenings or at other flexible times, including weekends and holidays. To join, you must be employed in a registered organisation, have at least one year of full-time work experience, and work within a set distance of the college.

These rules are the reason the programme is valid where a distance B.Tech is not. Below we go through each norm as reported for 2024-25, explain where figures differ, and show how KTU applies them in Kerala. The norms are revised in AICTE's approval process each year, so always check the current notice.

## What are AICTE's rules for B.Tech for working professionals?

In short, the programme must run at an AICTE-approved institution with approval for working-professional seats, and the candidate must be a genuinely employed person who can attend in person. The key norms reported for 2024-25 are:

- the candidate is employed in a registered industry or organisation;
- the candidate has a minimum of one year of full-time or regular work experience;
- the workplace is within a radial distance of the institute (reported as 50 km);
- classes are held in the evening hours or at other flexible, convenient timings, including weekends and holidays;
- the admission qualification is the same as for lateral entry to the second year;
- one seat per institute is reserved for central government or industry candidates, filled on merit;
- institutions whose average enrolment was over 80% in the past three years may admit working professionals only into the first year.

## Why did AICTE stop evening B.Tech and then bring it back?

AICTE discontinued the old evening and part-time B.Tech around 2019. From 2023-24 it allowed "B.E./B.Tech for working professionals" at approved institutions as a flexible-timing course, with tighter conditions on who can join.

The background matters. In November 2017, the Supreme Court held in *Orissa Lift Irrigation Corp. Ltd. v. Rabi Sankar Patro* that engineering degrees awarded by distance mode by deemed universities without AICTE approval were not valid, and that technical education by distance could not continue without AICTE approval. The UGC's 2020 regulations on open, distance and online programmes then listed engineering among the subjects that cannot be offered in those modes, and the 2021 and 2022 amendments kept it excluded. The working-professional programme sits inside that framework: it is classroom-based, at an approved campus, just at different hours. Our explainer on [distance B.Tech in Kerala](/blog/distance-btech-kerala) covers the ruling in more detail.

## Who counts as a working professional?

Someone who is currently employed in a registered industry or organisation and has at least one year of full-time or regular work experience. Self-declared freelancing or informal work is unlikely to satisfy the norm; the institution will expect proof from the employer.

In Kerala, KTU asks for at least one year of continuous full-time employment in a registered organisation, supported by an Employment Certificate from the employer. If you have changed jobs, ask the college how it treats the break between employers before you apply.

## How far can my workplace be from the college?

The workplace must be within a radial distance of the institute. The figure reported in the 2024-25 norms is 50 km, down from 70 km in earlier norms. At least one Kerala college's admission page states 75 km.

We cannot tell you which figure a particular college will apply for the next admission, because the sources do not agree and the norms are revised. Read the current AICTE handbook and the college's own notice, and if you are near the limit, ask the college in writing before you apply. The rule exists so that working students can actually attend; someone working in the Gulf cannot meet it.

## When can classes be held?

In the evening hours, or at any flexible and convenient timings, which can include weekends and holidays. AICTE leaves the exact timetable to the institution.

What does not change is that you attend. Labs, workshops, internal assessments and university examinations are part of the programme in the same way as for full-time students. A programme that asks you to attend only for exams is not this programme.

## What qualification do I need?

The admission qualification is the same as for lateral entry to the second year of B.E./B.Tech. In practice that means a diploma in engineering, or another qualification the state and university accept for lateral entry.

KTU's eligibility for its working-professional B.Tech, from its notice and college pages, is:

- a diploma in engineering (three-year, or two-year lateral) with 45% marks (40% for reserved categories);
- or a B.Sc with 45% marks and Mathematics at Plus Two, possibly with Engineering Graphics and Engineering Mechanics to clear;
- or a B.Voc or three-year D.Voc;
- with no upper age limit.

## What are the seat rules?

Two norms affect how seats work. First, one seat per institute is reserved for candidates from central government or industry, filled on merit. Second, as reported for 2024-25, an institution whose average enrolment over the past three years was above 80% may admit working professionals only into the first year.

Individual colleges may add their own conditions. SJCET Palai, for example, listed 30 seats for its 2026 working-professional B.Tech in Electrical and Electronics, and said the batch would run only if at least 10 students joined. Ask any college about such conditions before paying.

## How many institutes offer it?

AICTE reported 137 institutes approved for working-professional programmes across India in October 2023. That number will have changed since, and it covers the whole country, not Kerala alone.

In Kerala, college websites for the 2026 admission show examples such as College of Engineering Thalassery (B.Tech Mechanical for working professionals, 30 seats) and SJCET Palai (B.Tech Electrical and Electronics). These are examples, not a full list.

## How does KTU apply these rules in Kerala?

KTU runs its B.Tech for working professionals from 2024-25 at AICTE-approved institutions. Students join the third semester, study for not less than three years, and attend in the evening or at flexible timings including weekends and holidays.

Admission requires qualifying the B.Tech Lateral Entry Test (LET) conducted by LBS Centre for Science and Technology for the Directorate of Technical Education. Colleges rank candidates by LET score, and if LET-qualified candidates run out, others may be admitted on qualifying marks. Our full guide to the [B.Tech for working professionals in Kerala](/blog/btech-working-professionals-kerala) covers admission step by step, and the [Kerala LET guide](/blog/kerala-btech-lateral-entry-let) covers the test.

## How do I check that a programme follows AICTE's rules?

Verify it yourself before you pay anything:

1. **AICTE approval.** Check that the institution and its working-professional intake appear in AICTE's approval for the current year.
2. **University affiliation.** Check that the college is affiliated to KTU and that the programme appears in the university's notice.
3. **Mode.** The admission letter and fee receipt should describe the programme as regular (working professional), never distance or online.
4. **Conditions.** Ask for the college's written conditions on work experience, distance from the workplace and class timings.

If a provider offers a B.Tech with no attendance, no entrance test and no proof of employment, it does not meet these rules. Our guide on [spotting a fake degree provider](/blog/spot-fake-degree-provider) lists the common warning signs.
