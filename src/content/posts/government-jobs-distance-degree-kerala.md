A recognized distance or online degree lets you apply for most government jobs in Kerala and across India where the eligibility is simply "a degree from a recognized university". That covers Kerala PSC degree-level posts, SSC graduate-level exams, bank officer recruitment and the UPSC civil services. The exception is any post whose Special Rules or notification ask for a qualification earned through a regular course of study. For those, a distance degree does not qualify, and the only way to know is to read each notification.

This guide explains which categories of posts are usually open to you, what makes a distance degree acceptable in the first place, and where the limits are. It does not promise you any particular post. Recruitment rules change, and the notification for the post you want is always the final word.

## What makes a distance degree count at all

Before looking at jobs, the degree itself has to pass three tests.

- **The university is recognized.** It must be a university established by a central or state Act, or a deemed-to-be university, listed by the UGC.

- **The programme had DEB entitlement for your admission year.** The UGC Distance Education Bureau approves distance and online programmes year by year. A university can be recognized while a particular programme, in a particular year, is not. Our guide to [verifying UGC-DEB approval](/blog/ugc-deb-approval-search-verify-recognition) shows how to check.

- **The notification accepts it.** Under the UGC (Open and Distance Learning Programmes and Online Programmes) Regulations, 2020, degrees from recognized institutions with valid entitlement are equivalent to conventional degrees. But a recruiting body can still set its own conditions for a specific post.

If any of the three fails, nothing below applies. If all three hold, you are in a strong position for most graduate-level recruitment.

## Kerala PSC degree-level posts

Kerala PSC recruits for a large number of posts where the basic qualification is a graduate degree. Many of these are filled through common degree-level preliminary examinations followed by main examinations for groups of posts. Clerical, assistant and similar administrative posts in government departments and secretariat-type offices commonly fall into this group.

For most of these, a recognized distance degree is accepted in the same way as a regular one. What the PSC checks at document verification is the university, the programme, and that your certificates are in order.

Read the full notification anyway. Some posts ask for a degree in a specific subject, a degree plus a further qualification, or a degree earned in a particular way. Our article on [whether a distance degree is valid for Kerala PSC](/blog/distance-degree-valid-kerala-psc) covers the PSC side in more detail.

## Central government: SSC, UPSC and railways

The Staff Selection Commission's Combined Graduate Level examination recruits for a range of central government posts, and its basic eligibility is a bachelor's degree from a recognized university. A few posts within it ask for a particular subject or additional qualification, so check the post-wise table in the notice.

The UPSC Civil Services examination asks for a degree from a recognized university, without specifying the mode of study. Other UPSC examinations have their own conditions, and some technical services require specific professional degrees that cannot be earned through distance mode in the first place.

Railway recruitment for graduate-level non-technical posts follows the same general pattern: a degree from a recognized university, with any extra condition stated in the notice.

## Banking: IBPS and SBI

Probationary officer and clerical recruitment through IBPS, and SBI's own officer and associate recruitment, generally require graduation in any discipline from a recognized university. A recognized distance degree satisfies that in the ordinary case.

Banks are particular about dates. The degree usually has to be completed, with the result declared, by a cut-off date stated in the notice. If you are in your final year, check whether the notice allows provisional applications and what proof you must show later. Some notices also set a minimum percentage, so keep your consolidated marksheet to hand when you apply.

## The regular-course exception

This is the part most guides leave out. Some posts, often those connected to specific professions or technical roles, have Special Rules that require a qualification obtained through a "regular course of study". For those posts, a distance degree does not qualify, however well recognized the university.

In January 2026 the Kerala High Court applied this rule in *Kerala Public Service Commission v. Vaisakh C.L.* (2026:KER:6103), concerning the post of Librarian Grade IV. A library science qualification earned through distance education was held not to satisfy a notification that required a regular course. An equivalency certificate did not change that outcome.

The lesson is narrow but important. Equivalence under UGC rules is the general position. A post's own rules can still set a higher bar. Our longer explainer on [distance degrees and government jobs](/blog/is-a-distance-degree-valid-for-government-jobs) walks through the ruling.

## Posts a distance degree will not get you

Some government roles need professional qualifications that UGC rules do not allow in distance or online mode, such as engineering, medicine, nursing, pharmacy, law and architecture. No distance B.Com or BA will make you eligible for a post that requires one of those degrees. Teaching posts often need a B.Ed and subject-specific qualifications with their own rules. If you have a particular post in mind, start from its eligibility and work backwards.

## How to check a notification before you enrol

- **Find the most recent notification for the post.** Kerala PSC notifications are on its official website. Read the qualification section in full, not a summary on a coaching page.

- **Look for the words "regular", "full-time" or "regular course of study".** If they appear, a distance degree is likely to be a problem. Ask before you choose a programme.

- **Check the subject requirement.** "A degree in Commerce" is not the same as "a degree in any discipline".

- **Check the cut-off date.** Work out whether your degree will be completed, with results, before the date the notice sets.

- **Keep your name consistent.** The name on your 10th certificate, degree certificate and application should match exactly.

None of this is complicated, but it has to be done for each post. A degree that suits one notification may not suit another.
