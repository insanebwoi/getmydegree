A B.Tech in Electrical and Electronics Engineering (EEE) has steady scope in Kerala in 2026. Every home, factory, hospital and building needs electrical engineers to design, run and maintain power, and two growing areas, rooftop solar and electric vehicle charging, are adding new kinds of work on top of the traditional ones.

For working electrical diploma holders, such as technicians with the electricity board, contractors, plant electricians and service engineers, the B.Tech is usually the step that moves them from technician to engineer. This guide explains where EEE graduates work, how solar and EVs change the picture, and how to earn the degree without leaving your job.

## Is EEE a good branch in 2026?

Yes, for students who like physics and practical systems. EEE is a core branch, which means it is the qualification named in electrical engineering posts in government, utilities and industry, and its work cannot be outsourced or automated away easily.

It is a demanding branch. Circuits, machines, power systems and control need strong mathematics, and the labs matter. Students who choose it because it "sounds safe" without liking the subject often find it hard going.

## What jobs can an EEE graduate get in Kerala?

EEE graduates work across power, buildings, industry and electronics. Common paths include:

- **Power utilities:** generation, transmission and distribution, including the Kerala State Electricity Board (KSEB).
- **Government engineering posts** recruited through Kerala PSC.
- **Electrical design and contracting** for buildings, hospitals and industrial plants.
- **Maintenance and plant engineering** in factories, refineries, ports and shipyards.
- **Renewable energy:** solar design, installation and operation.
- **EV charging and electric mobility.**
- **Electronics, automation and control** roles that overlap with ECE.

Gulf countries have long hired Kerala electrical engineers for building services and maintenance, subject to the [degree attestation](/blog/degree-attestation-gulf) and registration rules of each country.

## How do I get a job in KSEB as an engineer?

Engineering posts in KSEB and in state government departments are generally filled through Kerala PSC recruitment, and Assistant Engineer posts ask for a degree in the relevant branch, usually electrical engineering, from a recognised university. Some notifications list other accepted equivalents, so read each one carefully.

Diploma holders are usually recruited at lower technical grades, and promotion to the engineer cadre generally depends on the degree. We do not quote KSEB pay here; check the scale in the specific Kerala PSC notification. Our guide to [Kerala PSC Assistant Engineer qualification](/blog/kerala-psc-assistant-engineer-btech) explains the posts and branches.

## Is solar energy a good career for electrical engineers?

Yes, it is one of the most practical growth areas for EEE graduates in Kerala. The Union Cabinet approved the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana in February 2024 to install rooftop solar in one crore households, and Kerala households take part through the national portal, with KSEB, as the distribution company, handling the connection side such as feasibility and net metering.

Rooftop and commercial solar need engineers for:

- **System design:** sizing, layout, cable and protection design.
- **Grid connection:** inverters, metering, safety and compliance with utility rules.
- **Installation supervision and commissioning.**
- **Operation and maintenance** of larger plants.
- **Energy audits** for businesses trying to cut their power bills.

Many solar firms are small, so engineers often handle sales, design and site work together. That suits people with field experience.

## What jobs are there in EV charging?

EV charging needs electrical engineers to plan, install, connect and maintain chargers, and the work is growing. In Kerala, KSEB is the designated nodal agency for EV charging stations under the state's 2019 electric vehicle policy, and it has installed pole-mounted chargers as well as larger charging stations. Nationally, the PM E-DRIVE scheme of the Ministry of Heavy Industries supports public charging infrastructure, and KSEB has been the state nodal agency submitting Kerala's charging proposals under it.

The work includes:

- **Site planning and load assessment**, checking that the local network can carry the extra demand.
- **Installation and protection** of AC and DC fast chargers.
- **Operation and maintenance** of charging networks.
- **Integration with solar and storage**, an area that combines both growth fields.

We do not quote job numbers for this sector, because reliable figures for Kerala are not published.

## Can an electrical diploma holder join B.Tech EEE in the second year?

Yes. Holders of a diploma in electrical and electronics engineering can join the third semester of B.Tech EEE through the Lateral Entry Test (LET) conducted by the LBS Centre, either in the regular programme or, if they work, in a B.Tech for working professionals. Holders of related diplomas should check the LET prospectus for the branches their diploma is eligible for.

The 2026 LET was held on 13 June 2026. The 2027 notification is not out yet, so watch the LBS Centre website. Our guide to [Kerala B.Tech lateral entry (LET)](/blog/kerala-btech-lateral-entry-let) explains the steps.

## Can I do B.Tech EEE while working in Kerala?

Yes, through the KTU B.Tech for working professionals at AICTE-approved colleges that offer it in EEE. You join the second year, classes are held in the evenings or at flexible times including weekends and holidays, and the course runs not less than three years.

For example, SJCET Palai lists B.Tech Electrical and Electronics for working professionals with 30 seats, running only if at least 10 students join. Its 2026 admission page lists tuition of ₹50,250 per semester, a refundable caution deposit of ₹10,000, a special fee of ₹10,000 a year and an exam fee of ₹2,625 a semester, plus KTU fees. Fees and seats change every year, so check the current page.

Eligibility includes a diploma in engineering with 45% (40% for reserved categories), at least one year of continuous full-time employment in a registered organisation with an employer's certificate, an LET qualification, and a workplace within the distance AICTE sets from the college. See [B.Tech for working professionals in Kerala](/blog/btech-working-professionals-kerala) and [what a B.Tech for working professionals costs](/blog/btech-fees-kerala-working-professionals).

## Which skills should an EEE graduate add?

The degree covers the theory. These additions make you more employable:

- **Solar design and simulation software.**
- **Electrical design and drafting** for buildings and plants.
- **PLC, SCADA and automation**, used in factories and utilities.
- **Power electronics**, the heart of inverters, motor drives and EV chargers.
- **Electrical safety and codes**, which every employer checks.

Working electricians often have the field skills already; the B.Tech adds the design and analysis that lets them take responsibility for a system.
