A B.Tech for working professionals in Kerala costs very different amounts depending on the college. For the 2026 admission, for example, College of Engineering Thalassery listed tuition of ₹40,000 a year for its B.Tech Mechanical for working professionals, while SJCET Palai listed ₹50,250 per semester for its B.Tech Electrical and Electronics programme, plus other fees. Over a three-year programme, that is a large difference.

Fees change every year, and each college sets its own, so treat the figures below as examples, not a price list. What does not change is how to read a fee structure: tuition is only part of the cost, and you should know the full total before you pay anything.

## How much does a B.Tech for working professionals cost in Kerala?

It depends on the college, and you will only know the exact figure from that college's admission page or prospectus for the year you join. As examples from the 2026 admission, one college listed ₹40,000 a year in tuition and another ₹50,250 per semester, each with other fees on top.

The programme runs for at least three years, because working professionals join the third semester. So plan for six semesters of fees, not one year's figure.

## What did colleges list for the 2026 admission?

For example, College of Engineering Thalassery listed ₹40,000 a year in tuition for its working-professional B.Tech Mechanical, and SJCET Palai listed ₹50,250 per semester for its working-professional B.Tech Electrical and Electronics. Both figures are as listed on each college's 2026 admission page:

| | College of Engineering Thalassery | SJCET Palai |
|---|---|---|
| Programme | B.Tech Mechanical (working professionals) | B.Tech Electrical and Electronics (working professionals) |
| Seats | 30 | 30, runs only if at least 10 students join |
| Tuition | ₹40,000 a year | ₹50,250 per semester |
| Other listed fees | Total first payment listed as ₹56,460 | Caution deposit ₹10,000 (refundable), special fee ₹10,000 a year, exam fee ₹2,625 a semester, plus KTU fees |

These are examples, not a full list of colleges, and the figures may be different for the 2027 admission. Check the college's own page for the year you apply.

At the 2026 listed rates, and assuming they stayed the same, tuition alone would come to about ₹1,20,000 at Thalassery (three years at ₹40,000) and about ₹3,01,500 at SJCET Palai (six semesters at ₹50,250). At Palai, the listed special fee and exam fees would add roughly another ₹45,750 over three years, before KTU fees.

This is simple arithmetic on one year's figures, not a quote. Fees are often revised, so ask each college whether the rate is fixed for your batch or can change each year.

## What other costs should I budget for?

Beyond tuition, expect university fees, examination fees, deposits and the everyday costs of attending classes after work. These are easy to forget when comparing colleges.

A practical list:

- **LET application fee**, paid to LBS Centre when you apply for the entrance test;
- **KTU fees** for registration and examinations, charged separately at some colleges;
- **caution deposit**, usually refundable when you finish;
- **special or development fees** charged by the college each year;
- **supplementary exam fees** if you need to rewrite a paper;
- **lab records, drawing instruments, textbooks** and project costs;
- **travel** to evening or weekend classes, which adds up over three years;
- **certificates and attestation** at the end, especially if you plan to work in the Gulf.

## Why do fees differ so much between colleges?

Each college sets its fee within the rules that apply to it, and the type of institution and how it is funded make a big difference. Programmes in the same branch at two colleges can be far apart in cost, even though both lead to the same KTU B.Tech.

When comparing, look beyond the fee:

- Is the college and the working-professional programme AICTE-approved for the year?
- Is the college within reach of your workplace, so that you can attend after work?
- Does the programme have a minimum batch size, and what happens if it is not met?
- What is the total over three years, not the first payment?

## Can I pay the fee in instalments?

Usually you pay semester by semester or year by year, which already spreads the cost. Some colleges may allow further instalments, but that is the college's decision, so ask before you assume.

Other ways people manage the cost:

- **Education loans** from banks, which have their own terms and interest;
- **Employer support**, where your company reimburses fees, sometimes with a service bond;
- **Saving ahead** for the first payment, which is often the largest because of deposits.

Get any instalment arrangement in writing from the college, and pay into the college's official account, not to an individual.

## Will my employer pay for my B.Tech?

Some employers do, especially when the degree leads to a promotion within the company. Ask HR whether there is a policy for fee reimbursement or study leave, and whether it comes with a service bond requiring you to stay for a period after you finish.

You will also need your employer's Employment Certificate for admission, so it helps to talk to them early. Our article on [how a B.Tech changes grade and promotion](/blog/diploma-vs-btech-salary-promotion) may help you make the case.

## How do I avoid paying for the wrong programme?

Pay only to the college, only for an AICTE-approved working-professional programme, and only after you have seen the approval. The most expensive mistake is paying for a "distance B.Tech", which is not valid in India at any price.

Warning signs include a fee that is much lower than any college lists, an offer to skip the LET, a promise to finish in one or two years, and pressure to pay in cash to an agent.
