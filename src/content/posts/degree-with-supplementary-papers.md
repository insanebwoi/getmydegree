If you have failed subjects or supplementary papers pending, you can usually still finish your degree in one of two ways: clear the pending papers with your own university, if you are still within its permitted time limit, or move the papers you have already passed to another recognized university through credit transfer and complete the remainder there. Which route is right depends on how many papers are pending, whether your university still allows you to write them, and how long ago you studied.

Many people we meet have carried a few papers for years, sometimes a decade, assuming the degree was lost. Often it is not. The first job is to find out exactly where you stand.

## Step one: get the facts on paper

Before deciding anything, collect three pieces of information.

- **Your full mark record.** Every semester or year, every paper, passed or failed. If you have lost marksheets, request duplicates from the university's examination wing.

- **Your registration details.** The register number, the year you were admitted, and the scheme or syllabus you were admitted under.

- **Your university's time limit.** Universities set a maximum period within which a programme must be completed, counted from admission. Find the regulation that applies to your admission year, not the current one.

With these three in hand, you will know how many papers remain and whether your university still lets you sit them.

## Route one: clear the papers with your own university

If you are within the permitted period and only a few papers remain, this is usually the simplest route. Your degree comes from the institution you started with, and nothing needs mapping.

- Watch the examination notifications for supplementary or arrear examinations and the registration window for each. Missing a window can mean waiting another cycle.

- Check whether you will be examined on the old syllabus or a revised one. Older candidates are sometimes moved to a new scheme, which changes what you need to study.

- Look for any internal or continuous assessment component tied to the failed paper. Some universities carry forward old internal marks; others need them redone.

- If you are past the normal period, ask whether a special or one-time chance has been announced. Universities, including some in Kerala, have announced such chances from time to time for candidates beyond the usual limit, but they are not routine and cannot be counted on.

Practise past papers under timed conditions rather than re-reading notes. A paper you failed once is usually failed on time management or structure, not only on content.

## Route two: credit transfer to another university

If your university's time limit has run out, if too many papers remain, or if you cannot attend examinations under its schedule, transferring what you have passed may be the better route.

- Papers you have passed can be considered for credit transfer by a receiving university, subject to its rules. Papers you failed generally do not transfer; you complete those requirements, or their equivalents, in the new programme.

- Under the Academic Bank of Credits, credits earned at registered institutions are stored against your APAAR ID. Older studies that predate ABC can still be evaluated from your marksheets.

- The receiving university decides how much counts, which year you join, and what remains. Get that decision in writing before paying.

## A typical case we see

This is an illustrative composite, not a real person. A woman from Malappuram finished all six semesters of a B.Com at a Kerala university about eight years ago but left four papers uncleared when she married and moved. She assumed the degree was gone.

Her first step was writing to the university's examination wing to ask whether she was still within the permitted period for her admission year. She was not, and no special chance was open. Her marksheets, though, showed that most of her papers were passed. A receiving university evaluated them and told her, in writing, what it would accept and what she would need to complete in its distance B.Com. The remainder was smaller than the full degree she had feared.

Not every case ends this way. Sometimes the old university still allows the attempt, and that is the quicker route; sometimes the receiving university accepts very little. The point is to find out before assuming either outcome.

## Choosing between the two

- **Few papers, still within the time limit:** clear them with your own university. It is almost always faster.

- **Many papers, or time limit expired:** credit transfer to a recognized university is usually more realistic than waiting for a special chance.

- **Studied long ago, syllabus has changed completely:** expect the receiving university to accept less, and plan for a longer remainder. Our article on [finishing a degree after a long gap](/blog/degree-after-long-gap) goes through this.

- **Need the degree by a specific date:** work backwards from the date. A supplementary examination cycle, its result and the issue of a provisional certificate all take time.

## If you need the degree for a job or a PSC exam

The date by which you hold the qualification matters as much as the qualification itself. Kerala PSC notifications generally require candidates to possess the qualification by the last date for receipt of applications, so a degree with papers still pending on that date is unlikely to count. Read the notification for the exact condition.

If you finish through a distance or online programme, the usual checks apply: the university must be UGC recognized, the programme must have DEB entitlement for your admission year, and the post's own rules must not require a regular course of study. Our guide to [distance degrees for Kerala PSC](/blog/distance-degree-valid-kerala-psc) explains the 2026 ruling on that last point.

## Keeping it from happening again

Whatever route you take, the habits that get people through pending papers are the same ones that stop new ones from appearing.

- Put every examination registration date in your phone calendar the day the notification comes out.

- Clear a pending paper at the first available attempt. Each cycle you defer adds distance from the material.

- Study the paper you failed first, before the new ones. It is the one with a time limit attached.

- Ask for help early. A counsellor or teacher who looks at your answer pattern can usually spot what went wrong in one sitting.

## Practical next steps

Put your mark record, register number and admission year together, and ask your university in writing for the time limit that applies to you and the next available examination dates. If the answer is that you can still sit the papers, do that. If not, bring the same documents to a counsellor and ask which recognized universities will evaluate them for credit transfer, and what would remain.
