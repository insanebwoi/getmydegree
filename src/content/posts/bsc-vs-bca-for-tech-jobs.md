BCA and B.Sc in computer science or IT overlap enough to look interchangeable and differ enough to matter. The right answer depends on what you want the degree to do.

## What each one covers

BCA is an applications degree. Programming, databases, web and application development, software engineering practice. It is built to produce someone who can work on software, and the coursework is closer to the job.

B.Sc in computer science is a science degree. More mathematics, more theory, algorithms and computing fundamentals, often with a laboratory component. B.Sc IT sits between the two, weighted toward systems and information management.

BCA teaches you to build. B.Sc teaches you why it works.

## How employers read them

For most development, support, testing and implementation roles in the private sector, they are treated as equivalent, and three years of relevant work will outweigh the distinction entirely.

Where the difference shows up is postgraduate study and specialised technical work. A B.Sc with a solid mathematics base is the smoother entry to an M.Sc in computer science or a data-focused postgraduate programme. BCA is the natural feed into MCA.

For government recruitment, read the notification. Some name the degree precisely; many ask for a bachelor's degree in any discipline, in which case either qualifies.

## If you already work in technology

This is the common case: a support engineer, tester or junior developer who came in without a formal degree and now needs one for a promotion or a visa.

BCA is usually the better fit. The material matches what you already do, which makes it faster to get through, and it leads cleanly to MCA if a postgraduate qualification comes next.

Choose B.Sc if you intend to move toward data science, machine learning or research, where the mathematics is not optional and being made to learn it properly is the point.

## The practical factors

Check whether the programme has laboratory or practical components requiring attendance, because that decides whether it works around your job. Check the mathematics requirement at entry for B.Sc, which catches out people whose 10+2 did not include it. And check the examination format.

## In short

Want to keep doing the work you already do, with a degree behind it: BCA. Want to move into the quantitative end of the field: B.Sc.
