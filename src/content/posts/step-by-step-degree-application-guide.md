To apply for a distance or online degree, you decide what the degree is for, check what you have already earned, verify the university and programme, gather your documents and IDs, apply and pay, then wait for the enrolment number that actually confirms your admission. Admission is not complicated, but it does have a sequence, and problems almost always come from doing a step out of order. The commonest example is paying first and checking afterwards.

Here is the whole process in the order it should happen, starting with the documents, since those are what hold most people up.

## What documents do I need to apply for a distance degree?

You will need your school certificates, a photo ID, a photograph and signature, and your digital IDs, plus earlier degree or college records if you have any. Requirements vary a little by university, so confirm the final list with the one you choose.

| Document | Who needs it | Where it comes from | What goes wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10th (SSLC) certificate | Everyone | Your school record, or the board for a duplicate | Often the proof of date of birth, so errors here spread |
| 12th marksheet and certificate | Everyone | Your board, such as the Higher Secondary or VHSE board in Kerala | Lost originals take time to replace |
| Bachelor's certificate and consolidated marksheet | PG applicants | Your university | Certificate not yet collected, or papers still uncleared |
| Earlier semester marksheets | Anyone with previous college study | Your old university | Only the final one is kept, or some were lost |
| Transfer or migration certificate | Where one was issued or is asked for | Your old college or university | Can take weeks from a college you left years ago |
| Government photo ID | Everyone | Aadhaar is the usual choice | Name differs from your certificates |
| Photograph and signature | Everyone | Scanned at home | Wrong file size for the portal |
| ABC/APAAR ID and DEB ID | Applicants to distance and online programmes | DigiLocker and the UGC DEB portal | Left until the last week |

Start any duplicate or reissue requests the day you decide to apply. If there has been a long break since your last study, some universities may also ask for a gap certificate or affidavit, so ask about that early.

## What are the steps in a degree application?

There are eight, and each one depends on the one before it.

### Step 1: Decide what the degree is for

A promotion criterion, a government eligibility rule, a visa category or a change of field. The reason determines the level, sometimes the discipline and occasionally the mode. If a written requirement exists, get a copy before choosing anything. A Kerala PSC aspirant should read the qualification clause of the notification at this point, since some posts require a regular course of study, and a distance degree does not satisfy that.

### Step 2: Check what you have already earned

If you studied before and stopped, those semesters may still carry credit value at a recognized university, which can shorten the programme considerably. Gather every marksheet you hold before assuming that you are starting from the beginning. Our [credit transfer service](/credit-transfer) can assess them, and the first assessment is free.

### Step 3: Shortlist, then verify

List the universities that offer your programme, then run three checks on each. Is it recognized by the UGC as a university? Is the programme entitled by the Distance Education Bureau, in this mode, for your session? Does the programme appear on the university's own website? Our [guide to verifying UGC-DEB approval](/blog/ugc-deb-approval-search-verify-recognition) shows where to look. Only compare what survives all three. Accreditation, examination format and fees are how you choose between valid options, not a substitute for validity.

### Step 4: Assemble your documents and IDs

Work through the table above. Create your DigiLocker account first, then your ABC/APAAR ID, then your DEB ID, since each depends on the last. Our [explainer on the ABC ID](/blog/academic-bank-of-credits-abc-id-explained) covers the snags. Check that your name and date of birth match across everything.

### Step 5: Get the full fee in writing

Before paying anything, ask for tuition, registration, examination, certification and any other charges, in writing, and ask who the payment goes to and whose name will be on the receipt. This is our own rule too: we never take payment until you have seen the university, programme, DEB entitlement and full fee in writing. Our instalment plans are monthly, with no interest and no processing fee.

### Step 6: Apply and pay

Complete the application and upload everything. Then confirm that the portal shows it as submitted and not saved as a draft. Keep every receipt.

### Step 7: Get your enrolment number

Admission is confirmed when the university issues an enrolment number in your name, visible on its own portal. It is not confirmed when a payment clears or when an office issues a receipt. If a week passes with no enrolment number and no explanation, ask in writing.

### Step 8: Onboard and keep the file

You receive portal access, learning material, the semester calendar and your examination dates. Put the assessment dates in your work calendar the day you get them. Then keep your enrolment number, fee receipts, entitlement evidence for your session and every certificate as it is issued, all in one place. Verification may happen years later.

## Which mistakes do people make when applying?

Most application problems come from a few repeated mistakes, and all of them can be avoided.

- **Paying before verifying.** Someone is told on the phone that a seat is about to go, and pays before anything is verified. Genuine admissions wait for you to check.

- **Not comparing your name across documents.** A small difference between your SSLC, Aadhaar and degree can surface at verification years later. Sort out spelling differences at the start.

- **Checking the wrong year's entitlement.** An approval for an earlier session says nothing about yours.

- **Uploading an unreadable scan.** A blurred marksheet can stall verification. Open each file and read it before you upload it.

- **Taking a receipt as admission.** Until you see your enrolment number on the university's portal, you are an applicant.

## How long does the application process take?

It depends mostly on your documents, not on the application form, which is the quick part. If you hold everything, the work can be done in days. If you need a duplicate marksheet or a migration certificate, allow weeks. Time your start against the last date for your session, and our article on [planning backward from admission deadlines](/blog/online-degree-admission-deadlines) shows how.

Remember that nothing in this process, however carefully you follow it, settles every use of the degree. Validity always depends on the university's recognition, the programme's DEB entitlement for your admission year, and the wording of the job notification you are applying under.
