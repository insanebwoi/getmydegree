NBA accreditation means the National Board of Accreditation has assessed a particular engineering programme at a particular college and found that it meets its standards, for a set period. It is given programme by programme, not to the whole college, and it expires unless renewed. So "this college is NBA accredited" is never the full answer; the question is which branch, and for which years.

The Washington Accord is the reason NBA accreditation matters beyond India. India, represented by the NBA, became a permanent signatory of the accord on 13 June 2014. For undergraduate engineering programmes accredited by the NBA under Tier I, the accord means other signatory bodies treat the degree as substantially equivalent to their own accredited programmes. Below we explain what that does and does not give you.

## What is NBA accreditation?

NBA accreditation is a quality assessment of a single programme, such as B.Tech Civil Engineering at one college. The National Board of Accreditation looks at how the programme is run and what its students achieve, and if it meets the standard, accredits it for a limited period.

Three features are worth remembering:

- **Programme-wise.** The Mechanical branch at a college may be accredited while the Electrical branch is not.
- **Time-limited.** Accreditation has a validity period. Once it lapses, the programme is not accredited unless the college applies again and succeeds.
- **Applied for by the college.** Colleges seek it programme by programme, and not every programme at every college has it.

Check the current status of any programme on the NBA's own website, nbaind.org, rather than on a brochure or an agent's word.

## Is NBA accreditation the same as AICTE approval or NAAC?

No. They answer different questions, and you should check each separately.

| Check | What it covers | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| University recognition (UGC) | The university awarding the degree | The degree is from a recognised university |
| AICTE approval | The institution and its intake for each course | The college is allowed to run that B.Tech, with that number of seats |
| NBA accreditation | A specific programme, for a set period | That programme met the NBA's quality standard |
| NAAC accreditation | The institution as a whole | An overall grade for the college |

For a B.Tech to be a valid degree in India, the university's recognition and AICTE approval are the essentials. NBA accreditation is an additional quality mark on top. Our explainer on [NAAC grades and NIRF rankings](/blog/naac-grades-and-nirf-rankings-explained) covers the institution-level measures.

## What is the Washington Accord?

The Washington Accord is an international agreement between the bodies that accredit engineering degrees in their countries. Each signatory recognises that programmes accredited by the other signatories are substantially equivalent to its own, so graduates are treated as having met the academic requirement for engineering practice.

India's signatory is the NBA. India became a permanent signatory on 13 June 2014. The accord covers professional engineering degrees; engineering technology and technician-level qualifications are dealt with under separate agreements, the Sydney and Dublin Accords.

## Which Indian B.Tech programmes does the Washington Accord cover?

It covers undergraduate engineering programmes accredited by the NBA under Tier I. A B.Tech that is not NBA accredited, or that was accredited only under another tier, does not carry Washington Accord recognition simply because the college has other accredited branches.

Two further points matter for individual graduates:

- **Your graduation year should fall within the accreditation.** Because accreditation is time-limited, check whether the programme was accredited for the years that cover your batch.
- **Recognition is not retroactive.** Under the accord, recognition generally applies only to qualifications awarded after a signatory joined, not to older degrees.

If you are joining a college now and expect to work abroad, ask the college which tier its accreditation is under, and for which period, and check the answer on the NBA website.

## Does the Washington Accord give me a licence to work abroad as an engineer?

No. The accord deals only with the academic part of becoming a professional engineer. It does not give anyone a licence or registration to practise in another country.

Licensing and registration remain with each country's own engineering authority, which may still look at your experience, set exams, and check documents. The Gulf countries have their own systems; our articles on Saudi Council of Engineers registration and on engineering jobs in the UAE, Qatar and Oman explain those. Whatever the country, your degree will also need the usual [attestation for the Gulf](/blog/degree-attestation-gulf) or the equivalent verification elsewhere.

## Is my B.Tech worth less if it is not NBA accredited?

Not in India for most purposes. A B.Tech from a recognised university at an AICTE-approved college is a valid engineering degree for Kerala PSC, central government recruitment and most private employers, whether or not that branch held NBA accreditation.

Where it can make a difference is abroad, in countries that rely on the Washington Accord when assessing engineers, and with some employers who prefer accredited programmes. So if working overseas is a serious plan, NBA accreditation under Tier I is worth weighing when you choose a college. If your goal is promotion in your current Kerala job, the priority is a valid, AICTE-approved B.Tech you can actually attend.

## What does this mean for a B.Tech for working professionals?

The working-professional B.Tech at an AICTE-approved institution is a regular-mode programme with in-person classes in the evenings or at weekends, so it is a genuine engineering degree in India. Whether a particular working-professional programme is NBA accredited is a separate question, and the scheme is relatively new, having been allowed by AICTE from 2023-24 onwards.

So ask the college directly whether the specific programme you are joining is NBA accredited, and do not assume the accreditation of its regular full-time branch automatically covers it. If it is not, the degree is still valid in India; it simply does not carry the Washington Accord recognition. Our guide to [B.Tech for working professionals in Kerala](/blog/btech-working-professionals-kerala) covers eligibility and admission.

A distance or online B.Tech is a different matter altogether. It is not valid in India, and no accreditation applies to it. See [distance B.Tech in Kerala](/blog/distance-btech-kerala) for why.

## How do I check whether a programme is NBA accredited?

Check it yourself on the NBA website, nbaind.org, rather than relying on a brochure, and ask the college for its accreditation letter if the website is unclear.

When you look, confirm:

1. **The exact programme name and college,** since colleges with similar names exist.
2. **The period of accreditation,** and whether it covers the years you will study or graduate.
3. **The tier,** if Washington Accord recognition matters to you.

Then check the college's AICTE approval for the same programme and intake, and the university's recognition.
